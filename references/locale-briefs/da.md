## Address and gender

- `du` throughout app-owned copy: second-person singular pronoun and possessive (`din`/`dit`/`dine`, agreeing with the noun's own grammatical gender and number, never with the reader), never the archaic formal `De`. Apple's Danish does the same on the exact screens this app mirrors: "%@" vil have adgang til Skærmtid" then "Hvis du giver "%@" adgang..." (`Applications/FamilyControlsAuthenticationUI.app | Localizable.strings | NOTIFICATION_AUTH_REQUEST_TITLE`, `NOTIFICATION_AUTH_REQUEST_MESSAGE_INDIVIDUAL`); "Du kan slå sundhedsdatakategorier til senere i appen Sundhed." (`HealthUI-Localizable.strings | AUTHORIZATION_DONT_ALLOW_ALERT_MESSAGE`); "Modtag en notifikation, når din ugentlige rapport er tilgængelig." (`System/Library/PrivateFrameworks/ScreenTimeSettingsUI.framework | Localizable.strings | WeeklyReportNotificationGroupSpecifierFooterText`). This is not an open decision: modern Danish consumer software, Apple's own interface and the app's target register all sit in one register here, with formal `De` reserved for the royal family, some legal text and a handful of very formal institutional letters.
- Controls are second-person singular imperatives: "Tillad" / "Tillad ikke" / "Fortsæt" (`System/Library/PrivateFrameworks/UserNotificationsServer.framework | Localizable.strings | PERMISSION_ALERT_ALLOW`, `PERMISSION_ALERT_DENY`; `FamilyControlsAuthenticationUI.app | Localizable.strings | COMMON_CONTINUE`). Danish verbs do not conjugate for person or number in the present tense, but the imperative is not simply the present-tense form: it drops the present/infinitive ending (`annullerer` -> `Annuller`, `gemmer` -> `Gem`). That truncation is the one thing that can go wrong — shipping a control label with the present-tense ending still attached (e.g. "Annullerer" instead of "Annuller") — not a full conjugation table the way a Romance or Finno-Ugric target would need.
- The app speaks about itself in the first person plural on 19 `en` keys ("We couldn't load the data.", `component.errorView.message`; "we'll use %@", `onboarding.hourlyWage.footer`; "We covered %@ with a streak freeze.", `streakFreeze.usedBody`). Danish keeps the actor: "Vi kunne ikke indlæse dataene.", "bruger vi %@", "Vi dækkede %@ med en frys." Do not turn these into a passive that hides who acted (Danish has a productive `-s` passive that would make this easy to slip into).
- Gender: Danish nouns carry grammatical common/neuter gender (en bil / et hus), and attributive or predicate adjectives agree with that noun's gender and number — but never with the biological sex of the person being addressed. `du` is grammatically common gender and invariant; a predicate adjective after `du` ("du er glad", "du er klar") takes the same unmarked common-gender form for every reader, so this app needs no neutral-pronoun or dual-form strategy at all. The `en` keys that force a gendered rewrite in `ro` carry none in Danish: `achievements.streak.body` ("You showed up %lld days in a row." -> "Du mødte op %lld dage i træk."), `achievements.badge.fullDaysFifty.earned`, `achievements.badge.hired.name`, `achievements.badge.salaried.name`, `achievements.badge.rehired.name`, `achievements.badge.clockedIn.earned`, `achievements.gauge.title`, `widgets.tasks.allDone`. Reader predicates behave the same way: `focusTask.alert.deleteTask.message` ("Are you sure…"), `onboarding.dailyFocusGoal.subtitle`, `screenTime.createRestrictedList.savedMessage`, `shield.reflection.begin.title`.
- What does need agreement is the grammatical gender and number of the noun a phrase is actually about, not the reader: "%lld app blokeret" (singular, `en` gender) / "%lld apps blokeret" (`screenTime.restrictedApps.appsRestricted`), and a fragment with no visible noun needs the noun named rather than a bare, ungendered participle standing alone (`## Header and label forms`).
- The only reader-gender surface left is the picker itself, four plain nouns: `user.gender.female`, `.male`, `.other`, `.preferNotToSay`. The stored profile gender (`Manifesting/Features/User/Domain/Entities/Gender.swift`) is not a copy input without a product decision.
- Grep aid, not a validator, for the archaic formal register slipping in: `De`/`Dem`/`Deres` capitalised mid-sentence where it means "you"/"your" rather than "they"/"their", and the formal imperative pattern "De bedes" ("you are asked to").

## Header and label forms

- Sentence case everywhere in app-owned copy: Danish capitalises the first word and proper names only, never every content word. Apple's Danish multi-word strings do this even for menu labels ("Hjemmeskærm & appbibliotek", `Applications/Preferences.app | Localizable.strings | Home Screen & App Library`; "Rediger hjemmeskærm", `SpringBoardHome.strings | REARRANGE_ICONS_SHORTCUT_ITEM_TITLE`). The 114 Title Case `en` keys lose their inner capitals in `da` unless the capitalised word is a proper name from `## Platform terms` ("Edit Focus Task" -> "Rediger fokusopgave", `focusTask.createTask.screenTitleEdit`; "Total Screen Time" -> "Skærmtid i alt", `profile.appUsage.totalScreenTimeTitle`).
- Eyebrows and stat captions stay uppercase where `en` is uppercase (34 `en` strings have no lower-case letter): MOOD -> STEMNING, DAY STREAK -> the `streak_term` open decision's form in caps, EST. TIME VALUE -> ANSLÅET TIDSVÆRDI, TODAY -> I DAG, "%@ / HOUR" -> "%@ / TIME".
- Uppercasing Danish is safe and lossless: æ ø å keep their marks and have standard uppercase forms (Æ Ø Å), and Danish has no dotted/dotless i pair (that is Turkish) and no ß (that is German), so `.textCase(.uppercase)` and `String.uppercased()` need no locale argument for `da`; the cost of uppercase is width, not correctness (`## Length trigger`).
- Section headers default to the unmarked (common/neuter singular) participle as a category label, not a plural agreeing with the listed items: PLANNED / DONE / ANYTIME (`plan.dailyPlan.plannedHeader`, `.doneHeader`, `.anytimeHeader`) -> PLANLAGT / UDFØRT / NÅR DET PASSER; "Recently earned" (`achievements.gallery.recentSection`) -> "Senest optjent". The same unmarked form now covers `completed_count_header` in `## Control forms`, so the DONE-type header is one form everywhere rather than singular in one place and plural ("Udførte") in another.
- Tab titles are single nouns (`main.tab.*`): Moneyfesting, Plan, Statistik, Profil.
- Toggles and state labels take the passive past participle or an adjective agreeing in gender and number with the labelled noun, never with the reader: "%lld app restricted" -> "%lld app blokeret" (singular, common gender) / "%lld apps blokeret" (plural) (`screenTime.restrictedApps.appsRestricted`); "Focus paused" -> "Fokus sat på pause" (`widgets.startFocus.paused`); "In progress" -> "I gang" (`widgets.startFocus.inProgress`).
- A fragment with no visible noun names the subject rather than standing on a bare participle, because Danish past participles used as adjectives still need a noun to agree with in gender and number: `widgets.streak.copy.frozen.small` "Covered by a freeze." -> "Gårsdagen blev dækket af en frys.", beside `widgets.streak.a11y.frozen` "Yesterday was covered by a freeze." -> "I går blev dagen dækket af en frys." The packet's `agreement_target` names the noun for the editor to check.
- Status pills that describe the action rather than the reader take the participle alone ("Gemt", `common.editor.saved`).

## Numbers, dates, currency and punctuation

Foundation and ICU output below is `da_DK` on the 2026-09-16 harvest; invisible characters are named.

- Runtime values arrive formatted through `AppLanguage.effectiveLocale` (app `CLAUDE.md:42`), so strings never hard-code an amount or a currency name. Grouping is U+002E FULL STOP and the decimal mark is U+002C COMMA — the exact opposite of English's own scalars, not merely different symbols: "2.000", "12.000", "1.250,5", "1.250.000". Four-digit numbers are grouped, so "2,000" in a literal becomes "2.000" ("2,000 hours of focus", `achievements.badge.wageYear.name` -> "2.000 timers fokus").
- Money: `AppCurrency.format` prints "120 DKK" and "1.250,5 DKK" with U+00A0 before the code, the code after the number; the locale's own standard form is "120,00 kr." (with the symbol "kr.", not the ISO code, and two decimal places). Compact figures print "1,25k DKK", "1,25m DKK", "1,25b DKK", while `da_DK`'s own compact tokens are "t" (thousand, no period in this position), "mio." (million) and "mia." (billion): "1,25 t DKK", "1,25 mio. DKK". That is an engineering finding to report, never something to translate around (open decision `compact_currency_suffix`); note that the locale's own thousand token "t" is visually identical to this app's own hour-duration abbreviation "t." above, an added source of confusion beside a money figure.
- Percent follows the number with U+00A0: "40 %", "100 %". So a badge reads "-56 %", not "-56%".
- Time is 24-hour (`Locale.hourCycle` h23, `DateFormatter` template `j` -> `HH`) and the separator is a period, not a colon: "09.41", "23.59". The axis labels `insights.appUsage.axisMidnight`, `axisSixAm`, `axisNoon`, `axisSixPm` and their `profile.appUsage.axis.*` and `screenTimeReport.axis*` twins become "0.00", "6.00", "12.00", "18.00". The widget countdown prints "1.45.00" and "25.00" (period, not colon, even in a countdown timer).
- Dates: numeric "31. dec. 2026"; wide "31. december 2026"; weekday "tirsdag"; weekday with a numeric day "tirsdag 12. maj".
- Ranges: no direct Apple `da` en-dash range was found in this harvest; follow the general rule and flag any numeric range key for a render check rather than assuming a spacing convention (`en` has 3 numeric-range keys).
- Quotation marks are “ ” at both ends (U+201C / U+201D, alternates U+2018 / U+2019 — the same delimiters as English), which is what Foundation/ICU report for `da_DK` and what the app's own formatter will therefore produce. This differs from the traditional Danish printing convention (Dansk Sprognævn's own recommended „ ‟, or the older »…«/›…‹ style; "Retskrivningsordbogen", `dsn.dk`, fetched 2026-09-16, and "Citationstegn", `da.wikipedia.org`, fetched 2026-09-16) — follow the ICU output the app actually renders, and see `## Decided conflicts`. The 11 `en` keys with a straight `"` take “ ” (`moneyfesting.details.quoteWrapper` -> "“%@”", `notifications.contentExtension.quoteDecoration`, `moneyfesting.main.quotePlaceholder`, `theme.createTheme.sampleQuote`, `widgets.quotes.placeholderText`); the mock-alert keys are governed by `## Platform terms` instead, because Apple's own Danish mock alerts keep quotes around the app name (unlike `fi`).
- Ellipsis follows the source character per key: 9 `en` keys use three periods and 2 use "…"; do not normalise one into the other. Attribution keeps the source's dash (`quote.display.attribution`), and the multiplication sign × stays × ("Dine fokustimer × din timesats", `achievements.category.wages.description`).
- Plural categories are `one` and `other` only (ICU 76.1.0.0 / CLDR 46.0.0.0, and Xcode's `variationConfigFile.plist` `PluralRule.ComplementedPluralKeys.da` lists the same two). `one` is selected by the bare integer 1, and — unlike `en`, `ro` or `fi` — also by any decimal whose integer part is 0 or 1 (0.5 and 1.5 both select `one`); `other` covers every other integer including 0, and every decimal whose integer part is 2 or more (2.5 selects `other`). Exercise 0, 1, 2, 11, 21, 101 and 1000 for integers, and 0.5, 1.5 and 2.5 for decimals, and note the two traps: 0 is `other`, and a decimal can be `one` even when its rounded value would read as plural.
- Numerals attach to nouns as ordinary Germanic count nouns, not a special case form: "1 dag" (one) / "2 dage" (other), "1 minut" / "2 minutter", "1 time" / "2 timer", exactly mirroring the plural split above — there is no separate partitive or genitive form the way Finnish requires.
- Six `en` keys have no plural variations but need the `one`/`other` split in Danish for the same reason `ro` and `fi` split them: `achievements.streak.body`, `achievements.streak.comebackBody`, `achievements.streak.headline`, `achievements.streak.milestoneBody`, `achievements.streak.nextMilestoneBody`, `achievements.streak.weekAccessibility`. Report them as variation additions, not as prose fixes.
- When the counted phrase is not the subject, the numeral and its noun still inflect together only for definiteness and number, never for case ("en 5-dages [stribe]", "efter %lld dage"); a composed string that concatenates a number with a fixed noun fragment is far less fragile in Danish than in a case language, but still flag a concatenation that would need the noun's plural form to change under the placeholder, and propose one merged key with a format string rather than splicing (`references/agent-protocol.md` `## A. ## Length trigger

- A Danish string longer than 1.15× its English length goes on the render list (`scripts/catalog_check.py` C5), not into findings. The ratio is measured from Apple's own Danish translations at iOS 26.1 (23B86), 77,922 English/Danish pairs from `da.lproj` of which 44,339 are unique. On the unique, unweighted set 12.4% sit above 1.35×; 1.15× is the ratio, rounded to 0.05, that Apple's own `da` translations exceed as often as Apple's `ro` translations exceed 1.35× (31.6% by the ro-catalog-matched statistic; the recomputed unrounded match value is 1.17).
- Across all 77,922 pairs the median is 1.067, p75 1.229, p90 1.429, and 14.0% exceed 1.35×. Short strings expand hardest, exactly where this app's controls and captions live: 1-10 characters median 1.0, p90 1.625, 22.8% above 1.35×; the 946 all-caps pairs run even hotter (median 1.0, p90 1.75, 24.6% above 1.35× in the 1-10 character band).
- Counts never establish fit; verdicts come from render evidence against each view's own floor and line limit (`references/surface-budgets.md`, `references/render-evidence.md`). Shortening cuts words, never facts; accessibility strings are exempt.

## Emoji and intensity

- Preserve every source emoji and add none: 4 `en` keys carry one (`onboarding.notificationsSetup.motivationOn` 💪🏼, `motivationOff` 😞, `screenTime.createRestrictedList.selectionInstructions` ⚠️, and the `🔥` key literal).

## Open decisions

- Streak term (`streak_term`). Keys: the `streak` concept's whole surface, `widgets.streak.*` (about 30), `share.card.streak*`, `streakFreeze.*` (11), `paywall.benefit.streak.*`, `onboarding.welcome.slide.streak.*`, `insights.summary.dayStreakTitle`, `achievements.streak.*`. Rule: one head noun per concept, decided once and applied everywhere (`glossary/concepts.json` `streak`: consecutive days of enough focus, a fact about the calendar, not praise). Options: "stribe", the everyday Danish word language- and habit-tracking apps reach for to describe an unbroken run, attested for this exact sense by general Danish dictionaries (techdico.com, bab.la, both fetched 2026-09-16) though not by any Apple string; "serie", a cognate that Danish sports writing uses for a winning run ("sejrsserie") and that Microsoft's own da-DK support page for the Rewards streak feature uses standalone for this exact referent — "din Microsoft Rewards-serie", "beskytter din serie" (support.microsoft.com/da-dk/topic/microsoft-rewards-streak-beskyttelse-bc0753f8-be5b-4284-9fcd-ee93946ec822, fetched 2026-09-16) — a real, shipped, consumer-facing precedent that reads fine on its own for a persistent day-streak, not only the broader "series of items" sense; "række", the everyday word for "a row" that ties directly to "i træk" ("in a row"), the phrase Apple's own Fitness translations use instead of any noun (`## Idioms and figurative language`). Recommended: "stribe", still, but only weakly over "serie" — its case rests on general-dictionary evidence for this exact sense, and it does not need a qualifying phrase, while "serie" is real shipped precedent but the dictionary evidence for "serie" naming this exact referent unaided is thinner outside the one Microsoft example. This is close enough to a real product call that the owner should read both examples before the first batch rather than take the dictionary-only case at face value. Meanwhile agents write the days instead of the noun ("%lld dage i træk"), exactly matching Apple's own practice, never pick per string, and the adjudicator lists every key that touched the concept; nothing enters `glossary/da.json` `terms` until the owner closes this.
- Focus session term (`focus_session`). Keys: `moneyfesting.details.*` (33), `focusTask.*`, `widgets.startFocus.*`, `profile.sessionDetails.*`, `onboarding.welcome.preview.*`, `profile.home.focusSection`, `insights.focus.title`, `onboarding.dailyFocusGoal.*`. Rule: one term per concept, and the app's own focus session is not the iOS Focus mode. Options: "fokusperiode", a solid Danish compound from the verb Apple itself uses for focusing ("hjælpe dig med at fokusere", iOS 26.1 (23B86) runtime: `System/Library/CoreServices/AccessibilityUIServer.app/Extensions/AccessibilityAppIntents.appex | Localizable.strings | BACKGROUND_SOUNDS_DESCRIPTION`), and aligned with Apple's own system mode name "Fokus" (`Applications/Preferences.app | Localizable.strings | Focus`); "koncentrationsperiode", built on the competing everyday word Apple itself also uses in a comparable line ("forbedre koncentrationen i klasseværelset", `System/Library/PrivateFrameworks/SchoolTimeSettingsUI.framework | SchoolTimeSettings.strings | SCHEDULE_FEATURE_EXPLANATION_FOOTER`), which avoids any overlap with the system Focus mode's name but reads a step more clinical; "session", Apple's own bare loanword for a technical session (`System/Library/PrivateFrameworks/SensorKitUI.framework/AuthGroups/ECG.bundle | InfoPlist.strings | Session`), which fits a medical or technical register rather than a consumer-facing coach. Recommended: "fokusperiode", because it stays in plain, everyday Danish and deliberately leans into the same root Apple's own system Focus mode uses, rather than avoiding it — the overlap is a shared root, not a shared referent, and the app never claims to be the iOS Focus mode. Meanwhile a new string follows its own screen's majority and the packet's same-source siblings, and the adjudicator lists every key that touched the term.
- Mock permission alerts (`mock_permission_alerts`). Keys: `onboarding.screenTimePermission.alertMessage` (title and buttons already match Apple's own English closely enough to mirror verbatim without a decision; see the Platform terms row). Rule: a mock of an iOS alert mirrors the device verbatim. The app's own `en` mock is an imperative ("Provide "%@" access to Screen Time to allow it to see your activity data...") while Apple's own English (and its Danish translation) is a conditional gerund ("Providing "%@" access to Screen Time may allow it to see..." -> "Hvis du giver "%@" adgang til Skærmtid, kan appen muligvis se..."), so the two structures do not match even before translation. Options: translate Apple's Danish wording verbatim, which changes the sentence's shape relative to the app's own `en` mock; translate the app's own `en` imperative shape into Danish, which keeps parity with the app's other mock ("Provide access to allow it to see...") but no longer mirrors the device precisely. Recommended: Apple's Danish wording verbatim, because the same onboarding step raises the real alert moments later and a preview in a different shape shows the reader a screen iOS will not present. A yes needs a source flag first, because the `en` mock already differs from Apple's own English, independent of any Danish work. Meanwhile the key stays unwritten in Danish and findings go to an audit scope the owner names.
- Compact currency suffix (`compact_currency_suffix`). Keys: every surface that renders a compact amount, `widgets.focusRing.row.money`, `widgets.monthlyTimeValue.*`, `insights.savings.*`, `screenTimeReport.*`, and the insights, celebration and session screens (`Manifesting/Foundation/Localization/AppCurrency.swift:53-56`). Rule: a figure the app formats is engineering, not copy; a locale brief reports it and never writes around it. `AppCurrency.formatCompact` appends a hardcoded Latin "k", "m" or "b", so Danish renders "1,25k DKK" and "1,25m DKK", while `da_DK`'s own compact tokens are "t", "mio." and "mia." ("1,25 t DKK", "1,25 mio. DKK"; Foundation `Decimal.FormatStyle.Currency(...).notation(.compactName)`). A second, Danish-specific wrinkle: the locale's own thousand token "t" is visually identical to this app's own hour-duration abbreviation "t." used a few lines away in the same insights and session screens (`## Numbers, dates, currency and punctuation`), so even after fixing the Latin letters, "t" beside a money figure risks being read as an hour count. Options: keep "k"/"m"/"b" for Danish and accept a form no Danish reader writes; use the locale's own compact notation ("t"/"mio."/"mia."), which changes the formatter for every locale, not just `da`, and still leaves the "t"/hour collision to resolve separately (for example by keeping the currency code visible, "1,25 t DKK", rather than a bare symbol, so "t" always sits beside a currency marker). Recommended: the locale's own notation with the currency code kept visible, because the suffix sits directly against a money figure the money frame already asks readers to read carefully, and a Latin "k" beside a currency code reads as a bug regardless of the second wrinkle. Meanwhile agents never translate around the suffix, never spell it into copy, and report it once per batch that renders a compact amount; accessibility labels use the full format and are unaffected.
