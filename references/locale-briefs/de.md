## Audience and register

- A friendly coach in everyday German: short sentences, one idea per line, concrete verbs, no jargon and no English sentence shapes. A reader who knows no English must understand each line at first read, which rules out the loanwords German tech writing reaches for (`Streak`, `tracken`, `Reminder`, `Goal`) even though a German reader would recognise them.
- Drop the source's scaffolding: 15 `en` keys carry "please" (14 capitalised; en-inventory `## Please (15)`), and German UI copy for this kind of message never writes "Bitte" — `common.editor.deleteFailedMessage` "The deletion couldn't be saved. Please try again." is one sentence plus "Versuch es erneut."
- German writes compounds closed, but hyphenates a compound whose non-German element stays untranslated. Apple's own German shows both patterns side by side in the same harvest: closed where every element is native (`Bildschirmzeit`, `Fokus`), hyphenated where an English word is kept as-is (`Home-Bildschirm`, `Home-Bildschirm & App-Mediathek`) — the ordinary Duden rule for compounding with a foreign or untranslated element. Spaced-out compounds ("Fokus Sitzung", "Streak Widget") are the single most recognisable mark of a translated German app and the first thing the editor should flag.
- Imported shapes the editor flags. The first two columns are the marker list the coordinator appends to the editor prompt, target text only; the key column stays coordinator-side (`references/agent-protocol.md` `## Packet separation`). **The German examples are agent-authored illustrations, not shipped copy** — no `de` string exists yet.

## Address and gender

- **`du`/`dir`/`dein` throughout, never `Sie`.** Apple's German iOS uses `du` in every register-bearing string harvested: "„%@“ möchte dir Mitteilungen senden" (`UserNotificationsServer.framework | Localizable.strings | USER_NOTIFICATION_PERMISSION_ALERT_TITLE`), "Mit einer Zugriffserlaubnis auf die Bildschirmzeit kann „%@“ deine Aktivitätsdaten einsehen…" (`FamilyControlsAuthenticationUI.app | Localizable.strings | NOTIFICATION_AUTH_REQUEST_MESSAGE_INDIVIDUAL`), "Du kannst die Gesundheitsdatenkategorien später in der Health-App aktivieren." (`HealthUI.framework | HealthUI-Localizable.strings | AUTHORIZATION_DONT_ALLOW_ALERT_MESSAGE`), "Erhalte eine Mitteilung, wenn dein Wochenbericht verfügbar ist… wie oft du dein Gerät in die Hand genommen hast." (`ScreenTimeSettingsUI.framework | Localizable.strings | WeeklyReportNotificationGroupSpecifierFooterText`). No `Sie` form appears anywhere in the harvest (platform-terms `register_summary`), and Apple's own German support pages address the reader the same way: "Mit „Bildschirmzeit“ kannst du verschiedene Einstellungen … nutzen …" (https://support.apple.com/de-de/105121, fetched 2026-09-16) and "Halte auf dem Home-Bildschirm ein Widget … gedrückt…" (https://support.apple.com/de-de/118610, fetched 2026-09-16). This settles the caution to check the harvest before assuming `Sie`: the mock alerts need no register switch, because iOS German and app-owned German both say `du`.
- Use the unstressed `dir`/`dein` by default; `Sie`/`Ihr`/`Ihnen` anywhere is a finding.
- The app speaks about itself in the first person plural, as `en` does in 19 units (en-inventory `## App voice in first person plural`): "We couldn't load the data." -> "Wir konnten die Daten nicht laden." (`component.errorView.message`), "We covered %@ with a streak freeze." (`streakFreeze.usedBody`), "we'll use %@" (`onboarding.hourlyWage.footer`), "We'll try again soon." (`widgets.focusRing.a11y.unavailable`). Keep `wir`.
- **Gender.** German predicate adjectives and participles do not inflect for the reader, so the shapes that force a choice in Romance locales are already neutral here (predicative position is invariant in German exactly as it is in Dutch and English). The 44 reader-predicate `en` units (`you_are` 5, `are_you` 5, `you_showed_up` 1, `welcome_back` 1, `ready` 7, `focused` 19, `done` 15 — en-inventory `## Reader predicates`) need no gender strategy at all.
- The one hotspot is **role nouns**, confined to the badge family: `achievements.badge.hired.name` "Hired", `.salaried.name` "Salaried", `.rehired.name` "Rehired", `.clockedIn.name` "Clocked in", `.backOnShift.name` "Back on shift", `.permanent.name` "Permanent", `.tenure.name` "Tenure", plus the category names `achievements.category.payroll.name` "Payroll" and `.shift.name` "The Shift". German has the whole workplace vocabulary available as participles, adjectives or plain nouns, so no agent noun is ever needed here: "Eingestempelt" (Clocked in), "Eingestellt" (Hired), "Auf Gehalt" (Salaried), "Wieder angeheuert" (Rehired), "Zurück im Dienst" (Back on shift), "Festangestellt" (Permanent — contract status, distinct from the pay-type sense "Auf Gehalt" takes for Salaried), "Doppelschicht" / "Lange Schicht" / "Volle Schicht" (the shift-length badges), "Die Lohnliste" (Payroll), "Die Schicht" (The Shift), "Dienstjahre" (Tenure, a plain HR noun for seniority, shorter than the payroll-paperwork register of "Betriebszugehörigkeit"). Never a paired or slashed form ("Mitarbeiter/in"), never a generic-masculine agent noun aimed at the reader.
- `user.gender.*` is a data field, not voice: `user.gender.female` "Female", `.male` "Male", `.other` "Other", `.preferNotToSay` "Prefer not to say" — seed 2026-09-16: "Weiblich", "Männlich", "Divers", "Keine Angabe". "Divers" is Germany's own legal term for a third gender option, introduced into the *Personenstandsgesetz* in 2018, not a coinage. The stored profile gender (`Manifesting/Features/User/Domain/Entities/Gender.swift`) is not a copy input without a product decision.
- Grep aid, not a validator: `\bSie\b`, `\bIhr\b`, `\bIhre\b`, `\bIhnen\b`, and paired or slashed gendered forms such as `Mitarbeiter/in`, `Angestellte(r)`. Bare `-er` is not a stem: it is the ending of countless ordinary German nouns and adjectives that have nothing to do with a gendered role.

## Header and label forms

- **German capitalizes every noun, in every position, regardless of any style rule** (*Substantivgroßschreibung* — this is German orthography, not a design choice). "Sentence case" for German therefore means something narrower than it does for English, Dutch or Romanian: lower-case the sentence-initial capital that Title Case scatters onto adjectives and verbs, but every noun anywhere in the line keeps its capital either way. The 114 Title Case `en` keys (en-inventory `## Case of 2-4 word strings`) become German sentence case with nouns still capitalized: "Set Your Hourly Wage" -> "Lege deinen Stundensatz fest" (`onboarding.hourlyWage.title`) keeps the capital on "Stundensatz" because it is a noun, not because of Title Case; "Edit Focus Task" -> "Fokusaufgabe bearbeiten" (`focusTask.createTask.screenTitleEdit`); "My Tasks" -> "Meine Aufgaben" (`focusTask.picker.myTasksHeader`). Proper names keep their capitals regardless: Moneyfesting, Bildschirmzeit, Face ID, App Store, Fotos.
- **Uppercase eyebrows and captions stay uppercase where `en` is** (34 all-caps `en` strings: `insights.mood.mood` "MOOD", `insights.summary.dayStreakTitle` "DAY STREAK", `widgets.focusRing.title` "TODAY'S FOCUS", `plan.dailyPlan.{planned,done,anytime}Header`, `%@ / HOUR`, `/%lld MIN` — en-inventory `## All-caps en strings`). One German-specific hazard applies:
  - **ß uppercases to SS.** `"straße".uppercased(with: Locale(identifier: "de_DE"))` returns `STRASSE` on Foundation, host macOS Version 15.7.9 (Build 24G830) — the same host that produced `locale-formats.json` — and the root locale gives the same result; `"ß".uppercased()` alone also gives `SS`. So an eyebrow or caption typed by hand must write `STRASSE`, never `STRAßE`, and `.textCase(.uppercase)` already produces this. No catalog string at snapshot contains ß, so this is a standing rule rather than a live finding.
  - German uppercasing otherwise round-trips cleanly: umlauts keep their diaereses when uppercased (`ÜBER`, `GRÖSSE`), and there is no dotted/dotless `i` hazard.
  - Uppercase captions expand hardest here: of Apple's own all-caps German strings of 1-10 English characters, 39.2% exceed 1.35× and 8.0% exceed 2× (length-stats `all_caps`, bucket 1-10). Prefer the shorter everyday noun over the exact one and file the row when none exists (`references/surface-budgets.md` `## Catalog microcopy templates`).
- Section headers are plural nouns or participles agreeing with the listed items; the three all-caps plan headers take participles ("GEPLANT", "ERLEDIGT", "UNGEPLANT" — seed 2026-09-16, the call site appends the count).
- Tab titles are bare nouns: Moneyfesting, Plan, Statistiken, Profil (`main.tab.*`; seed 2026-09-16 — "Einblicke" is the literal for "Insights" but the tab shows charts and totals, so "Statistiken" wins on plainness, mirroring the same reasoning the Dutch brief used for its own tab noun).
- Toggles and state labels take a predicative participle or adjective, which does not inflect in German any more than it does in Dutch: "Eingefroren", "Erledigt" (seed 2026-09-16, no Apple citation for this exact app state in the current harvest — a gap to close with a device check before the achievements batch).
- **The German agreement rule for a fragment with no visible noun is grammatical gender — with three genders to track, not two.** Any article, demonstrative or attributive adjective in the fragment depends on whether the implied noun is `der`, `die` or `das`, so the packet's `agreement_target` must name it: `der Fokus`, `der Tag`, `der Wert`/`der Zeitwert`, `der Stundensatz`, `der Bildschirm` (hence `der Home-Bildschirm`) against `die Aufgabe`, `die Sitzung`, `die Serie`, `die Liste` against `das Ziel` and — the one genuinely non-obvious case — **`das Widget`**. Apple's own German fixes the gender: the indefinite article stays `ein Widget`, not the masculine accusative `einen Widget` ("Halte auf dem Home-Bildschirm ein Widget oder einen leeren Bereich gedrückt…", https://support.apple.com/de-de/118610, fetched 2026-09-16), and the genitive `-s` in "Durch Entfernen dieses Widgets werden keine Apps oder Daten gelöscht." (`SpringBoardHome.framework | SpringBoardHome.strings | REMOVE_WIDGET_BODY_REMOVE_DATA`) is consistent with a neuter noun. Example of the fault the editor checks: `widgets.streak.copy.frozen.small` "Covered by a freeze." beside `.medium` "Yesterday was covered." — the small tile's fragment has no noun, so a gender-free participle like "Abgedeckt." is safe while an article-bearing rewrite needs the right one.

## Numbers, dates, currency and punctuation

All values below are Foundation/ICU output for `de_DE` (locale-formats, ICU 76.1.0.0 / CLDR 46.0.0.0), or the app's own `AppCurrency` path on that locale.

- **Numbers.** Grouping separator `.` (U+002E), decimal separator `,` (U+002C): `2000` -> "2.000", `12000` -> "12.000", `1250.5` -> "1.250,5", `1250000` -> "1.250.000". Four-digit numbers are grouped, so a literal in copy follows suit: `achievements.badge.wageYear.*` "2,000 hours of focus" -> "2.000 Stunden Fokus".
- **Money.** Runtime values arrive formatted through `AppLanguage.effectiveLocale`, so no string hard-codes a currency. `AppCurrency.format` gives "120 €" and "1.250,5 €" with a **no-break space** (U+00A0) between number and symbol; the locale's own standard form is "120,00 €". Compact: the app prints "1,25k €" / "1,25m €" / "1,25b €" where the locale prints no thousands abbreviation at all (plain "1250 €") and "1,25 Mio. €" / "1,25 Mrd." for millions and billions (Identity, engineering flags). Never write a currency name into copy.
- **Percent** carries a no-break space before the sign: "40 %", "100 %" (U+00A0 + U+0025). "%lld%%" and "-%lld%%" should carry that space where the render allows it.
- **Time** is 24-hour (`Locale.hourCycle` h23): "09:41", "23:59"; the shortened form drops the leading zero ("9:41"). Foundation's `DateFormatter` template `j` resolves to `HH 'Uhr'` for German — a detail worth checking against any hand-built time string, since German sometimes spells the word "Uhr" where `en`/`ro` use a bare colon format. The twelve `en` clock literals (`12AM`, `06PM` and kin on `insights.appUsage.{axisMidnight,axisNoon,axisSixAm,axisSixPm}`, `profile.appUsage.axis.*`, `screenTimeReport.{axisMidnight,axisNoon,axisSixAM,axisSixPM}`) become "00:00", "06:00", "12:00", "18:00" — which is also why "Mitternacht" should never appear in German chart copy (Money frame, widgets bans).
- **Dates**: "31. Dez. 2026", "31. Dezember 2026", "12. Mai", "Dienstag, 12. Mai". Month and weekday names are lower case mid-sentence, capitalized when they are the sentence-initial word (ordinary German capitalization, not a special date rule).
- **Ranges** take an en dash without spaces: "1–280 Zeichen" (`onboarding.personalGoal.validation`), "5–120 Minuten" (`widgets.startFocus.description`), and `onboarding.deviceUsage.oneToThreeHours` "1-3 hours" is normalised to "1–3 Stunden".
- **Dashes and marks**: the two em dashes (`achievements.streak.comebackBody`, `share.alert.savedToPhotosMessage`) stay em dashes with spaces, matching German attribution convention; the quote attribution `quote.display.attribution` keeps its en dash "– %@"; the five spaced-hyphen author decorations keep the hyphen the source uses; the interpunct "·" is preserved in the eight middle-dot keys; ellipsis follows the source's own split (2 keys use "…", 9 use "...") until an owner call.
- **Quotation marks are settled, not an open item.** Foundation reports `„` (U+201E) / `“` (U+201C) as the primary delimiter for `de_DE` and `‚` (U+201A) / `'` (U+2018) as the alternate (for a quotation nested inside another). This is exactly what Apple's own German UI does: "„%@“ möchte auf die Bildschirmzeit zugreifen" (`FamilyControlsAuthenticationUI.app | Localizable.strings | NOTIFICATION_AUTH_REQUEST_TITLE`) and "„%@“ möchte Objekte zu „Fotos“ hinzufügen." (`TCC.framework | Localizable.strings | REQUEST_ACCESS_SERVICE_kTCCServicePhotosAdd`). Use `„ … “` for the 11 `en` keys carrying a straight `"` (`moneyfesting.details.quoteWrapper`, `notifications.contentExtension.quoteDecoration`, `moneyfesting.main.quotePlaceholder`, `theme.createTheme.sampleQuote`, `widgets.quotes.placeholderText`, `focusTask.picker.emptyMessage`, `onboarding.alert.screenTimeMessage`, `onboarding.notificationPermission.alertTitle`, `onboarding.screenTimePermission.{alertTitle,alertMessage,message}`; en-inventory `## straight double quote (11)`), and a mock or quoted iOS label follows the device exactly, including any nested pair.
- **Plurals: two categories, `one` and `other`** (ICU and Xcode's `variationConfigFile.plist` agree; no optional categories) — the same two categories `en` itself uses. `one` selects on the integer 1 alone; `other` selects on 0, on every integer from 2 up, and on every fraction tested (0.5, 1.5, 2.5). Boundary integers to exercise: **0, 1, 2, 11, 20, 21, 100, 101, 120, 1 000 000**, plus the fraction 1,5 for the decimal hours line (`widgets.cash.focusedHoursDecimal` "%@ focused hours", which is not a plural key and reads "1,5 Stunden Fokus"). Because German's categories match `en`'s one-for-one, most plural keys need no restructuring at all — the exception is the six single-`stringUnit` keys flagged in Identity's engineering table.
- **How numerals attach to nouns.** The numeral stands before the bare noun with an ordinary space and no linking word: "1 Tag", "3 Tage", "%lld Minuten". There is no Romanian-style partitive, so a single `%lld` never forces anything beyond the ordinary singular/plural choice.
- Six keys are a single `en` `stringUnit` but need German `one`/`other` branches, because the count is inside the sentence: `achievements.streak.body`, `.comebackBody`, `.headline`, `.milestoneBody`, `.nextMilestoneBody`, `.weekAccessibility`.

**Verbatim rule.** A string that quotes an iOS label or mocks an iOS alert equals Apple's German value exactly — the mock alert bodies and buttons (`onboarding.screenTimePermission.{alertTitle,alertMessage,alertDoNotAllow}`, `onboarding.notificationPermission.{alertTitle,alertMessage,alertAllow,alertDoNotAllow}`), the `onboarding.widgets.howTo.*` mirror labels, and the steps that quote them (`step2`, `step5`) — abbreviations, no-break spaces and Apple's own quotation habits travel with the value. App-owned copy keeps `du` and uses Apple's app and feature names; deviate only with a recorded reason. The Continue mismatch (Control forms, Open decisions `continue_mirror_mismatch`) and the abbreviated Edit pill ("Bearb.") are the two places in this table where following this rule literally means writing something a fluent German speaker would not write unprompted — both are called out explicitly for that reason.

Fixed names for the translator prompt: the brand row above, the Apple terms in this table, quote authors, and the restricted-apps group literal "No Restricted Apps" (`ToDoTaskRepository.swift:333`, unlocalized — flag it, never translate it in place). See `references/voice-card.md` `## Brand and fixed names`.

## Length trigger

- **1.35×.** A German string longer than 1.35 times its English length goes on the render list; `scripts/catalog_check.py` C5 puts it there, and it is never a finding by itself. The ratio is measured from Apple's own German translations: over unique English/German pairs (unweighted), German exceeds 1.35× in 28.6% of pairs (unrounded match at 1.335×), which is the ratio that reproduces the same share Apple's `ro` exceeds at 1.35× (31.6%, the Apple statistic closest to the `ro` catalog's own 299 of 1135); a flat 1.35× is therefore used directly, since rounding to the nearest 0.05 lands exactly there (length-stats `recommendation`).
- Where it bites: median ratio 1.222 over all 77,450 English/German pairs (44,489 unique), but 1.25 for English strings of 1-10 characters with 42.8% over 1.35×, against 1.228 and 22.9% for strings of 41-120 characters (length-stats `metrics.chars`). All-caps strings of 1-10 characters are the worst case: 39.2% over 1.35× and 8.0% over 2× — exactly the eyebrows and widget captions (`insights.mood.mood` "MOOD", `widgets.focusRing.row.money` "TIME VALUE, 30 DAYS", `insights.summary.lastThirtyDaysSubtitle` "LAST 30 DAYS").
- No catalog share can be quoted for `de`: there are 0 German units at the snapshot. Re-state this line with a real share after the first wave.
- Counts never establish fit. Verdicts come from render evidence against each view's own floor and line limit (`references/surface-budgets.md`, `references/render-evidence.md`). Shortening cuts words, never facts; accessibility strings are exempt.

## Decided conflicts

| Conflict | Winner | Rule |
|---|---|---|
| Address: `Sie` versus `du` | `du` throughout, mocks included | Apple's German iOS uses `du` in every register-bearing string harvested and has no `Sie` form; two fetched Apple support pages (support.apple.com/de-de/105121, /118610) address the reader the same way. Precedence line: Apple's German wording for anything mirroring iOS |
| Button form: infinitive versus conjugated imperative | infinitive ("Abbrechen", "Bearbeiten", "Erneut versuchen") | Apple's runtime uses the infinitive for 8 of 9 harvested generic controls, and Mozilla's German localization style guide states the rule explicitly against the du-imperative (fetched 2026-09-16) |
| Compound spelling: closed versus hyphenated | closed for native elements ("Bildschirmzeit", "Fokussitzung"), hyphenated only where an untranslated foreign element is kept ("Home-Bildschirm") | Duden's compounding rule for a foreign or proper-noun element; Apple's own German shows both patterns side by side in the same harvest |
| Case: Title Case versus German sentence case | German sentence case, with every noun still capitalized regardless of position | German orthography (*Substantivgroßschreibung*) capitalizes all nouns; 114 `en` keys carrying Title Case do not carry that convention into German, but they do keep every noun's capital |
| Uppercase ß | ß -> SS in an uppercased caption | Foundation `"straße".uppercased(with: Locale(identifier: "de_DE"))` returns "STRASSE" on host macOS 15.7.9 (Build 24G830), the same host that produced `locale-formats.json` |
| Continue: one shared key versus two Apple wordings | flagged as a finding for the owner, not silently resolved by the brief | `UIKitCore`'s generic Continue is "Weiter" while `FamilyControlsAuthenticationUI`'s Screen Time access alert Continue is "Fortfahren"; the app has a single shared key `common.continue`, so the brief cannot make both true at once (Open decisions `continue_mirror_mismatch`) |
| Discard and Delete would both be "Löschen" | the object spelled out ("Änderungen verwerfen") | the discard sense keeps a distinct verb ("verwerfen") from the delete sense ("löschen"), so a discard control never reads as deleting the quote |
| Minute unit: bare "m" versus "Min." | "Min." for every label and running line; single-letter units only in the share-card narrow triple, itself a seed | Foundation's German abbreviated minute unit is "Min." ("1 Std., 45 Min."); a bare "m" beside a number would be ambiguous the way it is in Dutch, though German has no metre-abbreviation collision specifically — the fuller abbreviation is simply what Foundation itself produces |

| `Add Widget`: one key or two | one key | iOS 26.1 German labels the edit-menu item and the gallery button identically ("Widget hinzufügen"), so the `ro`/`he`/`tr`-style split does not arise |

## Open decisions

Each item is mirrored in `glossary/de.json` `open` with the fields `scripts/term_audit.py` reads. Nothing here is translated yet, so "meanwhile" means what the first batches do, not which shipped keys stay locked.

- **`streak_term` — the noun for a streak.** Affects every noun slot: `widgets.streak.smallHeader` "Streak", `.currentStreakHeader` "Current streak", `.displayName` "Focus Streak", `.daysCount`, `.dayStreakCount`, `insights.summary.dayStreakTitle` "DAY STREAK", `share.card.streakEyebrow`, `achievements.streak.title`, `onboarding.welcome.slide.streak.{eyebrow,headline}`, `paywall.benefit.streak.title`, the whole `streakFreeze.*` family. Rule: everyday German a reader without English understands at first read, and it must fit a one-word widget header and an uppercase caption (Length trigger). Options: **"Serie"** — the plain German noun (5 characters), used generically for winning/losing streaks in sport, fills every header and takes a compound ("Serienwidget"); no Apple citation for this exact sense was found in this harvest (gap). **"Tage in Folge"** — a plain phrase, natural in running text, but cannot fill a one-word header or a compact compound the way a noun can. **"Streak"** — the English loan several German apps use; ruled out by the skill's own rule that a reader who knows no English must understand each line. Recommended: **"Serie" in noun slots, with "Tage in Folge" as the phrase in running text**, on the same reasoning the Dutch and Romanian briefs reached independently for their own equivalent nouns. Meanwhile: the first batch writes "Serie" in headers and "Tage in Folge" in sentences and lists every key that touched the term, so a flip is one recorded global change rather than a per-string choice.
- **`streak_freeze_term` — the noun and verb for a streak freeze.** Keys: the 12 `streakFreeze.*`, `widgets.streak.copy.frozen.*` and `paywall.benefit.streak.detail` keys. Rule: everyday German that keeps the "frozen" image the source relies on, and that still works as a countable noun where a badge or inventory count needs one ("%lld freezes ready"). Options: **"Frost"** — a short, punchy noun that fits a one-word widget label ("1 Frost verfügbar") and pairs with a verb phrase "die Serie einfrieren"; no Apple citation and no verified precedent, purely an agent proposal, and a web check for how German speakers discuss the identical Duolingo streak-freeze mechanic surfaced only the verb-phrase pattern ("den Streak einfrieren"), never a coined noun resembling "Frost" — a real risk, since "Frost" is ordinarily an uncountable weather noun in German and its rare plural "Fröste" reads oddly pluralized as a UI count ("%lld Fröste bereit"). **"Frostschutz"/"Serienschutz"** — a compound built the way German normally coins a countable protection-noun, avoiding bare "Frost"'s countability problem at the cost of being a longer, still-uncited coinage that needs a native check before the pilot. **"Serie einfrieren" as a verb phrase only, with no matching noun coined** — avoids inventing a noun German does not already have for this sense, at the cost of leaving badge and inventory labels without a clean short form. **"Streak-Freeze"** — the English loan some fitness and language-learning apps keep in German; ruled out for the same reason as the English loan for `streak_term`. Recommended: no option is decided with confidence; test **"Frostschutz"** or **"Serienschutz"** with a native reviewer before the pilot rather than shipping bare "Frost", which the countability evidence above argues against for exactly the countable-noun slots this term exists to fill. If a native check is not available before the first batch, prefer the verb-phrase-only option on badge/inventory copy and flag the missing noun as a gap. Meanwhile: the first batch marks every use of "Frost"/"Frostschutz" as a proposal and lists the keys touched, so a flip is one recorded change.
- **`badge_conceit` — whether the workplace metaphor travels into German.** Keys: the 24 `achievements.badge.*.name` values and the 5 `achievements.category.*.name` values, with their `.earned` lines. Rule: one conceit per locale, decided once and kept across the family, gender-neutral, with plain descriptors left plain (`references/project-context.md` `## Content policy`). Options: **keep it** — German has the vocabulary natively and entirely through participles, adjectives and plain nouns, with no gendered agent noun anywhere in the family: "Eingestempelt", "Eingestellt", "Auf Gehalt", "Wieder angeheuert", "Zurück im Dienst", "Festangestellt", "Doppelschicht", "Die Lohnliste", "Die Schicht", "Dienstjahre"; **drop it** — replace the employment names with plain achievement names ("Erste Sitzung", "Sieben Tage in Folge", "Fünfzig volle Tage"), which loses the wink but removes any reading of the app as an employer, next to a money frame that already has to say five times that nothing is earned. Recommended: **keep it**, because the conceit is what makes the badge family cohere in `en` and German carries it with ordinary HR vocabulary rather than jargon or gendered role nouns; the money frame is protected by the fixed disclaimers and the banned-word table, not by the badge names. Meanwhile: the first achievements batch drafts the conceit, marks every name a proposal, and the adjudicator reports the whole family together so a flip is one batch.
- **`continue_mirror_mismatch` — the Screen Time access alert's Continue button versus the app's generic Continue.** Key: `common.continue`, shared by the generic Continue control and quoted inside `onboarding.alert.screenTimeMessage` to describe the mock alert's own button. Rule: a string that mocks an iOS alert equals Apple's value verbatim (Platform terms `## Verbatim rule`). Evidence: `UIKitCore.framework | Localizable.strings | Continue` gives "Weiter"; `FamilyControlsAuthenticationUI.app | Localizable.strings | COMMON_CONTINUE` gives "Fortfahren" for the same concept in the Screen Time alert specifically — the two Apple frameworks disagree with each other in German, unlike in Dutch, where they happened to coincide. Options: **keep the single shared key at "Weiter"** — accepts a small mismatch against the mocked alert's own wording, no code change needed; **add a dedicated key for the mirror sense at "Fortfahren"** — a faithful mock, at the cost of a new catalog key and call-site change (an engineering ask, not a translation-only fix). Recommended: **add the dedicated key**, because the entire purpose of a mock alert is to look identical to the real one, and a visible mismatch on the one button most likely to be compared side by side with the device undermines that. Meanwhile: ship the shared key as "Weiter" and record the mismatch in the report; do not add the key without the owner.
- **`de_at_audience` — whom the German copy is written for.** Affects vocabulary across the catalog, not any single key. Rule: one `de` resource serves both audiences, because `de-DE` and `de-AT` both negotiate to `de` and iOS ships only `de.lproj` (apple-coverage `system_negotiation`). Options: **Germany wording** — the larger market, and iOS's own `de.lproj` on this host reads as standard German rather than Austrian German throughout the harvest; **Austria-aware wording** — check the handful of terms Austrian German spells or uses differently (for instance calendar month names, though this app does not write them as literal copy) before the first batch. Recommended: **Germany wording**, with a lighter case than the Dutch brief's Netherlands/Belgium item made for its own locale, because both the Germany and Austria App Store storefronts already default to German (unlike Belgium's English-first default), so there is less risk that Austria-specific vocabulary would read as wrong rather than merely unfamiliar. Meanwhile: write Germany German, and record any term an editor flags as Austria-divergent in the batch report rather than hedging the line.
