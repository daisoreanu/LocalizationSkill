## Identity

- Tag `es-ES`. iOS ships only `es.lproj` (Spain Spanish; no `es_ES.lproj` and no `es-ES.lproj` on disk, 24 of 26 UI bundles, missing only in `UIKit.framework` and `ScreenTime.framework`); `es-ES` negotiates to `es` in all six checked system bundles. App Store Connect's code is `es-ES` (Spain storefront: default Spanish (Spain), additional Catalan and English (U.K.)). `xcstringstool compile` writes an `es-ES.lproj` for the app catalog; `LOCLocaleCode.plist` has no entry for `es-ES` itself, only for bare `es` (displayName "Spanish", lproj `es`) (apple-coverage 2026-09-16).
- **`es-ES` is the recommended catalog tag, not a bare `es`.** With `[en, es-ES, es-MX]` an `es-ES` device gets the Spain text while `es-MX`, `es-419` and `es-US` devices get the Mexico text; a bare `es` next to `es-MX` would instead pull `es-419` and `es-US` readers into the Spain text (apple-coverage `tag_reason`, `host_fallback_checks`). This brief covers Spain only: Latin American and US Spanish readers are `es-MX`'s brief, written in parallel, and no form here should be chosen because it is standard in Latin American Spanish.
- Audience: Spanish speakers in Spain on an iPhone in Spanish; Latin script, left to right; format locale `es_ES`, audience currency EUR.
- Snapshot 2026-09-16 against catalog commit `c8a49dd`. Spanish (Spain) units in the catalog: 0. There is no shipped `es-ES` copy to count, no `needs_review` backlog and no baseline to defer to.
- Precedence: app `CLAUDE.md`, then Apple's own Spanish (Spain) wording for anything that mirrors iOS (Platform terms), then this brief. `glossary/es-ES.json` records the outcomes.
- **Every row here is agent research. No native speaker, owner or certified translator has reviewed it.** I am an AI language specialist, not a native person, and nobody has reviewed this brief. A native read of the hero set, the money frame and the shield pairs is recommended before the pilot batch (`references/agent-protocol.md` `## Human review`); the pilot's findings are corrections to this brief before any fan-out.
- Tone examples: none yet; the first batch returns six rows in the `references/examples-ro.md` format, which the coordinator saves as `references/examples-es-ES.md` (SKILL.md `## Build context before words`). Budgets: `references/surface-budgets.md`.

## Audience and register

- A friendly coach in everyday Spanish (Spain): short sentences, one idea per line, concrete verbs, no jargon, no scaffolding. Apple's own register evidence (`platform-terms` register summary) confirms sentence case, infinitive buttons and second-person `tú` throughout; that register carries into app-owned copy (Address and gender).
- Drop the source's scaffolding: 15 `en` keys carry "please" (`common.editor.deleteFailedMessage` "The deletion couldn't be saved. Please try again." is one), and Spanish app copy never writes "Por favor" as a stock opener — Apple's own error and retry strings never use it either ("Vuelve a intentarlo", "Inténtalo de nuevo"). The line becomes "No se ha podido guardar la eliminación. Inténtalo de nuevo." — one imperative, no "por favor".
- Drop the English possessive habit where the referent is obvious: a Spanish sentence names the actor with the verb, not with a possessive on every noun. `onboarding.widgets.howTo.placedConfirmation` "It's now on your Home Screen." reads as "Ya está en la pantalla de inicio." (the possessive "tu pantalla de inicio" is not wrong, but the plain article reads less translated once the sentence already has a subject).

## Address and gender

- **`tú` throughout, never `usted`.** Apple's Spanish (Spain) iOS uses `tú` in every register-bearing string harvested: "Si permites que %@ acceda a..." (`FamilyControlsAuthenticationUI.app | Localizable.strings | NOTIFICATION_AUTH_REQUEST_MESSAGE_INDIVIDUAL`), "Puedes activar las categorías de datos de salud en otro momento..." (`HealthUI.framework | HealthUI-Localizable.strings | AUTHORIZATION_DONT_ALLOW_ALERT_MESSAGE`), "Recibe una notificación cuando tu informe semanal esté disponible..." (`ScreenTimeSettingsUI.framework | Localizable.strings | WeeklyReportNotificationGroupSpecifierFooterText`). No `usted` form appears anywhere in the harvest (platform-terms `## Register evidence`). App buttons stay infinitive ("Permitir", "No permitir", "Continuar"), matching Apple's own generic controls.
- The app speaks about itself in the first person plural, as `en` does in 19 units: "We couldn't load the data." -> "No hemos podido cargar los datos." (`component.errorView.message`); keep "hemos"/"vamos a", not the more distant "se ha podido".
- **Gender.** Spanish inflects predicate adjectives and past participles for the reader ("estás listo/lista"), which the source's reader-predicate sentences do not need to say at all: 44 `en` units of this shape (`you_are`, `are_you`, `you_showed_up`, `welcome_back`, `ready`, `focused`, `done`) are handled by rewriting around the adjective rather than by a slash form — "You're ready" as "Ya puedes empezar" (a verb phrase, not "Estás listo/a"), never "Estás listo/a" and never a doubled ending. Second-person imperatives and the present tense stay neutral by construction (`references/locale-decisions.md` `## Voice choices that need locale review`).
- The one hotspot is **role nouns**, confined to the badge family: `achievements.badge.hired.name` "Hired", `.salaried.name` "Salaried", `.rehired.name` "Rehired", `.clockedIn.name` "Clocked in", `.backOnShift.name` "Back on shift", `.permanent.name` "Permanent", `.tenure.name` "Tenure", plus `achievements.category.payroll.name` "Payroll" and `.shift.name` "The Shift". Strategy, in this order: (1) a noun for the state or the event rather than an agent adjective — "Fichado" (clocked in, the everyday Spanish verb for punching a time clock), "Contratación" (hired), "Indefinido" (permanent — reusing the Spanish employment-law term for a permanent contract, "contrato indefinido", which needs no gendered agreement because it modifies the invisible noun "contrato"), "Antigüedad" (tenure), "Vuelta al turno" (back on shift); (2) where an agent noun cannot be avoided, the common-gender form, never a paired or slashed one ("trabajador/a"). Whether the workplace conceit travels into Spanish at all is an owner call (Open decisions `badge_conceit`).
- `user.gender.*` is a data field, not voice: `.female` "Female", `.male` "Male", `.other` "Other", `.preferNotToSay` "Prefer not to say" — seed 2026-09-16: "Mujer", "Hombre", "Otro", "Prefiero no decirlo". The stored profile gender (`Manifesting/Features/User/Domain/Entities/Gender.swift`) is not a copy input without a product decision.
- Grep aid, not a validator: `\busted\b`, `\bél o ella\b`, `\bsu/su\b`, and any slashed pair `o/a` glued onto an adjective or noun.

## Header and label forms

- **Sentence case everywhere in app-owned copy.** Apple's Spanish is sentence case throughout the harvest ("Añadir widget", "Editar pantalla de inicio", "Gestionar suscripciones"), and locale-decisions.md's own catalog-majority reading of `en` (314 of 413 two-to-four-word strings, 61 of 89 button strings, 67 of 111 title strings) is already sentence case, so Spanish needs no case conversion rule beyond following that majority. The minority Title Case `en` keys become sentence case: "Set Your Hourly Wage" -> "Establece tu tarifa por hora" (`onboarding.hourlyWage.title`). Proper names keep their capitals: Moneyfesting, Tiempo de uso, Face ID, App Store, Fotos, Salud, Mensajes, Ajustes.
- **Uppercase eyebrows and captions stay uppercase where `en` is** (34 all-caps `en` strings: `insights.mood.mood` "MOOD", `insights.summary.dayStreakTitle` "DAY STREAK", `widgets.focusRing.title` "TODAY'S FOCUS", `plan.dailyPlan.plannedHeader`/`.doneHeader`/`.anytimeHeader`). Spanish has no case-folding hazard like German ß or Turkish dotted/dotless i; accented vowels drop their accent when uppercased in the traditional rule but modern usage and Apple's own UI keep them ("MÁS INFORMACIÓN"), so an eyebrow typed by hand keeps its accents and `.textCase(.uppercase)` already renders correctly.
- Uppercase captions expand hardest here: of Apple's own all-caps Spanish (Spain) strings of 1-10 English characters, 37.0% exceed 1.35× and 16.9% exceed 2× (length-stats `all_caps`). Prefer the shorter everyday noun over the exact one and file the row when none exists (`references/surface-budgets.md` `## Catalog microcopy templates`).
- Section headers are plural nouns or participles agreeing with the listed items; the three all-caps plan headers take participles ("PLANIFICADO", "COMPLETADO", "EN CUALQUIER MOMENTO" — seed 2026-09-16, the call site appends the count).
- Toggles and state labels take a predicative participle: "Congelado", "Completado", "Bloqueado" (`Blocked` -> Apple's own Spanish is confirmed elsewhere in this catalog family as "restringido"/"bloqueado" depending on the concept — see Platform terms and `restricted_list` in the glossary). A participle used as a standalone state label with no visible referent defaults to masculine singular, the same convention Spanish badge and status systems already use generically.
- **The Spanish agreement rule for a fragment with no visible noun is grammatical gender, not number**, exactly as for Dutch's `de`/`het` split, but Spanish only has two genders and no neuter escape: the packet's `agreement_target` must name the implied noun so an editor can pick the article and adjective ending correctly — "la racha", "la tarea", "la sesión", "la lista", "el objetivo", "la tarifa", "el widget" (masculine, confirmed by Apple's own "Haz una pulsación larga en los widgets para editarlos.", `SpringBoardHome.framework | SpringBoardHome.strings | WIDGET_DISCOVERABILITY_DESCRIPTION`). Example of the fault the editor checks: `widgets.streak.copy.frozen.small` "Covered by a freeze." beside `.medium` "Yesterday was covered." — the small tile's fragment has no noun, so an invariable form like "Cubierto por una congelación." is safe while an agreeing adjective glued onto an implied feminine noun without checking gender is a finding.

## Numbers, dates, currency and punctuation

All values below are Foundation/ICU output for `es_ES` (locale-formats, ICU 76.1.0.0 / CLDR 46.0.0.0), or the app's own `AppCurrency` path on that locale.

- **Numbers.** Grouping separator `.` (U+002E), decimal separator `,` (U+002C): `1250.5` -> "1250,5", `1250000` -> "1.250.000". **Four-digit numbers stay ungrouped**: `2000` renders as "2000", not "2.000", while `12000` groups to "12.000" (locale-formats `numbers.four_digit_grouping`). This means a literal like `achievements.badge.wageYear.name` "2,000 hours of focus" becomes "2000 horas de enfoque" in Spanish, with no thousands separator — a detail an editor unfamiliar with this quirk would "fix" incorrectly.
- **Money.** Runtime values arrive formatted through `AppLanguage.effectiveLocale`, so no string hard-codes a currency (`en` has no "EUR" or "euro" literal; the placeholder carries the amount, `references/project-context.md` `## Currency display`). `AppCurrency.format` gives "120 €" and "1250,5 €" with a **no-break space** (U+00A0) between the number and the symbol; the locale's own standard form is "120,00 €". Compact figures are the engineering flag above (Identity): never translate around the `k`/`m`/`b` collision. Never write a currency name into copy.
- **Percent** is followed by a no-break space then the sign: "40 %", "100 %" (U+00A0, U+0025) — unlike `en`'s glued "40%". "%lld%%" keeps the source's glued form only where the render is already fixed; a spelled percent in running prose takes the space.
- **Time** is 24-hour (`Locale.hourCycle` h23, `DateFormatter` template `j` -> `H`): "9:41", "23:59" — Spanish does **not** zero-pad a single-digit hour in either the full or the shortened form, unlike Romanian's "09:41". The twelve `en` clock literals (`12AM`, `06PM` and kin on `insights.appUsage.axisMidnight` and its siblings) become "0:00", "6:00", "12:00", "18:00".
- **Dates**: "31 dic 2026" (abbreviated month, no "de"), "31 de diciembre de 2026" (wide), "martes" (weekday alone), "martes, 12 may" (weekday + abbreviated month + day). Month and weekday names are lower case in Spanish, mid-sentence and at the start of a label alike.
- **Quotation marks are settled, not open.** ICU reports `“ ”` (U+201C/U+201D) as the primary delimiter for `es_ES` and `‘ ’` as the alternate (locale-formats `quotes`), and Apple's own Spanish (Spain) UI uses the same curly double form around a feature name: "quiere acceder a "Tiempo de uso"" is actually stored as `"%@" quiere acceder a “Tiempo de uso”` with real curly quotes (`FamilyControlsAuthenticationUI.app | Localizable.strings | NOTIFICATION_AUTH_REQUEST_TITLE`), and "“%@” quiere añadir ítems a tu fototeca" the same way (`TCC.framework | Localizable.strings | REQUEST_ACCESS_SERVICE_kTCCServicePhotosAdd`). Use `“ ”` for a displayed quotation or a quoted UI label, `‘ ’` for a quotation nested inside one; the 11 `en` keys using a straight `"` (`moneyfesting.details.quoteWrapper`, `component.errorView.message` and others) convert on sight.
- **Plurals: three ICU categories, `one`, `many`, `other`, but only `one` and `other` are reachable below one million** (locale-formats `plural.categories_by_integer_below_1000000`). `one` selects on the integer 1 alone; `other` selects on 0, every integer 2 through 999 999, and every fraction tested (0.5, 1.5, 2.5); `many` appears only at 1 000 000 and above (1 000 000, 2 000 000, 10 000 000 all select it; 999 999 and 1 000 001 do not). Xcode's own plural table for `es-ES` lists only `one`/`other` and omits `many`; a hand-added `many` variant still compiles and is honoured at runtime, but nothing in this app's catalog is expected to reach a seven-digit count, so `many` is optional, never required. Boundary integers to exercise: **0, 1, 2, 20, 21, 100, 101, 1 000 000**, plus the fraction 1,5.
- **How numerals attach to nouns.** The numeral stands before the noun with an ordinary space and no linking word: "1 día", "3 días", "%lld minutos"; there is no Romanian-style partitive, so a single `%lld` never forces a special plural rewrite beyond the ordinary `one`/`other` split above.

## Length trigger

- **1.35×.** A Spanish (Spain) string longer than 1.35 times its English length goes on the render list; `scripts/catalog_check.py` C5 puts it there, and it is never a finding by itself. The ratio is measured from Apple's own Spanish (Spain) translations: across 45,663 unique English/Spanish pairs in the iOS 26.1 runtime, 31.43% exceed 1.35×, which is the rounded ratio that matches the Romanian calibration point (Apple's `ro` exceeds 1.35× in 31.6% of pairs, the statistic closest to the `ro` catalog's 299 of 1135) — length-stats `recommendation`.
- Where it bites: short strings expand more than long ones. Median ratio 1.25 for English strings of 1-10 characters (38.3% over 1.35×), against 1.146 and 15.2% for strings of 41-120 characters. All-caps strings of 1-10 characters are the worst case: 37.0% over 1.35× and 16.9% over 2× — exactly the eyebrows and widget captions (`insights.mood.mood` "MOOD", `widgets.focusRing.title` "TODAY'S FOCUS", `insights.summary.dayStreakTitle` "DAY STREAK").
- No catalog share can be quoted for `es-ES`: there are 0 Spanish (Spain) units at the snapshot. Re-state this line with a real share after the first wave.
- Counts never establish fit. Verdicts come from render evidence against each view's own floor and line limit (`references/surface-budgets.md`, `references/render-evidence.md`). Shortening cuts words, never facts; accessibility strings are exempt.

- Preserve every source emoji and add none: 4 `en` keys carry one (`onboarding.notificationsSetup.motivationOn` "On 💪🏼", `.motivationOff` "Off 😞", `screenTime.createRestrictedList.selectionInstructions` "⚠️ You need to select...", and one key literal that is not user-facing copy).
- Exclamation marks only where the source has one: 3 `en` keys (`plan.dailyPlan.futureEmptyMessage`, `plan.dailyPlan.timelinePotentialSuffix`, `streakFreeze.info.body`). These are source lines to flag, not a licence to add more (`references/voice-card.md`). Peninsular Spanish marketing copy often opens an exclamation with `¡`; where the app does add one under this rule, it takes both marks, `¡...!`, never the closing mark alone. A brief may soften intensity, never raise it.
- Tests that ban intensity: `ManifestingTests/Onboarding/OnboardingWidgetsStepTests.swift:203-204` bans "!" on the widgets step for `en` and `ro`; the Spanish word list and the paywall hero's are under `## Money frame`, as proposals for extending those tests.

## Decided conflicts

| Conflict | Winner | Rule |
|---|---|---|
| Address: `tú` versus `usted` | `tú` throughout, mocks included | Apple's Spanish (Spain) iOS uses `tú` in every register-bearing string harvested and has no `usted` form. Precedence line: Apple's Spanish wording for anything mirroring iOS |
| Button form: infinitive versus imperative | infinitive ("Cancelar", "Guardar", "Omitir") | Apple's runtime uses the infinitive for every generic button, and the register summary states it directly ("buttons: infinitive") |
| Case: Title Case versus sentence case on app-owned copy | sentence case | Apple's Spanish is sentence case throughout; Title Case is an English convention that locale-decisions.md already finds in the minority of `en` keys, so Spanish follows the catalog's own majority convention as well as Apple's |
| "Done" mirror versus "Done" in general use | Apple's literal value ("OK") only where the string quotes an iOS label (`ios_mirror_pill`); "Listo" everywhere else, app-owned | Apple's Spanish for "Done" is genuinely "OK" in every harvested instance, including the exact Home Screen edit-mode pill this app quotes — but a general in-app "finished" button reading "OK" would be read as a dialog confirmation rather than a completion, so app-owned copy deviates with this recorded reason while the mirror role stays exact |
| "Got it" versus Apple's literal mirror | "Entendido", app-owned | Apple's own value for this exact English phrase is "OK" in two harvested instances, but neither is a mirror of an iOS label this app quotes (both are unrelated system features acknowledging a tip), so the app is free to use the fuller, warmer everyday word for a non-imperative acknowledgement |
| Compact money suffix `k`/`m`/`b` against the locale's `mil`/`M`/`mM` | engineering finding, not a translation choice, severity **high** | `AppCurrency.formatCompact` hardcodes the Latin suffix; the collision between the app's "m" (millions) and the locale's own "mil" (thousands) is worse than the low-severity cases Apple's `en` and `ro` show, because the two abbreviations share a first letter across a thousand-fold difference in magnitude (`compact_finding`) |
| Four-digit number grouping | ungrouped ("2000", not "2.000") | Foundation's own `es_ES` output leaves four-digit integers ungrouped while grouping five digits and up (locale-formats `numbers.four_digit_grouping`); an editor who "corrects" a bare 2000 into 2.000 is introducing an error, not fixing one |

## Open decisions

Each item is mirrored in `glossary/es-ES.json` `open` with the fields `scripts/term_audit.py` reads. Nothing here is translated yet, so "meanwhile" means what the first batches do, not which shipped keys stay locked.

- **`focus_session_term` — whether the app's own "focus session" concept shares Apple's word for the system Focus mode.** Affects every key naming a session: `widgets.startFocus.*`, `onboarding.welcome.preview.*`, `profile.sessionDetails.*`, `insights.focus.title`, `onboarding.dailyFocusGoal.*`; the timer screen itself stays "Moneyfesting" per `glossary/concepts.json` `focus_session`, not this noun. Options: **"sesión de concentración"** — reuses Apple's own Spanish for the system Focus mode ("Modos de concentración", `Applications/Preferences.app | Localizable.strings | Focus`), which is the word a Spanish reader already associates with focus/attention on iOS, but it overlaps the name of a distinct system feature the app never touches, on the bare label `profile.home.focusSection` "Focus" and wherever the word appears without "sesión" attached. **"sesión de enfoque"** — a fully plain, everyday Spanish noun with zero overlap with any Apple feature name, at the cost of not reusing the word iOS itself trained the reader on. Recommended: **"sesión de enfoque"**, because the Screen Time permission flow sits right next to Apple's own Focus-mode language in the same onboarding sequence, and a reader who has just seen "Modos de concentración" in Settings should not also read the app's unrelated concept under the same word. Meanwhile: the first batch writes "enfoque" in every session key and lists every key that touched the term, so a flip is one recorded global change rather than a per-string choice.
- **`badge_conceit` — whether the workplace metaphor travels into Spanish.** Keys: the 24 `achievements.badge.*.name` values and the 5 `achievements.category.*.name` values, with their `.earned` lines. Rule: one conceit per locale, decided once and kept across the family, gender-neutral, with plain descriptors left plain (`references/project-context.md` `## Content policy`). Options: **keep it** — Spanish has the vocabulary natively, and mostly as invariable nouns rather than gendered role adjectives: "Fichado", "Contratación", "Indefinido", "Recontratación", "Vuelta al turno", "Nómina", "El turno", "La remontada" (Idioms and figurative language); **drop it** — replace the employment names with plain achievement names ("Primera sesión", "Siete días seguidos", "Cincuenta días completos"), which loses the wink but removes any reading of the app as an employer, next to a money frame that already has to say five times that nothing is earned. Recommended: **keep it**, because the conceit is what makes the badge family cohere in `en`, and the Spanish vocabulary above carries it with ordinary words rather than gendered role nouns; the money frame is protected by the fixed disclaimers and the banned-word table, not by the badge names. Meanwhile: the first achievements batch drafts the conceit, marks every name a proposal, and the adjudicator reports the whole family together so a flip is one batch.
- **`streak_freeze_image` — whether the freeze image survives the move into Spanish.** Keys: `streakFreeze.*` (11 total), `widgets.streak.copy.frozen.small`/`.medium`, `widgets.streak.a11y.frozen`, `paywall.benefit.streak.detail`. Options: **"congelación de racha"** — the literal image, backed by Spanish's own live use of "congelar" for pausing something abstract without ending it (a frozen bank account, frozen prices), so the metaphor is not a fresh coinage; **"protección de racha"** — a plain functional description of what the mechanic does (a freeze protects one missed day), with no image at all, avoiding any chance a reader takes "congelar" literally as stopping the streak rather than covering one gap in it. Recommended: **"congelación de racha"**, because Spanish already uses the freeze image for exactly this kind of abstract-pause sense elsewhere, and the fixed rule that one freeze covers one day, never two (`StreakFreeze.swift:6`, `cap = 1`), travels in the sentence regardless of which noun is chosen. Meanwhile: the first streak batch writes "congelación" throughout and flags every key it touches, so a flip to the plainer noun is one batch, not a per-string rewrite.
