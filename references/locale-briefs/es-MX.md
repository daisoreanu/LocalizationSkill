## Audience and register

- A friendly coach in everyday Mexican-facing Spanish: short sentences, one idea per line, concrete verbs, no jargon and no English sentence shapes carried over word for word. Apple's own `es_419` register is `tú` and infinitive-verb buttons throughout (Address and gender, Control forms), so the app's existing dry, second-person voice needs no register invention — only vocabulary.
- Drop the source's scaffolding: 15 `en` keys carry "please" (14 capitalised), and Apple's own `es_419` never writes "por favor" on a retry or save message — `common.editor.deleteFailedMessage` "The deletion couldn't be saved. Please try again." is one sentence plus "Inténtalo de nuevo." (or, mirroring the harvested error-retry wording, "Vuelve a intentarlo.").
- **Gender is the real hotspot, not address.** Spanish predicate adjectives and participles agree with the referent's gender; where the referent is the reader, Romance-locale practice is to prefer verb tenses and invariable forms over predicate adjectives (`references/locale-decisions.md` `## Voice choices…`). The 44 reader-predicate `en` units (`you_are` 5, `are_you` 5, `you_showed_up` 1, `welcome_back` 1, `ready` 7, `focused` 19, `done` 15) mostly resolve without a gendered adjective once rendered as a verb: "You are making time for what matters." -> "Estás haciendo tiempo para lo que importa." (present progressive, no gender); "You showed up %lld days in a row." -> "Estuviste presente %lld días seguidos." (perfect/past, invariant); "and you're back" -> "y ya volviste" (verb, no gender), never "bienvenido/a" as a bare adjective.
- Imported shapes the editor flags. The first two columns are the marker list the coordinator appends to the editor prompt, target text only; the key column stays coordinator-side (`references/agent-protocol.md` `## Packet separation`). **The Spanish examples are agent-authored illustrations, not shipped copy** — no `es-MX` string exists yet.

## Address and gender

- **`tú` throughout, never `usted`.** Apple's `es_419` runtime uses `tú` in every register-bearing string harvested: "Puedes activar después las categorías de datos en la app Salud." (`HealthUI.framework | HealthUI-Localizable.strings | AUTHORIZATION_DONT_ALLOW_ALERT_MESSAGE`), "Si permites que %@ acceda a Tiempo en pantalla, también podría consultar tus datos de actividad..." (`FamilyControlsAuthenticationUI.app | Localizable.strings | NOTIFICATION_AUTH_REQUEST_MESSAGE_INDIVIDUAL`), "Recibe una notificación cuando tu reporte semanal esté disponible..." (`ScreenTimeSettingsUI.framework | Localizable.strings | WeeklyReportNotificationGroupSpecifierFooterText`). No `usted` form appears anywhere in the harvest (facts `platform-terms.md` `## Register evidence`, `## Register summary`: `"address": "tú"`). No register switch is needed between iOS mirrors and app-owned copy, the way `ro`'s mocks need one.
- **Buttons and menu items are bare infinitives**, not `tú`-imperatives: Apple's own controls read "Cancelar", "Guardar", "Editar", "Eliminar", "Cerrar", "Reintentar", "Permitir", "No permitir", "Continuar" (Control forms table below; facts `platform-terms.md` `## Register summary`: `"buttons": "infinitive: Permitir / No permitir / Continuar"`). This is the standard split in Spanish software: infinitive on the control, `tú` in the sentence around it — not a conflict to resolve, since both forms already coexist in the same harvested strings ("Si permites que %@ acceda..." next to a "Continuar" button).
- The app speaks about itself in the first person plural, as `en` does in 19 units: "We couldn't load the data." -> "No pudimos cargar los datos." (`component.errorView.message`), "We covered %@ with a streak freeze." -> "Cubrimos %@ con una congelación de racha." (`streakFreeze.usedBody`), "we'll use %@" -> "usaremos %@" (`onboarding.hourlyWage.footer`), "We'll try again soon." -> "Lo volveremos a intentar pronto." (`widgets.focusRing.a11y.unavailable`). `nosotros` verb conjugation carries no gender marking, so this voice needs no strategy at all.
- **Gender.** Unlike Dutch, Spanish participles and adjectives *do* inflect for the reader's gender, so the neutral strategy is to prefer verb tenses over predicate adjectives wherever the sentence allows it (Audience and register, above). Two structural facts help: the Spanish compound perfect (`haber` + past participle) keeps its participle invariable regardless of the subject's gender — "Has terminado tu primera sesión." never becomes "terminada" — so recasting a state adjective as a perfect-tense verb removes the gender question outright; and several of the 44 reader-predicate units already name an object, not the reader ("Session done" -> "Sesión terminada", agreeing with the feminine noun *sesión*, not the user).
- The one hotspot resistant to that trick is **role nouns**, confined to the badge family: `achievements.badge.hired.name` "Hired", `.salaried.name` "Salaried", `.rehired.name` "Rehired", `.clockedIn.name` "Clocked in", `.backOnShift.name` "Back on shift", `.permanent.name` "Permanent", `.tenure.name` "Tenure", plus the category names `achievements.category.payroll.name` "Payroll" and `.shift.name` "The Shift" — Spanish equivalents like "Contratado/a" or "Asalariado/a" are person-descriptor nouns that inflect. Strategy, in this order: (1) nominalize the event rather than naming the person — "Contratación" (a hiring, always grammatically feminine as an abstract noun, never describing the user's own gender) instead of "Contratado/a", the same move `ro`'s "Primul pontaj" ("First clock-in", a noun) makes for `clockedIn`; (2) where the source name is already invariable in Spanish, keep it as is — "Permanente" ends in `-e` and does not inflect; (3) never a slashed or doubled form ("Contratado/a"), never defaulting silently to the masculine as if it were neutral. Whether the whole workplace conceit travels into Spanish at all is an owner call (Open decisions `badge_conceit`).
- `user.gender.*` is a data field, not voice: `user.gender.female` "Female", `.male` "Male", `.other` "Other", `.preferNotToSay` "Prefer not to say" — seed 2026-09-16: "Femenino", "Masculino", "Otro", "Prefiero no decirlo". This is the one place a grammatically masculine default ("Otro") is standard Spanish form-field practice, not a voice choice about the reader. The stored profile gender (`Manifesting/Features/User/Domain/Entities/Gender.swift`) is not a copy input without a product decision.
- Grep aid, not a validator: `\busted\b`, `\bcontratad[oa]\b`, `\basalariad[oa]\b`, `\bpermanente\b` (fine, invariable — do not flag), and any bare `-ado`/`-ada` pair used as a slashed form (`contratado/a`).

## Header and label forms

- **Sentence case everywhere in app-owned copy.** Apple's `es_419` is sentence case throughout the harvest ("Tiempo en pantalla", "Restaurar compras faltantes", "Idioma y región", "Editar pantalla de inicio"), and Spanish orthography capitalises only the first word plus proper names. The 114 Title Case `en` keys become sentence case: "Set Your Hourly Wage" -> "Configura tu tarifa por hora" (`onboarding.hourlyWage.title`), "Edit Focus Task" -> "Editar tarea de concentración" (`focusTask.createTask.screenTitleEdit`), "My Tasks" -> "Mis tareas" (`focusTask.picker.myTasksHeader`). Proper names keep their capitals: Moneyfesting, Tiempo en pantalla, Face ID, App Store, Fotos, Salud, Mensajes.
- **Uppercase eyebrows and captions stay uppercase where `en` is** (34 all-caps `en` strings: `insights.mood.mood` "MOOD", `insights.summary.dayStreakTitle` "DAY STREAK", `widgets.focusRing.title` "TODAY'S FOCUS", `plan.dailyPlan.{planned,done,anytime}Header`, `%@ / HOUR`, `/%lld MIN`). One rule governs every accented word inside an uppercase caption: **the tilde is obligatory on capitals, not optional.** The RAE states this plainly — "el uso de la mayúscula no exime de poner la tilde cuando así lo exigen las reglas de acentuación" — with examples such as SÁNCHEZ, PERÚ, BOGOTÁ (RAE, "Acentuación de las mayúsculas", fetched 2026-09-16 via es.wikipedia.org/wiki/Acentuación_de_las_mayúsculas, which restates the Academy's rule and its 1990 reversal of the old typewriter-era exemption). So `insights.summary.dayStreakTitle` "DAY STREAK" -> "RACHA DE DÍAS" keeps the accent on DÍAS; dropping it to "DIAS" is a defect, not a style choice.
- **Uppercase captions expand hardest here.** Across 1-10 character all-caps English/`es-MX` pairs, 34.2% exceed 1.35× and 12.1% exceed 2× (length-stats `all_caps`, chars "1-10"); overall across every all-caps pair, 33.8% exceed 1.35× (length-stats `all_caps.metrics.chars.all`). Prefer the shorter everyday noun over the exact one and file the row when none exists (`references/surface-budgets.md` `## Catalog microcopy templates`).
- Section headers are plural nouns or participles agreeing with the listed items; the three all-caps plan headers take participles ("PLANEADO", "COMPLETADO", "EN CUALQUIER MOMENTO" — seed 2026-09-16; "COMPLETADO" is masculine because it agrees with the implied noun *lo completado* / *el día*, not with the user).
- Tab titles are bare nouns: Moneyfesting, Plan, Estadísticas, Perfil (`main.tab.*`; seed 2026-09-16 — a literal "Perspectivas" for "Insights" reads as translated where the tab shows charts and totals, so the plainer noun wins).
- Toggles and state labels take a predicative participle or adjective, which *does* inflect in Spanish — this is where the fragment-agreement rule below matters most: "Congelada." (agreeing with *racha*, fem), "Completado." (agreeing with *día*, masc), "Bloqueada" (`Blocked` -> the app's own Health/Screen Time sense; Apple's own generic label is simply "Configuración"-adjacent chrome, not a state adjective, so this row has no direct Apple mirror to cite and is app-owned).
- **The agreement rule for a fragment with no visible noun is grammatical gender (masculine/feminine), not number.** Any article, demonstrative or attributive adjective in the fragment depends on whether the implied noun is masculine or feminine, so the packet's `agreement_target` must name it: `la racha`, `la tarea`, `la sesión`, `la lista`, `la meta`, `la tarifa` against `el día`, `el valor`, `el objetivo`, and — confirmed by Apple's own `es_419` wording, not assumed — **`el widget`** ("Eliminar este widget no borrará sus apps ni datos.", `SpringBoardHome.framework | SpringBoardHome.strings | REMOVE_WIDGET_BODY_REMOVE_DATA`, masculine demonstrative "este"; "Mantén presionado un widget para editarlo.", `WIDGET_DISCOVERABILITY_DESCRIPTION`, masculine indefinite article "un"). Example of the fault the editor checks: `widgets.streak.copy.frozen.small` "Covered by a freeze." beside `.medium` "Yesterday was covered." — the small tile's fragment has no noun, so "Congelada." (agreeing with the unstated *racha*) is right while "Congelado." (agreeing with an unstated *día*) is a different, also-plausible reading the editor must pick deliberately, not by default.

## Numbers, dates, currency and punctuation

All values below are Foundation/ICU output for `es_MX` (locale-formats, ICU 76.1.0.0 / CLDR 46.0.0.0), or the app's own `AppCurrency` path on that locale.

- **Numbers.** Grouping separator `,` (U+002C), decimal separator `.` (U+002E): `2000` -> "2,000", `12000` -> "12,000", `1250.5` -> "1,250.5", `1250000` -> "1,250,000" — the same separators as `en`, unlike `ro`'s reversed pair. Four-digit numbers **are** grouped ("2,000"), matching `en`'s own convention.
- **Money.** Runtime values arrive formatted through `AppLanguage.effectiveLocale`, so no string hard-codes a currency (`en` has no "MXN" or "peso" literal; the placeholder carries the amount, `references/project-context.md` `## Currency display`). `AppCurrency.format` gives "MXN 120" and "MXN 1,250.5" with a **no-break space** (U+00A0) between the ISO code and the number — the app uses `isoCode` presentation for MXN (it is neither USD, EUR, JPY nor GBP, the four currencies `AppCurrency` gives a narrow symbol), while the locale's own standard form is "$120.00" (dollar sign, since Mexico also uses `$` for pesos). Compact: the app prints "MXN 1.25k" / "MXN 1.25m" / "MXN 1.25b" where the locale would print "1.25 k$" / "1.25 M$" (Identity, engineering flags). Never write a currency name such as "pesos" into copy — the currency is the user's own choice and independent of the language, and es-MX and es-ES users can each pick any of the 32 catalog currencies.
- **Percent** is glued after the number with no space: "40%", "100%" (U+0025) — the same shape as `en`. "%lld%%" and "-%lld%%" keep that shape.
- **Time is 12-hour with lower-case "a.m."/"p.m." and a narrow no-break space** (U+202F) before them, not `en`'s uppercase "AM"/"PM": "9:41 a.m.", "11:59 p.m." (`Locale.hourCycle` h12, `DateFormatter` template `j` -> `h[U+202F]a`). Unlike `ro` (which switches to 24-hour and drops the leading zero differently depending on device region), `es-MX`'s time format is identical whether the override runs on a same-region or an `en_US` device (locale-formats `app_locale_override_path`), so there is no region-dependent formatting risk to flag here. The twelve `en` clock literals (`12AM`, `06PM` and kin) become "12:00 a.m.", "6:00 p.m." — spelled with the locale's own a.m./p.m., not translated as "medianoche" in a chart axis (Money frame, widgets bans).
- **Dates**: "31 dic 2026" (no comma between day and year, unlike `en`'s "Dec 31, 2026"), "31 de diciembre de 2026", "12 de mayo", "martes 12 de may" (no comma after the weekday, unlike `en`'s "Tuesday, May 12"). Weekday and month names are lower case in Spanish, mid-sentence and at the start of a label alike.
- **Ranges** follow the source's own convention pending an editor check; the `en` catalog itself is inconsistent (an en dash in `onboarding.personalGoal.validation` "1–280 characters", a hyphen in `onboarding.deviceUsage.oneToThreeHours` "1-3 hours"), so normalise to the Spanish convention of a plain hyphen with no spaces for a numeric range ("1-280 caracteres", "5-120 minutos") unless a later house-style decision says otherwise.
- **Quotation marks: curly `“ ”`, no open decision needed.** Foundation reports `“`/`”` (U+201C/U+201D) as `es_MX`'s *primary* delimiter (with `‘`/`’` as the alternate) — the same shape `en` already uses, unlike Dutch's single-primary case — and Apple's own `es_419` UI drops quotation marks around a quoted feature name entirely where `en` and `ro` use them ("Si permites que %@ acceda a Tiempo en pantalla…" carries no quotes around either the app name or "Tiempo en pantalla", where the `en` source and the `ro` mirror both quote it). So the 11 `en` keys using a straight `"` (`moneyfesting.details.quoteWrapper`, `component.errorView.message`, etc.) take curly `“ ”` for a displayed quotation, and the mock alerts that mirror Apple drop the quotes on the feature name exactly as Apple does (Decided conflicts, Platform terms).
- **Plurals: three categories, `one`, `many` and `other`.** `one` selects on the integer 1 alone; `many` selects only at round multiples of 1,000,000 (tested hits: 1000000, 2000000, 10000000, 100000000, 1000000000 all select `many`; 1000001 and 1500000 both give `other`); every other integer from 0 to 999,999 and every fraction tested (0.5, 1.5, 2.5) gives `other` (locale-formats `es-MX.plural`). Boundary integers to exercise: **0, 1, 2, 11, 20, 21, 100, 101, 120, 999999, 1000000, 1000001**. The surprise worth a test row: 999999 is `other`, 1000000 is `many`, and 1000001 is back to `other` — a one-off `many` variant only matters if a string ever needs to name an exact round million, which nothing in the current catalog does (the largest counted noun is `achievements.badge.wageYear` "2,000 hours", well under the threshold). Xcode's own plural table for `es-MX` lists only `one`/`other` (`many` is ICU-only and optional but compiles and is honoured at runtime if hand-added; locale-formats `xcode_vs_icu`).
- **How numerals attach to nouns.** The numeral stands before the noun with an ordinary space and no linking word, exactly as `en` does: "1 día", "3 días", "%lld minutos". There is no Romanian-style partitive, so a single `%lld` never forces a special-case rewrite.

## Length trigger

- **1.30×.** A Spanish string longer than 1.30 times its English length goes on the render list; `scripts/catalog_check.py` C5 puts it there, and it is never a finding by itself. The ratio is measured from Apple's own `es_419` translations: across 48,236 unique English/Spanish pairs in the iOS 26.1 runtime, 31.8% exceed 1.30× — this is the rounded value chosen because it flags real `es-MX` expansion as often as Apple's `ro` exceeds 1.35× (the statistic closest to the `ro` catalog's own 299 of 1135 hits), while a flat 1.35× would flag only 26.6% and let real Spanish expansion through (length-stats `recommendation`).
- Where it bites: short strings expand most. Median ratio 1.182 overall, but the 1-10 character bucket has median 1.25 with 38.9% over 1.35× and 8.5% over 2.00×, against the 41-120 character bucket's median 1.102 and 8.1% over 1.35× (length-stats `metrics.chars`). All-caps strings of 1-10 characters are close behind: 34.2% over 1.35× and 12.1% over 2× — exactly the eyebrows and widget captions (`insights.mood.mood` "MOOD", `widgets.focusRing.row.money` "TIME VALUE, 30 DAYS", `insights.summary.lastThirtyDaysSubtitle` "LAST 30 DAYS").
- No catalog share can be quoted for `es-MX`: there are 0 Spanish units at the snapshot. Re-state this line with a real share after the first wave.
- Counts never establish fit. Verdicts come from render evidence against each view's own floor and line limit (`references/surface-budgets.md`, `references/render-evidence.md`). Shortening cuts words, never facts; accessibility strings are exempt.

## Emoji and intensity

- Preserve every source emoji and add none: 4 `en` keys carry one (`onboarding.notificationsSetup.motivationOn` "On 💪🏼", `.motivationOff` "Off 😞", `screenTime.createRestrictedList.selectionInstructions` "⚠️ You need to select…", and the `🔥` key literal, which is not user-facing copy).
- Exclamation marks only where the source has one: 3 `en` keys (`plan.dailyPlan.futureEmptyMessage`, `plan.dailyPlan.timelinePotentialSuffix`, `streakFreeze.info.body`). These are source lines to flag, not a licence to add more (`references/voice-card.md`). Latin American marketing copy reaches for the exclamation more readily than this product does; a brief may soften intensity, never raise it.
- Spanish uses an opening inverted mark on genuine exclamatory and interrogative sentences (¡…!, ¿…?), not on a plain declarative that happens to end with "!" as emphasis; since all 3 source exclamation keys are short declaratives ("Nothing planned for tomorrow yet!"-style lines), keep a single trailing "!" rather than adding an opening "¡" that would change the sentence's grammatical mood — this is an editor call to record per key, not a blanket rule.
- Tests that ban intensity: `ManifestingTests/Onboarding/OnboardingWidgetsStepTests.swift:203-204` bans "!" on the widgets step for `en` and `ro`; the Spanish word list and the paywall hero's are under `## Money frame`, as proposals for extending those tests.

## Decided conflicts

| Conflict | Winner | Rule |
|---|---|---|
| Address: `usted` versus `tú` | `tú` throughout, mocks included | Apple's `es_419` iOS uses `tú` in every register-bearing string harvested and has no `usted` form. Precedence line: Apple's own wording for anything mirroring iOS |
| Button form: infinitive versus `tú`-imperative | bare infinitive ("Cancelar", "Guardar", "Editar", "Eliminar") | Apple's runtime uses the infinitive for every control harvested, with no exception, so there is no competing convention to weigh |
| Case: Title Case versus sentence case on app-owned copy | sentence case | Apple's `es_419` is sentence case throughout; Title Case is an English convention, and 114 `en` keys carrying it do not carry it into Spanish |
| Quote marks: straight `"` carried from the source versus the locale's own curly marks | curly `“ ”`, and no quotes at all where Apple's own mirror drops them | Foundation reports `“`/`”` as `es_MX`'s primary delimiter (the same shape `en` already uses), and Apple's own mock alerts quote no app or feature name at all |
| Gender on role nouns (badge family) | nominalize the event, never gender the person | Spanish role nouns like "Contratado/a" inflect for the reader's gender; an abstract noun ("Contratación") describes the event, not the user, and needs no gender call at all |
| `Add Widget`: one key or two | one key | iOS 26.1 `es_419` labels the edit-menu item and the gallery button identically ("Agregar widget"), so no split is needed |
| Compact money suffix `k`/`m`/`b` against the locale's `k`/`M` | engineering finding, not a translation choice | `AppCurrency.formatCompact` hardcodes the Latin suffix; severity low (`compact_finding`) |
| Plural structure: `one`/`other` in the catalog versus the locale's third `many` category | ship `one`/`other`; add `many` only if a key ever needs an exact round million | Xcode's own `es-MX` plural table lists only `one`/`other`; ICU's `many` compiles and works if hand-added, but no current key reaches the million-scale threshold that selects it |

## Open decisions

Each item is mirrored in `glossary/es-MX.json` `open` with the fields `scripts/term_audit.py` reads. Nothing here is translated yet, so "meanwhile" means what the first batches do, not which shipped keys stay locked.

- **`badge_conceit` — whether the workplace metaphor travels into Spanish, and if so, how it avoids gendering the reader.** Keys: the 24 `achievements.badge.*.name` values and the 5 `achievements.category.*.name` values, with their `.earned` lines. Rule: one conceit per locale, decided once and kept across the family, gender-neutral, with plain descriptors left plain (`references/project-context.md` `## Content policy`). Options: **keep it, nominalized** — Spanish has the whole employment vocabulary natively, and every name can be phrased as an event noun rather than a person-descriptor: "Primer fichaje" (Clocked in), "Contratación" (Hired), "Sueldo fijo" (Salaried), "Recontratación" (Rehired), "Turno completo"/"Turno largo"/"Doble turno" (Full/Long/Double shift), "Antigüedad" (Tenure), "Nómina" (Payroll), "El turno" (The Shift); **drop it** — replace the employment names with plain achievement names ("Primera sesión", "Siete días seguidos", "Cincuenta días completos"), which loses the wink but removes any reading of the app as an employer, next to a money frame that already has to say five times that nothing is earned. Recommended: **keep it, nominalized**, because the conceit is what makes the badge family cohere in `en`, Spanish carries the vocabulary with ordinary words, and nominalizing every name (rather than defaulting to a masculine person-descriptor) satisfies the gender-neutral requirement without inventing awkward slashed forms. Meanwhile: the first achievements batch drafts the nominalized conceit, marks every name a proposal, and the adjudicator reports the whole family together so a flip is one batch.
- **`es_mx_audience_scope` — whether copy should read as Mexico-specific or pan-Latin-American-neutral.** Affects vocabulary across the catalog, not any single key. Rule: this one `es-MX` resource is what App Store Connect actually serves to Mexico, Argentina and Colombia (all three storefronts default to "Spanish (Mexico)") and is offered as an additional language in the United States storefront alongside eight other languages (apple-coverage `app_store_connect`, `storefront_rows`); a skeptic run of the bare-code override matrix also shows that once a language ships, storing the tag as `es` (never done here — the app's own tag is the full `es-MX`) would collapse 55 device regions onto this resource, from Argentina and Brazil to Canada and the United States (apple-coverage `risks`). Options: **Mexico-specific wording** — natural, idiomatic Mexican Spanish (register, vocabulary, everyday word choices a Mexican reader expects), at the cost of a handful of terms reading as slightly foreign to a Colombian or Argentine reader; **pan-Latin-American-neutral wording** — avoid Mexico-only colloquialisms in favor of the vocabulary Apple's own `es_419` chrome already uses throughout the device, at the cost of the copy reading slightly less natural to the plurality Mexican audience the App Store code names. Recommended: **Mexico-specific wording, staying inside `es_419`'s own vocabulary wherever the two agree** — the catalog code and both non-US storefronts are named for Mexico specifically, and Apple's own `es_419` chrome (which every one of these devices already sees, in Settings, Screen Time and every system alert) is itself neutral enough that writing app copy inside its vocabulary serves the wider Latin American and US audience without requiring a second variant. Meanwhile: write within `es_419`'s own harvested vocabulary by default, and record any deliberately Mexico-specific word choice (one an Argentine or Colombian reader might find unfamiliar) in the batch report rather than guessing at a neutral synonym with no evidence behind it.
