## Audience and register

- A friendly coach in everyday French: short sentences, one idea per line, concrete verbs, no jargon and no English sentence shapes carried over word for word. **Address is `vous` throughout**, never `tu` (Address and gender).
- Drop the source's scaffolding: 15 `en` keys carry "Please" (14 capitalised, plus the case-insensitive hit `quotes.user.saveError`), and Apple's own French drops it too — the harvested notification-permission body reads as a plain statement, not a request: `component.errorView.message` "We couldn't load the data.\nTap "Try Again" to retry." becomes one flat sentence plus "Réessayez." or "Appuyez sur « Réessayer ».", never "Veuillez réessayer."
- French inflects for number and often for gender where English does not; a possessive, an agreement or a a plural marker that is silent or invariant in English is not silent in French, so a line-for-line word substitution is the single most common tell of translated French. ## Address and gender

- **`vous`/`votre` throughout, never `tu`.** Apple's French iOS uses `vous` in every register-bearing string harvested: "Autorisez-vous « %@ » à vous envoyer des notifications ?" (`UserNotificationsServer.framework | Localizable.strings | USER_NOTIFICATION_PERMISSION_ALERT_TITLE`), "Si l’app « %@ » est autorisée à accéder à Temps d’écran, celle-ci pourra consulter les données liées à votre activité…" (`FamilyControlsAuthenticationUI.app | Localizable.strings | NOTIFICATION_AUTH_REQUEST_MESSAGE_INDIVIDUAL`), "Activez les catégories de données de santé plus tard dans l’app Santé." (`HealthUI.framework | HealthUI-Localizable.strings | AUTHORIZATION_DONT_ALLOW_ALERT_MESSAGE`). No `tu` form appears anywhere in the harvest. Apple's own French support pages address the reader the same way: "il existe un certain nombre de réglages … dont **vous** pouvez vous servir", "peut **vous** aider à gérer", "**vous** devez configurer le partage familial sur **votre** appareil" (https://support.apple.com/fr-fr/105121, fetched 2026-09-16). The mock alerts therefore need no register switch: iOS French and app-owned French both say `vous`.
- The app speaks about itself in the first person plural, as `en` does in 19 units: "We couldn't load the data." -> "Nous n’avons pas pu charger les données." (`component.errorView.message`), "We covered %@ with a streak freeze." -> "Nous avons couvert %@ avec un gel de série." (`streakFreeze.usedBody`), "we'll use %@" -> "nous utiliserons %@" (`onboarding.hourlyWage.footer`), "We'll try again soon." -> "Nous réessaierons bientôt." (`widgets.focusRing.a11y.unavailable`). Keep `nous`; there is no register clash with `vous`.
- **Gender.** Unlike Dutch, French predicate adjectives and past participles agreeing with the subject *do* inflect, and `vous` still agrees with the addressee's real number and gender ("Vous êtes prêt." for a man, "prête" for a woman), so the 44 reader-predicate `en` units (`you_are` 5, `are_you` 5, `you_showed_up` 1, `welcome_back` 1, `ready` 7, `focused` 19, `done` 15) are a real hotspot, not a non-issue. Strategy, in this order: (1) restate the sentence as a verb clause with no adjective — "You are making time for what matters." -> "Vous prenez le temps de ce qui compte." (verb "prenez", no agreement); (2) drop the addressed subject and state the fact about the calendar instead — "You showed up %lld days in a row." -> "%lld jours de suite. C’est ça, l’habitude." (mirrors the idiom rule below: state the fact plainly rather than forcing a gendered predicate); (3) use an invariant phrase — "Welcome back." -> "De retour." (invariant, no adjective); never ship a slashed or parenthesised form ("prêt(e)").
- The one hotspot that cannot be dodged by rephrasing the sentence is **role nouns** in the badge family, because a badge title is a two-word label, not a full clause: `achievements.badge.hired.name` "Hired", `.salaried.name` "Salaried", `.rehired.name` "Rehired", `.clockedIn.name` "Clocked in", `.backOnShift.name` "Back on shift", `.permanent.name` "Permanent", `.tenure.name` "Tenure", plus the category names `achievements.category.payroll.name` "Payroll" and `.shift.name` "The Shift". Strategy, in this order: (1) prefer a process or status noun over an agent-descriptive participle — "Pointage" (Clocked in), "Salariat" (Salaried), "Ancienneté" (Tenure), "Retour" (Back on shift) — because these nouns describe the workplace concept, not the reader, so no gender choice about the reader is ever made; (2) where a participle is unavoidable, it agrees with the implicit noun "le badge" (masculine), the same fragment-gender rule Header and label forms states, never with the reader's own gender; (3) never a slashed or doubled form ("embauché/e"). The conceit itself is an owner call (Open decisions `badge_conceit`).
- `user.gender.*` is a data field, not voice: `user.gender.female` "Female", `.male` "Male", `.other` "Other", `.preferNotToSay` "Prefer not to say" — seed 2026-09-16: "Femme", "Homme", "Autre", "Préfère ne pas le dire"; "Autre" is invariant in French gender and needs no shipped-masculine exception, and "Préfère ne pas le dire" is a verb clause, not an adjective, so it needs none either. The stored profile gender (`Manifesting/Features/User/Domain/Entities/Gender.swift`) is not a copy input without a product decision.
- Grep aid, not a validator: `\btu\b`, `\bton\b`, `\bta\b`, `\btes\b`, `\bt'es\b`, and the paired feminine markers `é/e\b|\(e\)|é\.e\b`. Bare `e` is not a stem: it collides with far too many ordinary words.

## Control forms

**Local convention: the bare infinitive.** Apple's French iOS writes every button and menu item as a plain infinitive — "Autoriser", "Refuser", "Continuer", "Annuler", "Enregistrer", "Modifier", "Supprimer", "Fermer", "Réessayer" (the `generic_controls.*` rows in Platform terms) — never a `vous`-conjugated imperative ("Autorisez") and never a verbal noun. That holds even though `vous` is the address elsewhere: French system UI keeps the infinitive convention for controls the way English keeps the bare verb ("Cancel", not "You cancel"). ## Header and label forms

- **Sentence case everywhere in app-owned copy.** Apple's French is sentence case throughout the harvest ("Ajouter un widget", "Modifier les pages", "Gérer les abonnements"), and French orthography capitalises only the first word plus proper names. The 114 Title Case `en` keys become sentence case: "Set Your Hourly Wage" -> "Définissez votre taux horaire" (`onboarding.hourlyWage.title`), "Edit Focus Task" -> "Modifier la tâche de concentration" (`focusTask.createTask.screenTitleEdit`), "My Tasks" -> "Mes tâches" (`focusTask.picker.myTasksHeader`). Proper names keep their capitals: Moneyfesting, Temps d’écran, Face ID, App Store, Photos, Santé, Messages, Écran d’accueil.
- **Uppercase eyebrows and captions stay uppercase where `en` is** (34 all-caps `en` strings: `insights.mood.mood` "MOOD", `insights.summary.dayStreakTitle` "DAY STREAK", `widgets.focusRing.title` "TODAY'S FOCUS", `plan.dailyPlan.{planned,done,anytime}Header`, `%@ / HOUR`, `/%lld MIN`). French has no ß, no dotted/dotless `i` pair, and no word-initial digraph that doubles its capitals the way Dutch `ij` does, so uppercasing is unremarkable except for accents: French orthography keeps accents on uppercase letters in careful typesetting ("ÉTÉ", not "ETE"), and `.textCase(.uppercase)` on Foundation preserves them, so a hand-typed eyebrow must not drop its accents when capitalised ("DÉFINI", never "DEFINI").
- Uppercase captions expand hardest here: of Apple's own all-caps French strings of 1-10 English characters, 42.2% exceed 1.35× and 13.1% exceed 2× (length-stats `all_caps`, bucket `1-10`). Prefer the shorter everyday noun over the exact one and file the row when none exists (`references/surface-budgets.md` `## Catalog microcopy templates`).
- Section headers are plural nouns or participles agreeing with the listed items; the three all-caps plan headers take participles agreeing with "tâches" (feminine plural): "PLANIFIÉES", "TERMINÉES", "À TOUT MOMENT" (seed 2026-09-16, the call site appends the count).
- Tab titles are bare nouns: Moneyfesting, Plan, Statistiques, Profil (`main.tab.*`; seed 2026-09-16 — "Aperçus" is closer to the literal for "Insights" but the tab shows charts and totals, so the plainer "Statistiques" wins, matching the widget gallery's own use of "Statistics" -> `insightsLabel`; four labels share about 70pt each, `references/surface-budgets.md`).
- Toggles and state labels take a predicative participle, and unlike Dutch this **does** inflect in French for the gender and number of the noun it silently stands for: "Gelée", "Terminée", "Bloquée" (`Blocked` in a fragment with no visible noun) — see the agreement rule below for how to decide which form.
- **The French agreement rule for a fragment with no visible noun is grammatical gender and number, not a fixed default.** Any adjective or participle in the fragment depends on whether the implied noun is masculine or feminine, so the packet's `agreement_target` must name it: `la série`, `la tâche`, `la séance`, `la liste`, `la journée`, `la valeur` against `l’objectif`, `le taux horaire`, `l’écran d’accueil`, and — confirmed from Apple's own French, not assumed — `le widget` ("Si vous supprimez **ce** widget…", `SpringBoardHome.framework | SpringBoardHome.strings | REMOVE_WIDGET_BODY_REMOVE_DATA`, masculine demonstrative "ce"). Example of the fault the editor checks: `widgets.streak.copy.frozen.small` "Covered by a freeze." beside `.medium` "Yesterday was covered." — the small tile's fragment has no noun, so the packet must say whether the implied subject is "la journée" (fem., -> "Couverte par un gel.") or something else; shipping the wrong gender is the single most visible French bug a blind editor can catch and a coordinator cannot without the `agreement_target`.

## Numbers, dates, currency and punctuation

All values below are Foundation/ICU output for `fr_FR` (locale-formats, ICU 76.1.0.0 / CLDR 46.0.0.0), or the app's own `AppCurrency` path on that locale. Space characters below are spelled as code points because the difference is load-bearing: U+202F is the **narrow** no-break space, U+00A0 the **regular** no-break space, and the two are not interchangeable in this locale's own Foundation output.

- **Numbers.** Grouping separator is the **narrow** no-break space U+202F, decimal separator `,` (U+002C): `2000` -> "2 000" (narrow gap), `12000` -> "12 000", `1250.5` -> "1 250,5", `1250000` -> "1 250 000". Four-digit numbers **are** grouped, so a literal in copy follows suit: `achievements.badge.wageYear.*` "2,000 hours of focus" -> "2 000 heures de concentration".
- **Money.** Runtime values arrive formatted through `AppLanguage.effectiveLocale`, so no string hard-codes a currency (`en` has no "EUR" or "euro" literal; the placeholder carries the amount, `references/project-context.md` `## Currency display`). `AppCurrency.format` gives "120€" and "1 250,5€" — narrow no-break space (U+202F) for the thousands grouping, regular no-break space (U+00A0) between the number and "€" — against the locale's own standard form "120,00€" / "1 250,50€" (same two spaces, plus the cents). Compact: the app prints "1,25k€" / "1,25m€" / "1,25b€" where the locale would print "1,25 k€" / "1,25 M€" / "1,25 Md€" (Identity, engineering flags: thousands agree in spelling, millions differ only in case, billions do not match at all). Never write a currency name into copy.
- **Percent** takes a regular no-break space (U+00A0) before the sign: "40 %", "100 %". "%lld%%" and "-%lld%%" need that space inserted, unlike `en`'s glued "40%".
- **Time** is 24-hour (`Locale.hourCycle` h23; `DateFormatter` template `j` renders as `HH 'h'`, i.e. the literal word "h" is part of the pattern): "09:41", "23:59"; the shortened form drops the leading zero ("9:41"). The twelve `en` clock literals (`12AM`, `06PM` and kin on `insights.appUsage.{axisMidnight,axisNoon,axisSixAm,axisSixPm}`, `profile.appUsage.axis.*`, `screenTimeReport.{axisMidnight,axisNoon,axisSixAM,axisSixPM}`) become "00:00", "06:00", "12:00", "18:00" — which is also why "minuit" should never appear in French chart copy (Money frame, widgets bans).
- **Dates**: "31 déc. 2026", "31 décembre 2026", "12 mai", "mardi 12 mai". Month and weekday names are lower case in French, mid-sentence and at the start of a label alike.
- **Ranges**: `en` uses an en dash without spaces in two of three cases ("1–280 tekens"-style; `onboarding.personalGoal.validation` "1–280 characters", `widgets.startFocus.description` "5–120 minutes") and a plain hyphen in the third (`onboarding.deviceUsage.oneToThreeHours` "1-3 hours"). No `fr`-specific evidence on numeric ranges was harvested this run; propose normalising all three to the en dash without spaces, matching the majority source convention, and flag it for confirmation rather than treating it as settled.
- **Dashes and marks**: the two em dashes (`achievements.streak.comebackBody`, `share.alert.savedToPhotosMessage`) stay em dashes with spaces; the quote attribution `quote.display.attribution` keeps its en dash "– %@"; the five spaced-hyphen author decorations keep the hyphen the source uses; the interpunct "·" is preserved in the eight middle-dot keys; ellipsis follows the source's own split (2 keys use "…", 9 use "...") until an owner call.
- **Quotation marks.** Foundation reports **« »** (U+00AB/U+00BB) as both the primary and the alternate delimiter for `fr_FR` — an unusual case where French nested quoting collapses to one mark, unlike the double/single split most locales get — and Apple's own French UI confirms the mark and its spacing directly: the mock alert wraps the app name as "« %@ »" with a **regular** no-break space (U+00A0) inside the guillemets on both sides (`FamilyControlsAuthenticationUI.app | Localizable.strings | NOTIFICATION_AUTH_REQUEST_TITLE`), not the narrow space the numbers use. Use `« … »` for a UI label quoted inside a sentence and for the quote-card wrapper alike, pending Open decisions `quote_marks` on whether the app should actually insert that no-break space or accept a plain space as an approximation.
- **Plurals: `one` and `other` below one million, plus an optional `many` at exact millions** (ICU 76.1.0.0/CLDR 46.0.0.0; Xcode 26.1's own plural table omits `many` for `fr`, but a hand-added `many` variant compiles and is selected at runtime). `one` selects on the integers 0 and 1 alone (0 is `one` in French, a real surprise next to most locales); `other` selects on every integer from 2 up to 999,999 and on every tested fraction (0.5 and 1.5 are `one`, 2.5 is `other`); `many` is reached only at 1,000,000, 2,000,000, 10,000,000 and 100,000,000 exactly — 1,000,001 and 1,500,000 both fall back to `other`. Boundary integers to exercise: **0, 1, 2, 11, 20, 21, 100, 101, 120, 1 000 000**, plus the fractions 0,5 and 1,5. The surprises worth a test row: 0 is `one`, not `other`; and if a plural key is ever given an explicit `zero` variant, Foundation picks it for the literal 0 and overrides the CLDR `one` category (`_meta.runtime_zero_note`) — so a key with no `zero` variant reads "0 jour", not "0 jours".
- **How numerals attach to nouns.** The numeral stands before the plural noun with an ordinary space and no linking word: "1 jour", "3 jours", "%lld minutes". There is no Romanian-style partitive and no article inserted, so a single `%lld` never forces a different noun form beyond the ordinary singular/plural mark.
- Six keys are a single `en` `stringUnit` but need French `one`/`other` branches, because the count is inside the sentence: `achievements.streak.body`, `.comebackBody`, `.headline`, `.milestoneBody`, `.nextMilestoneBody`, `.weekAccessibility`.

## Length trigger

- **1.40×.** A French string longer than 1.40 times its English length goes on the render list; `scripts/catalog_check.py` C5 puts it there, and it is never a finding by itself. The ratio is measured from Apple's own French translations: across 45 417 unique English/French pairs in the iOS 26.1 runtime, 32.6% exceed 1.40× at the rounded value (31.6% at the unrounded 1.415×), which is the point that matches the Romanian calibration (Apple's `ro` exceeds 1.35× in 31.6% of pairs, the statistic closest to the `ro` catalog's 299 of 1135); a flat 1.35× would flag 39.0% of French pairs, more than the calibration intends (length-stats `recommendation`).
- Where it bites: short strings expand most, but less punishingly than in `nl` or `ro`. Median ratio 1.261 overall, 1.286 for English strings of 1-10 characters with 44.6% over 1.35×, against 1.237 and 28.4% for strings of 41-120 characters. All-caps strings of 1-10 characters are the worst case: 42.2% over 1.35× and 13.1% over 2× — exactly the eyebrows and widget captions (`insights.mood.mood` "MOOD", `widgets.focusRing.row.money` "TIME VALUE, 30 DAYS", `insights.summary.lastThirtyDaysSubtitle` "LAST 30 DAYS").
- No catalog share can be quoted for `fr`: there are 0 French units at the snapshot. Re-state this line with a real share after the first wave.
- Counts never establish fit. Verdicts come from render evidence against each view's own floor and line limit (`references/surface-budgets.md`, `references/render-evidence.md`). Shortening cuts words, never facts; accessibility strings are exempt.

## Emoji and intensity

- Preserve every source emoji and add none: 4 `en` keys carry one (`onboarding.notificationsSetup.motivationOn` "On 💪🏼", `.motivationOff` "Off 😞", `screenTime.createRestrictedList.selectionInstructions` "⚠️ You need to select…", and the `🔥` key literal, which is not user-facing copy).
- Exclamation marks only where the source has one: 3 `en` keys (`plan.dailyPlan.futureEmptyMessage`, `plan.dailyPlan.timelinePotentialSuffix`, `streakFreeze.info.body`). These are source lines to flag, not a licence to add more (`references/voice-card.md`). Some French marketing copy reaches for the exclamation more readily than this product does; a brief may soften intensity, never raise it.
- French inserts a no-break space before "!" and "?" in careful typesetting, evidenced directly in Apple's own mock alert title "Autorisez-vous « %@ » à vous envoyer des notifications ?" (a regular no-break space, U+00A0, before the question mark). Any exclamation this app does ship should carry the same no-break space before it, on the rare source lines that have one.
- Tests that ban intensity: `ManifestingTests/Onboarding/OnboardingWidgetsStepTests.swift:203-204` bans "!" on the widgets step for `en` and `ro`; the French word list and the paywall hero's are under `## Money frame`, as proposals for extending those tests.

## Decided conflicts

| Conflict | Winner | Rule |
|---|---|---|
| Address: `tu` versus `vous` | `vous` throughout, mocks included | Apple's French iOS uses `vous` in every register-bearing string harvested and has no `tu` form; Apple's French support pages do the same. Precedence line: Apple's French wording for anything mirroring iOS |
| Button form: infinitive versus `vous`-conjugated imperative | bare infinitive ("Autoriser", "Enregistrer", "Modifier") | Apple's runtime uses the infinitive for every button and menu item with no exception found in this harvest; the sole break is the discount badge's `vous`-imperative "Économisez", a deliberate exception because it addresses the reader's own saving, not a system action |
| Case: Title Case versus sentence case on app-owned copy | sentence case | Apple's French is sentence case throughout; Title Case is an English convention, and 114 `en` keys carrying it do not carry it into French |
| Home Screen edit-menu label: a word versus the glyph `+` | Apple's own value wins: `+` | `SpringBoardHome.framework | SpringBoardHome.strings | EDIT` ships the literal glyph "+" in `fr.lproj`; the illustrated mirror label must match the device exactly, not a French translation of "Edit" |
| Minute unit: bare "m" versus "min" versus glued "%lldmin" | "min" with a no-break space for running text and labels; "%lldmin" glued with no space for tight widget and report lines | Foundation's French abbreviated minute unit is "min" and its own narrow form already glues the unit with no space at all ("25min"), so the tight lines follow the locale's own convention rather than a width compromise |
| Guillemet spacing: narrow versus regular no-break space | regular no-break space (U+00A0) inside guillemets, narrow no-break space (U+202F) reserved for number grouping | Apple's own French mock-alert title uses U+00A0 inside the guillemets around the app name, while Foundation's own thousands grouping for `fr_FR` uses U+202F; the two spaces are not interchangeable and this harvest settles which goes where |
| `Add Widget`: one key or two | two keys | iOS 26.1 French labels the edit-menu item ("Ajouter un widget") and the gallery button (" Ajouter le widget") differently, confirmed independently by Apple's own support page, so the `nl` shortcut of one shared key does not hold here (Open decisions `add_widget_gallery_label`) |
| Compact money suffix `k`/`m`/`b` against the locale's `k`/`M`/`Md` | engineering finding, not a translation choice | `AppCurrency.formatCompact` hardcodes the Latin suffix; thousands happen to agree, millions differ only in case, billions do not match at all (`compact_finding`) |

## Open decisions

Each item is mirrored in `glossary/fr.json` `open` with the fields `scripts/term_audit.py` reads. Nothing here is translated yet, so "meanwhile" means what the first batches do, not which shipped keys stay locked.

- **`streak_term` — the noun for a streak.** Affects every noun slot: `widgets.streak.smallHeader` "Streak", `.currentStreakHeader` "Current streak", `.displayName` "Focus Streak", `.daysCount`, `.dayStreakCount`, `insights.summary.dayStreakTitle` "DAY STREAK", `share.card.streakEyebrow`, `achievements.streak.title`, `onboarding.welcome.slide.streak.{eyebrow,headline}`, `paywall.benefit.streak.title`, the whole `streakFreeze.*` family. Rule: everyday French a reader without English understands at first read, and it must fit a one-word widget header and an uppercase caption (Length trigger). Options: **"série"** — the plain French noun, 6 characters, fills every header and takes a compound ("série de focus", "gel de série"); no Apple citation was harvested for this exact sense this run, so this option carries no device evidence, only that it is the ordinary word a French speaker would reach for. **"jours de suite" / "jours d’affilée"** — the everyday French phrase for a run of consecutive days, but a phrase, not a noun: it cannot fill "Streak" as a bare header and is too long for the uppercase caption at this budget. **"streak"** — the English loan some French apps keep; ruled out by the same no-English-needed rule the Audience and register section states. Recommended: **"série" in noun slots, with "jours de suite" as the phrase in running text**, for the same reason `nl` recommends its own plain noun over the Apple phrase: no device evidence favours a noun here, and "série" is the shortest, plainest option that survives the widget budget. Meanwhile: the first batch writes "série" in headers and "jours de suite" in sentences and lists every key that touched the term, so a flip is one recorded global change rather than a per-string choice.
- **`quote_marks` — whether the app should actually insert the no-break space inside guillemets, and what a nested quotation takes.** Keys: `moneyfesting.details.quoteWrapper` "%@", `notifications.contentExtension.quoteDecoration` "%@", `moneyfesting.main.quotePlaceholder`, `theme.createTheme.sampleQuote`, `widgets.quotes.placeholderText`, plus the UI labels quoted inside `component.errorView.message`, `onboarding.dataMigration.errorMessage`, `theme.picker.errorMessage`, `onboarding.alert.screenTimeMessage`, `focusTask.picker.emptyMessage` (11 `en` keys use a straight double quote). Rule: the locale's own delimiters, and a mock or quoted iOS label follows the device. Options: **« … » with the no-break space typed into the string** — matches Apple's own harvested value exactly, at the cost of every occurrence carrying a literal U+00A0 that a careless edit could turn back into a plain space; **« … » with a plain space, no U+00A0** — simpler to type and edit, an approximation of Apple's own value rather than a copy of it. Both options keep the guillemet itself, since Foundation reports it as both the primary and the alternate `fr_FR` delimiter, so there is no first/second-level split to decide, unlike most locales. Recommended: **the no-break space, typed literally**, because it is what Apple's own French ships and the app already inserts no-break spaces elsewhere in formatted numbers, so the mechanism to do it correctly already exists in the codebase. Meanwhile: the first batch uses a plain space as a placeholder and flags every key above so the no-break space can be inserted in one pass once this closes.
- **`badge_conceit` — whether the workplace metaphor travels into French, and in which vocabulary.** Keys: the 24 `achievements.badge.*.name` values and the 5 `achievements.category.*.name` values, with their `.earned` lines. Rule: one conceit per locale, decided once and kept across the family, gender-neutral, with plain descriptors left plain (`references/project-context.md` `## Content policy`). Options: **keep it, with process nouns** — "Pointage", "Embauche", "Salariat", "Ancienneté", "De retour", "Double quart", "La paie", "Le quart" avoid agent participles entirely, so no reader-gender choice is ever made, at the cost of a noun-heavy register that reads slightly more bureaucratic than the source's punchy participles; **drop it** — replace the employment names with plain achievement names ("Première séance", "Sept jours de suite", "Cinquante journées pleines"), which loses the wink but removes any reading of the app as an employer next to a money frame that already says five times that nothing is earned. Recommended: **keep it, with process nouns**, because the conceit is what makes the badge family cohere in `en`, and the process-noun approach sidesteps French's heavier gender-agreement load better than a participle-based translation would. Meanwhile: the first achievements batch drafts the conceit, marks every name a proposal, and the adjudicator reports the whole family together so a flip is one batch.
- **`add_widget_gallery_label` — one shared key or two, for the Add Widget mirror.** Keys: `onboarding.widgets.howTo.addWidgetLabel`, drawn on the edit-menu row and on the gallery button (`HomeScreenJiggleIllustrationView.swift` lines 167 and 274), and quoted in `step2` and `step5`. Rule: illustrated labels and the steps that quote them mirror the device verbatim (Platform terms, Decided conflicts). Options: **add a second key** — `step2` and the edit-menu illustration keep "Ajouter un widget", a new key carries "Ajouter le widget" for the gallery button and `step5` (a code change; `en` stays "Add Widget" on both, so `OnboardingWidgetsStepTests.swift:178` still holds); **keep one shared key** — pick one of the two French forms and accept that one of the two illustrated positions will not match the device exactly. Recommended: **add a second key**, because iOS 26.1 genuinely ships two different French strings for the two positions (confirmed independently by Apple's own support page, not only the simulator harvest), so a single key cannot be correct in both places at once. Meanwhile: the first widgets-how-to batch drafts both French forms against the existing single key and flags the split in the report; no key is added without the owner.
