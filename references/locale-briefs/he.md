There is no `he` copy in the app: `Manifesting/Foundation/Localization/Localizable.xcstrings` holds 1189 keys with `en` 1142 and `ro` 1135 units and no `he` localization at all, so every count below describes `en` (snapshot 2026-09-16 of the working tree; catalog commit `c8a49dd`, 1187 keys). ## Identity

- Tag `he`. Ship `he`, never `iw`: `xcstringstool` writes `he.lproj` and Xcode's `LOCLocaleCode.plist` maps `he` to displayName "Hebrew" with lproj `he` and has no entry for `iw`; a catalog entry `iw` compiles to `he` anyway, and a catalog holding both produced a `he.lproj` whose value flipped between runs (6 of 8 with `he,iw` order, 5 of 8 with `iw,he`). iOS 26.1 ships `he.lproj` in 24 of the 26 UI bundles checked and `iw.lproj` in none; a device set to `he-IL` resolves to `he` in all six probed system bundles and, with `he` in the catalog, to `he` in the app (`apple-coverage.json`, 2026-09-16).
- Audience: Hebrew speakers in Israel on an iPhone in Hebrew; Hebrew script, right to left, no letter case. Format locale `he_IL`, audience currency ILS (`CurrencyCatalog.options`, `Manifesting/Foundation/Localization/CurrencyOption.swift:66`).
- Snapshot 2026-09-16 against catalog commit `c8a49dd`: 0 `he` units, 0 `he` plural keys, 0 `needs_review`. The `en` side the first batch translates is 1189 keys, 29 of them `en` plural keys and 6 more that `ro` splits on a count while `en` keeps one form.
- Precedence: the app `CLAUDE.md` first (key and lookup conventions, formatting through `AppLanguage.effectiveLocale`), then Apple's own Hebrew wording for anything that mirrors iOS (Platform terms below), then this brief. `glossary/he.json` records the outcomes; there is no catalog majority and no archived Hebrew guide to rank, which is why Apple sits where the catalog sits for `ro`.
- Budgets: `references/surface-budgets.md`. There is no `references/examples-he.md`; the first batch returns six rows to seed it (`references/agent-protocol.md` `## A. Forward translator`).

## Audience and register

- A friendly, plain-spoken app that sounds like the device: everyday Israeli Hebrew, short sentences, no jargon, no English sentence shapes. The copy sits next to money and a timer, so it stays dry rather than excited.
- Register calibration, all iOS 26.1 (23B86) runtime: buttons are verbal nouns or infinitives, never imperatives (`אישור`, `סירוב`, `המשך`, `ביטול`, `עריכה`, `לא לאפשר`); instructions are impersonal (`יש ללחוץ לחיצה ארוכה על הווידג׳טים לעריכה.`, `SpringBoardHome.framework | SpringBoardHome.strings | WIDGET_DISCOVERABILITY_DESCRIPTION`); explanations use `ניתן ל…` and `באפשרותך ל…` rather than a conjugated second person (`HealthUI.framework | HealthUI-Localizable.strings | AUTHORIZATION_DONT_ALLOW_ALERT_MESSAGE`; `ScreenTimeSettingsUI.framework | Localizable.strings | WeeklyReportNotificationGroupSpecifierFooterText`).
- Drop English scaffolding: 15 `en` keys carry "please" (14 capitalized), and Hebrew UI carries no equivalent word; write the action, not the request.
- Imported shapes the editor flags. ## Address and gender

- Address: second person singular, informal, with no T/V distinction to make. Hebrew's problem is not formality but gender: the verb, the adjective and the participle all mark it. Apple's he interface solves it by not conjugating — `ניתן להפעיל`, `באפשרותך לקבל`, `יש ללחוץ` — and by leaning on forms that are written the same for both genders: the unpointed pronoun and possessive (`לך`, `שלך`, `עבורך`) and the second-person past (`השתמשת`, `הרמת`). Evidence: `UserNotificationsServer.framework | Localizable.strings | USER_NOTIFICATION_PERMISSION_ALERT_TITLE`; `FamilyControlsAuthenticationUI.app | Localizable.strings | NOTIFICATION_AUTH_REQUEST_MESSAGE_INDIVIDUAL`; `HealthUI.framework | HealthUI-Localizable.strings | AUTHORIZATION_DONT_ALLOW_ALERT_MESSAGE`; `ScreenTimeSettingsUI.framework | Localizable.strings | WeeklyReportNotificationGroupSpecifierFooterText` (all iOS 26.1 (23B86)).
- The app's own voice: `en` speaks in the first person plural on 19 keys ("We couldn't load the data.", `component.errorView.message`; "We covered %@ with a streak freeze.", `streakFreeze.usedBody`). Hebrew keeps that as an impersonal or passive statement rather than a literal `אנחנו`, because a Hebrew app that says "we" on an error line reads as a company memo: `לא הצלחנו לטעון את הנתונים.` is acceptable where the sentence needs an actor, `הנתונים לא נטענו.` where it does not.
- Neutral strategies this language offers, in the order to reach for them:
  1. **Verbal nouns** for every control, header and action label (`שמירה`, `מחיקה`, `דילוג`, `הוספת משימה`). Carries no gender and matches iOS.
  2. **Infinitive** for an instruction or a headline that must feel like a step (`לסגור את הטבעת.`). No person, no gender.
  3. **Impersonal frames** for explanations and permissions: `ניתן ל…`, `יש ל…`, `אפשר ל…`, `באפשרותך ל…`.
  4. **Unpointed second-person past** for achievements, which is the one conjugated form spelled identically for both genders (`התמדת`, `חזרת`, `הרווחת`, `הגעת`). This is what makes a badge line possible at all.
  5. **Nominal sentences** with no verb for celebration copy (`שבעה ימים ברצף.`, `יום מלא.`), and **agreement with the object** rather than the reader (`‏%lld משימות הושלמו`).
  6. **Avoid**: present-tense participles about the reader (`מרוויח`, `מתרכזת`), predicate adjectives (`אתה מוכן`), reader-directed imperatives (`התחל`, `בחרי`), and role nouns naming the reader.
- Hotspot `en` keys where a gender choice becomes unavoidable, from `en-inventory.md` `## Address and gender`: `achievements.streak.body` ("You showed up %lld days in a row."), `achievements.streak.comebackBody`, `achievements.streak.weekAccessibility`, `achievements.badge.hired.earned`, `achievements.badge.rehired.earned`, `achievements.badge.backOnShift.earned`, `achievements.badge.fullWeek.earned` ("You can earn this one again."), `onboarding.age.title` ("What age are you?"), `onboarding.personalGoal.title` / `.personalizedTitle`, `onboarding.dailyFocusGoal.subtitle` ("how focused you are"), `focusTask.alert.deleteTask.message` ("Are you sure…"), `screenTime.restrictedAppsPicker.deleteAlertMessagePrefix`, `screenTime.createRestrictedList.savedMessage` ("when you're ready"), `profile.quoteCategories.comingSoonFootnote`, `shield.reflection.begin.title` ("Begin before you're ready."). The four badge role nouns are their own problem: `achievements.badge.hired.name` "Hired", `.salaried.name` "Salaried", `.rehired.name` "Rehired", `.permanent.name` "Permanent" have no gender-free Hebrew noun, so they become nominal states (`התקבלת לתפקיד` as a line, `קביעות` as a name) or the workplace conceit is renamed for this locale — a product call, listed under Open decisions.
- Shipped default where no neutral form exists: masculine singular, recorded per key and reported. Apple itself does this on the one string that must choose (`״%@״ מבקש לשלוח לך עדכונים`, masculine `מבקש` with a gender-free `לך`). Double address with a slash or a mid-word dot is banned: it is unreadable at widget sizes, no Apple he string in the harvest uses it, and `references/locale-decisions.md` `## Voice choices that need locale review` already rules out slash forms for every locale.
- `user.gender.*` is a picker value list (`זכר`, `נקבה`, `אחר`, `מעדיף/ה לא לציין` — the last one is itself an instance of this decision), and the stored profile gender (`Manifesting/Features/User/Domain/Entities/Gender.swift`) is not a copy input without a product decision.
- Grep aid, not a validator, for gendered forms slipping in: `אתה | את | שלך הוא | מוכן | מוכנה | בחר | בחרי | התחל | הזן | מרוויח | מתרכז`. The two-letter `את` also spells the accusative particle, so every hit is read by a human.

## Control forms

Counts are `en` catalog keys whose value is exactly the control word, the same match `scripts/term_audit.py` makes; there is no `he` value to count. Preferred and role forms mirror `glossary/he.json` `controls` exactly (role names as the glossary spells them), because the script reads the glossary, not this table.

**Local convention, with evidence:** iOS Hebrew buttons are verbal nouns (`שם פעולה`), not imperatives and not infinitives. iOS 26.1 (23B86) runtime: `UIKitCore.framework | Localizable.strings` gives `Cancel` = `ביטול`, `Save` = `שמירה`, `Edit` = `עריכה`, `Delete` = `מחיקה`, `Close` = `סגירה`, `Done` = `סיום`, `OK` = `אישור`, `Not Now` = `לא כעת`; `SpringBoardHome.framework | SpringBoardHome.strings` gives `EDIT` = `עריכה`, `DONE` = `סיום`, `EDIT_MENU_ADD_WIDGET` = `הוספת וידג׳ט`; `SpringBoard.app | SpringBoard.strings | ALARM_STOP` = `עצירה`; `ControlCenter/Bundles/TimerModule.bundle | TimerModule.strings | PAUSE` = `השהיה`, `START` = `התחלה`, `RESUME` = `המשך`. ## Header and label forms

- **Hebrew has no letter case.** A `.textCase(.uppercase)` site changes nothing in Hebrew text; the only characters that actually uppercase are Latin ones a placeholder brings in, such as the ISO currency code in `onboarding.hourlyWage.unit` / `profile.hourlyWage.unit` (`%@ / HOUR` -> `‏%@ / שעה`, where `%@` resolves to `ILS`). Apple confirms the same on its own side: the key `APPS` renders `יישומים` exactly as the key `Apps` does (`BridgeManifests.bundle | SettingsSearchManifest-com.apple.internal.CompanionReturnToClockSettings.strings | APPS` against `…NTKCTritiumSettings.strings | APPS`). Consequences: the 31 all-caps `en` strings lose their visual shout, so the distinction must come from position and brevity, not from the string; and the questions that matter for other locales — accented capitals, dotted and dotless i, ß — do not arise here at all.
- Eyebrows and stat captions (15 keys, among them `insights.summary.dayStreakTitle` "DAY STREAK", `insights.mood.mood` "MOOD", `widgets.focusRing.title` "TODAY'S FOCUS", `widgets.focusRing.row.money` "TIME VALUE, 30 DAYS"): choose the shortest natural noun phrase, because these lines cannot wrap and cannot shrink (`references/surface-budgets.md` `## Catalog microcopy templates`). `ימים ברצף`, `מצב רוח`, `ריכוז היום`, `ערך הזמן, 30 ימים` are agent-authored illustrations of the right length band.
- Screen titles and nav titles: a noun phrase or a verbal noun, never a sentence with a conjugated verb (`הגדרת התעריף השעתי` for `onboarding.hourlyWage.title` "Set Your Hourly Wage"). The 114 Title Case `en` strings carry no case information into Hebrew; they are read for their grammar, not their capitals.
- Section headers agree in gender and number with the items they head: `plan.dailyPlan.plannedHeader` / `.doneHeader` / `.anytimeHeader` head tasks (`משימות`, f. pl.), so `מתוכננות`, `הושלמו`, `בכל זמן` (not `בכל שעה`, which reads as "every hour" — a frequency, not "whenever, no fixed time" — Apple's own he for "Any Time" is `בכל זמן`, `System/Library/PrivateFrameworks/WorkflowUI.framework | Localizable.strings | Any Time`, iOS 26.1 (23B86)); `achievements.gallery.recentSection` heads badges (`עיטורים`, m. pl.), so `הושגו לאחרונה`.
- Tab titles are nouns: Moneyfesting, תוכנית, תובנות, פרופיל (`main.tab.*`). Apple's he for Insights is `תובנות` (`AccessibilitySharedUISupport.framework | AccessibilitySharedUISupport.strings | Insights`) and for Statistics `סטטיסטיקה` (same table, `Statistics`); `תובנות` is the one that means what the tab shows.
- Toggles and state labels take a passive participle agreeing with the labelled noun: `onboarding.welcome.preview.blocking.protected` "Selected apps are blocked" -> `היישומים שנבחרו חסומים` (m. pl. with `יישומים`, f. pl. `חסומות` if the app noun becomes `אפליקציות`, Open decisions `app_noun`); `screenTime.restrictedApps.appsRestricted` `%lld apps restricted` agrees the same way in all three plural categories.
- A fragment with no visible noun agrees with the noun the screen implies, and the packet's `agreement_target` must name it in Hebrew with gender and number. The recurring targets: `משימה` f. sg. / `משימות` f. pl., `רצף` m. sg., `דקה` f. sg. / `דקות` f. pl., `שעה` f. sg. / `שעות` f. pl., `יום` m. sg. / `ימים` m. pl., `הקפאה` f. sg. / `הקפאות` f. pl., `יישום` m. sg. (or `אפליקציה` f. sg.), `ערכת נושא` f. sg. The pair to watch is `widgets.streak.copy.frozen.small` "Covered by a freeze." beside `widgets.streak.copy.frozen.medium`: the small line has no noun on screen and must still agree with `יום` (m. sg.), so `היום כוסה בהקפאה`, not a feminine participle borrowed from `הקפאה`.
- Status pills that describe the action rather than the reader default to masculine singular (`common.editor.saved` "Saved" -> `נשמר`).

## Numbers, dates, currency and punctuation

All values below are Foundation/ICU output for `he_IL` on iOS 26.1 (23B86) unless marked otherwise (`locale-formats.json`, 2026-09-16).

- **Direction is `rtl`.** Runtime values arrive formatted through `AppLanguage.effectiveLocale` (app `CLAUDE.md:42`), so no string hard-codes a number, a currency or a date.
- **Numbers**: grouping `,` (U+002C), decimal `.` (U+002E), four digits grouped: `2,000`, `12,000`, `1,250.5`, `1,250,000`. Digits are Western, written left to right inside the Hebrew run, which is why a number that opens a string needs an explicit RLM.
- **Money**: the app prints `‏120 ‏ILS` and `‏1,250.5 ‏ILS` — U+200F, digits, U+00A0, U+200F, the ISO code — because `AppCurrency.format` reserves `.narrow` for four currencies (Identity, engineering flags). `he_IL`'s own presentation is `‏120.00 ‏₪`. Compact: app `‏1.25k ‏ILS` / `‏1.25m ‏ILS`; locale `₪1.25K‏` / `₪1.25M‏`.
- **Percent** is glued after the number with no space: `40%`, `100%` (U+0025). `-%lld%%` on the badge keeps the same shape.
- **Time** is 24-hour: `Locale.hourCycle` is `h23`, the `j` template resolves to `H`, and `9:41` / `23:59` render unchanged. Watch the override path: with the app language set to `he` on an `en_US` device the identifier becomes `he_US` and the same call prints `11:59 אח׳`, a 12-hour clock with a Hebrew day-period marker, because the in-app override swaps the language and keeps the device region. The 12 clock-literal `en` keys (`insights.appUsage.axis*`, `profile.appUsage.axis.*`, `screenTimeReport.axis*`) are hard-coded strings, so Hebrew writes them 24-hour: `00:00`, `06:00`, `12:00`, `18:00`.
- **Dates**: `31 בדצמ׳ 2026` abbreviated, `31 בדצמבר 2026` wide, `12 במאי` day-and-month, `יום שלישי` for a wide weekday. The `ב` prefix is part of the month name in this pattern and is never dropped.
- **Ranges** take an en dash U+2013 with spaces, and each side of a mixed run carries its own RLM, exactly as Apple writes `‏%@ – ‏%@` (`MobileMailUI.framework | Main.strings | MAILBOX_WITH_ACCOUNT`; `Weather.framework | WeatherFrameworkLocalizableStrings.strings | AQI_INDEX_STRING`). Keys: `onboarding.deviceUsage.oneToThreeHours` (`1-3 hours`), `onboarding.personalGoal.validation` (`1–280 characters`), `widgets.startFocus.description` (`5–120 minutes`).
- **Quotation marks**: Foundation and CLDR give `he_IL` straight `"` and `'` (CLDR 46 summary chart for Hebrew, fetched 2026-09-16), but Apple's shipped Hebrew UI wraps app and feature names in gershayim `״` (U+05F4) — `״הגדרות״`, `ל״זמן מסך״`, `ביישום ״בריאות״`, `״תמונות״` — and the same mark closes a quoted UI label. Use `״…״` (Decided conflicts). Keys: `moneyfesting.details.quoteWrapper` and `notifications.contentExtension.quoteDecoration` become `״%@״`; the 11 straight-double-quote keys and the 7 curly ones take the same mark; `quote.display.attribution` keeps its en dash, `– %@`.
- **Ellipsis**: one character, `…` (U+2026), even where `en` writes three periods — Apple does exactly this, rendering `Loading...` as `טוען…` (`UIKitCore.framework | Localizable.strings | CONTENT_UNAVAILABLE_LOADING_TEXT`). Covers the 9 three-period keys and the 2 that already use the character.
- **Bidi marks to write into the string**, as code points, all from Apple's own Hebrew values: U+200F RIGHT-TO-LEFT MARK before a leading digit, placeholder or Latin word (`‏%d דקות`; `‏Face ID`; `‏App Store`; `‏%@ ללא תשלום`); U+2068 FIRST STRONG ISOLATE and U+2069 POP DIRECTIONAL ISOLATE around an interpolated name that the app does not control, as the notification alert does (`״⁨%@⁩״ מבקש לשלוח לך עדכונים`, `UserNotificationsServer.framework | Localizable.strings | USER_NOTIFICATION_PERMISSION_ALERT_TITLE`); U+00A0 NO-BREAK SPACE inside a Latin product name that must not split (`ב-Face ID`, `AppProtectionUI.framework | Localizable.strings | REQUIRE_AUTH_FACEID`; `‏App Store`, `ScreenTimeSettingsUI.framework | Restrictions.strings | InstallingUIAppsSpecifierName`). Do not scatter these: one mark for one measured rendering problem, and a mirror key copies Apple's marks verbatim (`references/locale-decisions.md` `## RTL`).
- **Plural categories are one / two / other.** ICU 76.1.0.0 (CLDR 46.0.0.0) reports exactly those keywords for `he`, the compiled-catalog runtime check agrees, and the CLDR 46 plural-rules chart (fetched 2026-09-16) gives `one: i = 1 and v = 0 or i = 0 and v != 0`, `two: i = 2 and v = 0`, `other` for the rest. Xcode 26.1 also lists `many`, which no integer, large integer or fraction ever selects (Identity, engineering flags).
  - Boundary integers to exercise on every plural key: **0, 1, 2, 3, 12, 20, 100, 101, 102, 1000**. The surprises: `0` is `other`, `2` is `two` but `12`, `102` and `1002` are `other`, and `0.5` is `one` while `1.5` and `2.5` are `other`.
  - **How numerals attach to nouns.** The `one` form drops the placeholder and writes the noun with a trailing `אחד` / `אחת`, or the bare noun: `דקה אחת`, `שעה אחת`, `קובץ אחד`. The `two` form drops the placeholder too and uses either the counting word `שתי` / `שני` plus the plural noun or the dual: `שתי דקות`, `שני קבצים`, `שעתיים`, `יומיים`. The `other` form keeps the placeholder before the plural noun and opens with U+200F: `‏%d דקות`, `‏%d ימים`, `‏%d שעות`. Evidence: `AXNTKUtilities.framework | Accessibility.stringsdict | lifestyle.active.minutes.unit` and `lifestyle.active.hours.unit`; `SpotlightUIShared.framework | Localizable.stringsdict | %lld Files/value`; `AppSSOKerberos.framework | Localizable.stringsdict | NOTIFICATION_DESC_NORMAL_FORMAT/days` (`יום אחד` / `יומיים` / `‏%2$d ימים`); `NanoTimeKit/FaceBundles/NTKLeghornFaceBundle.bundle | Accessibility.stringsdict | LEGHORN_DAYS_AGO_ACCESSIBILITY/num` (`לפני יום` / `לפני יומיים` / `לפני %ld ימים`).
  - This makes the `one` and `two` forms **shorter than the source**, not longer, and it removes the number from the line. Any key whose layout assumes a digit is always present (`widgets.streak.daysCount`, `share.card.streakSupporting`, `achievements.streak.headline`) needs a render at 1 and 2, and a key whose comment says the number is the point may keep the digit in all three forms (`‏%lld ימים`, `‏2 ימים`) with the reason recorded.
  - The 29 `en` plural keys all need three forms. So do the 6 keys where `en` ships a single string and `ro` split on the count (`achievements.streak.body`, `.comebackBody`, `.headline`, `.milestoneBody`, `.nextMilestoneBody`, `.weekAccessibility`): Hebrew needs the split for the same reason, and `achievements.streak.comebackBody`'s `%lld-day run` becomes a counted noun phrase rather than a compound.

## Length trigger

- A `he` string longer than **1.00×** its `en` length goes on the render list; `scripts/catalog_check.py` C5 puts it there, and it is never a finding on its own.
- Source of the number: Apple's own Hebrew translations measured against their English, 79,880 pairs from 1,269 iOS 26.1 (23B86) UI bundles, 45,584 unique, VoiceOver tables excluded (`length-stats.json`, 2026-09-16). 1.00× is the ratio, rounded to 0.05, that Hebrew exceeds as often (31.8%) as Apple's Romanian exceeds 1.35× (31.6%) — the calibration point chosen because the `ro` catalog has 299 of 1135 units over its trigger. A flat 1.35× would flag only 7.7% of Hebrew strings and would miss the short controls that actually break layouts.
- Hebrew is usually **shorter** than English: median 0.923×, p75 1.098×, p90 1.333×. The expansion lives entirely in short strings: in the 1–10 character band the median is 1.0× and 20.1% pass 1.35×, while in the 41–120 band only 0.7% do. The all-caps band is the extreme — 1,425 pairs, median 1.25×, 45.3% over 1.35× and 36.1% over 2× — which is exactly the eyebrow and caption problem Header and label forms describes.
- Share of the catalog over the trigger at this snapshot: **not measurable**, because 0 `he` units exist. The 31.8% measured on Apple's own Hebrew is the expectation to plan renders against, not a catalog fact.
- Counts never establish fit. Verdicts come from render evidence against each view's own floor and line limit (`references/surface-budgets.md`, `references/render-evidence.md`), and for `he` the first render pass must also check RTL layout: semantic leading and trailing, mixed Latin and Hebrew runs, the price and the timer, punctuation position, and VoiceOver reading order (`references/locale-decisions.md` `## RTL`). No Moneyfesting screen has ever been rendered right to left.

## Decided conflicts

| Conflict | Winner | Rule |
|---|---|---|
| catalog tag `he` vs `iw` | `he` | Xcode emits only `he.lproj`, `LOCLocaleCode.plist` has no `iw` entry, iOS ships no `iw.lproj`, App Store Connect uses `he`, and a catalog holding both produced a nondeterministic value |
| plural columns: Xcode lists one/two/many/other, ICU reports one/two/other | one/two/other | the runtime selects the categories, not the editor; `many` is unreachable and holds a copy of `other` if Xcode writes it |
| quotation marks: Foundation/CLDR `he_IL` straight `"` vs Apple's gershayim `״` | `״…״` (U+05F4) | Apple's shipped Hebrew UI is the register the app mirrors, and the mock alerts must match it character for character; the CLDR delimiter is what a formatter would emit, not what iOS Hebrew prints |
| ellipsis: `en` ships 9 keys with `...` and 2 with `…` | `…` (U+2026) everywhere | Apple renders the English `Loading...` as `טוען…`, so Hebrew normalises the mark rather than mirroring the source's inconsistency |
| CTA form: the surface brief's bare imperative vs iOS Hebrew's verbal noun | verbal noun | `references/surface-briefs.md` `## CTA rule` says the app register, and no iOS Hebrew button is an imperative; the verbal noun also removes the gender the imperative would force |
| "Done" on the illustrated iOS pill | `סיום`, the same as the app's own Done | Apple's `he` uses one word where `ro` splits `Gata` from `OK`; the mirror happens to equal the preferred form, and the row still says **system mirror** so a future iOS change is caught |
| "Don't Allow": one `en` label, two Apple `he` values | `סירוב` by default, `לא לאפשר` on the Screen Time mock | both keys mirror a specific iOS alert, and the two alerts genuinely differ in Hebrew; the glossary carries the split as a role |
| notifications: `התראות` vs `עדכונים` | `עדכונים` for notifications, `התראות` for alerts inside one | Apple's own body sentence uses both words in one line with exactly that division |
| all-caps `en` headers | no case change; shorten by word choice | Hebrew has no letter case, so the layout intent has to be carried by the words |

## Open decisions

Each item is mirrored in `glossary/he.json` `open` with the fields `scripts/term_audit.py` reads. Until an item is closed, agents follow the "meanwhile" line and the adjudicator lists every key that touched it. There are no `translated` `he` keys to lock, so nothing here is blocked by the owner lock — only by the decision itself.

- **Gender strategy** (`gender_strategy`) — the central `he` decision, and the one that shapes every other line. Options: (1) gender-free phrasing throughout — verbal-noun buttons, impersonal `ניתן` / `יש ל־` / `באפשרותך`, unpointed second-person past, nominal headlines, no present-tense participle aimed at the reader; (2) masculine second person, the common Israeli consumer-app default; (3) double address with a slash or a mid-word dot. Evidence for (1): iOS Hebrew does exactly this across buttons (`אישור`, `סירוב`, `המשך`, `לא לאפשר`), instructions (`יש ללחוץ…`), explanations (`ניתן להפעיל…`, `באפשרותך לקבל…`) and possessives (`שלך`, `עבורך`), and the one string that must choose a gender chooses masculine (`מבקש`) while keeping the address to the reader unmarked (`לך`) — see Address and gender for the bundle/table/key of each. Evidence for (2): it is warmer on celebration copy, where (1) can turn a badge line into a notice, and it is what most Israeli apps ship; the cost is that every badge, every alert and every empty state tells a woman the app assumed a man. Evidence against (3): unreadable at widget and badge sizes, and already ruled out for every locale (`references/locale-decisions.md` `## Voice choices that need locale review`). Affected keys: the hotspot list in Address and gender, plus the four badge role nouns. **Recommended: (1)**, because it is the register the device itself uses for these exact jobs, so the app sounds native rather than cautious. **Meanwhile**: write (1); never invent the reader's gender; where no neutral form exists use masculine singular and list the key in the report.
- **App noun** (`app_noun`) — `יישום`, Apple's word, against `אפליקציה`, what Israelis say. Evidence for `יישום`: it is Apple's term everywhere in the harvest (`יישומים` for Apps, `הסרת היישום`, `שיתוף היישום`, `ביישומים ואתרים`, `ביישום ״בריאות״`). Evidence for `אפליקציה`: the skill's own rule is that app-owned copy reads as a local product team wrote it, and `אפליקציה` is the ordinary consumer word. Affected keys: the Screen Time, restricted-list, plan, share and widget families listed in `glossary/he.json`. **Recommended: `אפליקציה` for app-owned copy, `יישום` verbatim inside anything that mirrors an iOS alert**, because the Platform terms rule already splits those two registers and this keeps both honest. **Meanwhile**: follow the recommendation, swap `glossary/he.json` `restricted_list`'s form spellings together with the decision, and never mix the two words in one batch.
- **Focus term** (`focus_session`) — `ריכוז` against `מיקוד`. `ריכוז` is the everyday word and Apple's own name for the iOS Focus mode (`Preferences.app | Localizable.strings | Focus`; `FocusUIModule.bundle | Localizable.strings | MODULE_DEFAULT_TITLE`), which is also its cost: the app's bare labels (`profile.home.focusSection`, `onboarding.welcome.preview.blocking.active`, `widgets.startFocus.paused`) would read as the system mode at a glance, the same overlap `ro` accepted. `מיקוד` avoids the overlap but is the standard Israeli word for a postal code and appears in no Apple he UI string for attention. **Recommended: `ריכוז`**, because a reader must understand the word before they can confuse it with anything. **Meanwhile**: new strings follow the recommendation; a flip is a global term change with its own follow-up batch, never a per-string choice.
- **Forecast label** (`forecast`) — `תחזית` against `הערכה` against `צפי`, for `insights.savings.forecast` beside `insights.savings.monthEnd`. VoiceOver reads the two one after the other, so they need different words, and neither may read as a promise. **Recommended: `תחזית` for the forecast line, leaving `הערכה` / `משוער` for the month-end figure.** **Meanwhile**: keep the two distinct and use one form of the `Forecast:` prefix across all three explanation keys.
- **Add Widget on the gallery button** (`add_widget_gallery_label`) — `onboarding.widgets.howTo.addWidgetLabel` is drawn both on the edit-menu row (`HomeScreenJiggleIllustrationView.swift:167`) and on the gallery button (`:274`), and is quoted by `step2` and `step5`. iOS 26.1 Hebrew labels them differently (`הוספת וידג׳ט` against ` הוספת הווידג׳ט`), exactly as it does in `ro` and `tr`, so one key cannot mirror both. Options: a new gallery key for the button and `step5` (a code change in `WidgetsHowToViewModel` and the illustration; `en` stays "Add Widget", so `OnboardingWidgetsStepTests.swift:178` still holds), or the shared key with a recorded reason and one of the two buttons wrong. **Recommended: the new gallery key**, because it is the same fix `ro`, `tr` and `fr` need and doing it once serves all of them. **Meanwhile**: no key is added without the owner, and the illustration keeps the edit-menu value.
- **Badge role nouns** (`badge_role_nouns`) — `achievements.badge.hired.name` "Hired", `.salaried.name` "Salaried", `.rehired.name` "Rehired", `.permanent.name` "Permanent" label the reader, and Hebrew has no gender-free noun for any of them. Options: rewrite the four as states or events (`התקבלת`, `עברת לשכר חודשי`, `חזרת לתפקיד`, `קביעות`), which keeps the workplace conceit but changes the grammatical shape of four of 24 badge names; or keep role nouns in masculine singular under the `gender_strategy` default. **Recommended: rewrite the four**, because a badge name is read as a label on the reader and is the worst place to guess. **Meanwhile**: no badge name is written until `gender_strategy` is closed, since the two decisions move together.
- **Currency presentation** (`currency_presentation`) — not a wording decision, but it lands on the same screens: the app prints `‏120 ‏ILS` where `he_IL` prints `‏120.00 ‏₪` (`AppCurrency.swift:24-36`). Options: add ILS to the `.narrow` list, or ship the ISO code and let the copy around it carry the meaning. **Recommended: add ILS**, because no Israeli price is written with a three-letter code, and copy cannot compensate for a formatter. **Meanwhile**: agents never write `₪` or `ILS` into a string — the placeholder carries the amount — and the paywall, insights and widget renders record what the reader actually sees.
