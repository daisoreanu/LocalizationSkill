## Identity

- Tag `hu` (bare code). `hu-HU` negotiates to `hu` in both the v1 and wave-2 language lists, iOS ships `hu.lproj`, and `xcrun xcstringstool compile` writes `hu.lproj`; `LOCLocaleCode.plist` gives displayName "Hungarian", lproj "hu" (apple-coverage 2026-09-16). No regional variant is selectable in-app (`AppLanguage.overrideCode` stores a bare code, `Manifesting/Foundation/Localization/AppLanguage.swift:9-22`).
- Audience: Hungarian speakers on an iPhone set to Hungarian, region HU; format locale `hu_HU`, audience currency HUF. Latin script, left to right. The diacritics that carry meaning are á é í ó ö ő ú ü ű, and ő/ű are double acute, never tilde or circumflex (Typography check below).
- Snapshot 2026-09-16 against catalog commit c8a49dd: 1189 keys in the working tree, `en` 1142 localized, `ro` 1135, `hu` 0. Counts in this brief are `en` counts; there is no Hungarian copy to count and no shipped form to align to.

## Audience and register

- A friendly coach in everyday Hungarian: short sentences, one idea per line, words a reader who knows no English understands on first read. The copy sits beside a timer and a money figure, so plain beats clever and a common word beats a rarer exact one.
- Apple's Hungarian is the reference for anything that mirrors iOS, not for the app's own warmth: Apple writes sentence case, verbal-noun buttons and formal `Ön` (`Applications/FamilyControlsAuthenticationUI.app | Localizable.strings | NOTIFICATION_AUTH_REQUEST_MESSAGE_INDIVIDUAL`). Address is the open decision `address_formality`; the button form is decided (Control forms).
- Use `app` as the common noun, not `alkalmazás`: Apple's Hungarian says `appok`, `App megosztása`, `Főképernyő és appkönyvtár`, `az Egészség appban` (`System/Library/PrivateFrameworks/SpringBoardHome.framework | SpringBoardHome.strings | SHARE_APPLICATION_SHORTCUT_ITEM_TITLE`; `Applications/Preferences.app | Localizable.strings | Home Screen & App Library`; `System/Library/PrivateFrameworks/HealthUI.framework | HealthUI-Localizable.strings | AUTHORIZATION_DONT_ALLOW_ALERT_MESSAGE`).

## Address and gender

- **Gender is not a Hungarian problem.** Hungarian has no grammatical gender: one third-person pronoun `ő` covers every person, adjectives and participles do not agree with the subject, and nouns have no gender. The `en` hotspots that force a gendered choice in Romance locales are therefore free here, and a translator needs no neutral strategy for them: the reader predicates (`achievements.badge.hired.earned` "You are making time for what matters", `achievements.streak.body` "You showed up %lld days in a row", `achievements.streak.comebackBody` "Welcome back", `achievements.badge.rehired.earned` "you're back", `widgets.tasks.allDone` "All done for today"), the badge role nouns (24 `achievements.badge.*.name`, `Hired` / `Salaried` / `Rehired` / `Permanent` among them) and the badge category names (`achievements.category.payroll.name` and its four siblings) all take one invariant Hungarian form.
- The one gender surface left is the profile picker itself, which names genders rather than agreeing with one: `user.gender.male`, `.female`, `.other`, `.preferNotToSay`. Translate them as the plain list they are; `Other` takes `Egyéb`, not a person noun. The stored profile gender (`Manifesting/Features/User/Domain/Entities/Gender.swift`) is not a copy input without a product decision.
- **Address is the real choice.** Hungarian has a formal `Ön` (third-person verb) and an informal `te` (second-person), and every reader-directed line commits to one. Evidence on both sides, from Apple itself:

| For `Ön` (formal) | For `te` (informal) |
|---|---|
| Every iOS system surface the app mirrors is `Ön`: "A(z) „%@” értesítéseket szeretne **Önnek** küldeni" (`System/Library/PrivateFrameworks/UserNotificationsServer.framework \| Localizable.strings \| USER_NOTIFICATION_PERMISSION_ALERT_TITLE`), "láthatja **az Ön** tevékenységadatait" (`Applications/FamilyControlsAuthenticationUI.app \| Localizable.strings \| NOTIFICATION_AUTH_REQUEST_MESSAGE_INDIVIDUAL`), "bekapcsol**hatja**" with the pronoun dropped (`System/Library/PrivateFrameworks/HealthUI.framework \| HealthUI-Localizable.strings \| AUTHORIZATION_DONT_ALLOW_ALERT_MESSAGE`), "Értesítést kap**hat**, amikor elérhetővé válik a heti jelentése" (`System/Library/PrivateFrameworks/ScreenTimeSettingsUI.framework \| Localizable.strings \| WeeklyReportNotificationGroupSpecifierFooterText`). Apple's Hungarian support prose keeps the same distance, heading actions as verbal nouns: "A Wi-Fi jelszavának megkeresése és másolása" (support.apple.com/hu-hu/guide/iphone/iph9374b7411/ios, fetched 2026-09-16). | Apple's own Hungarian **switches to `te` for coaching copy**, in the same OS: the Fitness award tables shipped beside the formal ones read "Meg tudod csinálni", "figyelj a tested jelzéseire", "Ha teljesítesz egy {{goal_value}} napos Mozgás-szériát" (`System/Library/Health/Assets/FitnessUIAssets.bundle/ActivityAchievements/MonthlyAchievements/localization/challenge/MoveStreak_SMALL_INCREASE \| Localizable-Modified-tinker.stringsdict \| ACHIEVEMENT_UNACHIEVED_ALERT_DESCRIPTION_NAME`) and "Zárd be három napon keresztül a gyűrűidet" (`System/Library/Health/Assets/FitnessUIAssets.bundle/ActivityAchievements/365AllGoals/localization \| Localizable-Modified-tinker.stringsdict \| ACHIEVEMENT_UNACHIEVED_PROGRESS_THREE_AWAY_DESC`), against the formal "Zárja be a gyűrűit még 3 alkalommal" in the same folder's `Localizable.stringsdict`. Moneyfesting's whole register is that second column: streak celebrations, badge unlock lines, shield nudges and welcome headlines. |

  Recommendation and the meanwhile rule are under Open decisions (`address_formality`); the recommendation is `te`.
- Whichever way it lands, three rules do not move. (1) A string that mirrors or quotes an iOS alert keeps Apple's `Ön` verbatim, including "Önnek" in the notification mock title: the seven mock-alert keys are `onboarding.screenTimePermission.alertTitle`, `.alertMessage`, `.alertDoNotAllow`, `onboarding.notificationPermission.alertTitle`, `.alertMessage`, `.alertAllow`, `.alertDoNotAllow`. (2) Buttons are verbal nouns and carry no address at all (Control forms), so a flip costs no control key. (3) The app speaks about itself in the first person plural, as it already does in `en`: 19 `en` units open that way ("We couldn't save your session changes", `moneyfesting.details.finalizeFailureAlertMessage`; "We'll try again soon.", `widgets.focusRing.a11y.unavailable`, `widgets.streak.a11y.unavailable`, `widgets.streak.copy.unavailable.3`; "We covered %@ with a streak freeze.", `streakFreeze.usedBody`) -> Hungarian `-unk/-ünk` ("Nem sikerült elmentenünk…", "Hamarosan újra próbáljuk.").
- Grep aid for address, not a validator: `Ön|Önt|Önnek|Önnél|Önhöz|Önről|Önök` catches the formal pronoun, and the formal verb is the bare third-person singular (`koppintson`, `nyissa meg`, `kapcsolja be`) against the informal `-j/-d` imperative (`koppints`, `nyisd meg`, `kapcsold be`). Because Hungarian drops pronouns, the pronoun grep alone under-reports; the reliable signal is the verb ending, which no regular expression settles on its own.

## Header and label forms

- Sentence case for every app-owned string: screen titles, buttons, body, section headers. Apple's Hungarian is sentence case throughout ("Widget hozzáadása", "Hiányzó vásárlások visszaállítása", "Automatikus megújítás letiltva"), so the 114 Title Case `en` keys lose their capitals in Hungarian — `focusTask.category.selfCare` "Self-Care & Home Tasks" becomes one capital, not four.
- Screen titles and action headers take the verbal noun, matching Apple's own headings: "Set Your Hourly Wage" (`onboarding.hourlyWage.title`) is "Az óradíjad beállítása", not a command. Apple's Hungarian support headings do the same ("A Wi-Fi jelszavának megkeresése és másolása", support.apple.com/hu-hu/guide/iphone/iph9374b7411/ios, fetched 2026-09-16).
- Uppercase eyebrows and stat captions stay uppercase in the string where `en` is uppercase (34 all-caps `en` strings under the inventory's rule), and **every diacritic survives the uppercasing**, double acute included: Apple ships "IDŐZÍTŐ" (`System/Library/Messages/iMessageApps/SafetyMonitorMessages.bundle | Localizable.strings | TIMER`), "TŰZIJÁTÉK" (`System/Library/Messages/iMessageEffects/CKFireworksEffect.bundle | InfoPlist.strings | AXEffectPickerTitle`), "CÉL" (`System/Library/PrivateFrameworks/HealthToolbox.framework | WellnessDashboard-Localizable.strings | SLEEP_GOAL_HEADER`), "ÉRTESÍTÉSEK", "ESZKÖZÖK". Hungarian has no casing special case: `'ő'.upper()` is `Ő` and `'ű'.upper()` is `Ű` under Unicode simple case mapping (Python 3.14.6 on macOS 15.7.9, the same mapping Foundation applies), there is no dotless i, and there is no ß. A stripped-accent eyebrow ("IDOZITO") is a finding, never a layout fix.
- Known uppercase targets, seed 2026-09-16: `insights.mood.mood` MOOD -> LELKIÁLLAPOT (Apple's word for State of Mind, `MentalHealthWidgetUI.framework | Localizable.strings | MENTAL_HEALTH_WIDGET_LABEL`), `insights.summary.dayStreakTitle` DAY STREAK -> SZÉRIA NAPJAI, `insights.focus.minutesUnit` MIN -> PERC (`NanoTimeKit/ComplicationBundles/ActivityComplicationBundleCompanion.bundle | Localizable.strings | ACTIVITY_TITLE_MIN`), `onboarding.hourlyWage.unit` and `profile.hourlyWage.unit` "%@ / HOUR" -> "%@ / ÓRA" (`NanoTimeKit.framework | ClockFaces.strings | COUNTDOWN_HOUR_SINGLE`), `plan.dailyPlan.plannedHeader` / `doneHeader` / `anytimeHeader` -> TERVEZETT / KÉSZ / BÁRMIKOR. These expand hardest (Length trigger: the all-caps p90 is 2.18×).
- Section headers are plain nouns or noun phrases agreeing with nothing: Hungarian has no adjective agreement, so a fragment with no visible noun carries no agreement risk. What it does carry is **case government**: a fragment that continues a sentence or labels a value must take the case the sentence needs, and a suffix chosen for the wrong governing word is the fragment fault the editor checks (`widgets.streak.copy.frozen.small` "Covered by a freeze." beside `widgets.streak.copy.frozen.medium` "Yesterday was covered." must agree on what is covered, the day, not on gender).
- Tab titles are nouns: Moneyfesting, Terv, Statisztika, Profil (`main.tab.*`), seed 2026-09-16.
- Toggles and state labels take an invariant participle or adverbial: "Szüneteltetve" (`widgets.startFocus.paused`), "Folyamatban" (`widgets.startFocus.inProgress`), "Elmentve" (`common.editor.saved`), "%lld app letiltva" (`screenTime.restrictedApps.appsRestricted`). They name the state, never the button that caused it.
- Typography check the skill could police: `ő` is U+0151 and `ű` is U+0171 (LATIN SMALL LETTER O/U WITH DOUBLE ACUTE), capitals U+0150 and U+0170. The lookalikes `õ` U+00F5 (o with tilde), `û` U+00FB (u with circumflex), `Õ` U+00D5 and `Û` U+00DB are wrong in Hungarian and come from Latin-1 keyboards and legacy fonts. `scripts/catalog_check.py` has exactly this check for Romanian (`check_cedilla`, rule C4, major) but gates it on `locale == "ro"` (`:545`), so `hu` has no equivalent today: an engineering proposal for the report, never an edit from a translation run. Grep aid meanwhile: `[õûÕÛ]`.

## Short forms and units

One form per concept; exceptions are named per key. Apple's Hungarian keeps **a space between the number and the unit at every width**, unlike English `45m`: `%@h` -> `%@ ó`, `%@ hr` -> `%@ óra`, `%@m` -> `%@ p`, `%@ min` -> `%@ perc`, `%@s` -> `%@ mp` (`System/Library/PrivateFrameworks/HealthUI.framework | HealthUI-Localizable.strings | HOURS_PAIR_SHORT, HOURS_PAIR_MEDIUM, MINUTES_PAIR_SHORT, MINUTES_PAIR_MEDIUM, SECONDS_PAIR_SHORT`). Foundation agrees: `Duration.formatted(.units(width: .abbreviated))` renders 1 h 45 min as `1 ó és 45 p` and 25 min as `25 p` in `hu_HU` (locale-formats `durations`, ICU 76.1 on macOS 15.7.9).

## Numbers, dates, currency and punctuation

Values below are Foundation/ICU output for `hu_HU` rendered on macOS 15.7.9 (ICU 76.1.0.0, CLDR 46.0.0.0) through the app's own call sites, from `locale-formats.json`; invisible characters are written as code points. Device settings (a forced 12-hour clock, a custom region format) change output on a real device, and iOS 18, the deployment target, was not inspected.

- Numbers group with U+00A0 NO-BREAK SPACE and separate decimals with a comma: `12 000`, `1 250 000`, `1250,5`. Four-digit integers are **not** grouped (`2000` stays `2000`, `12000` becomes `12 000`), so a literal number in copy follows the same rule.
- Money never appears as a literal. Runtime values arrive through `AppCurrency.format` with `AppLanguage.effectiveLocale`, which uses the ISO code presentation for HUF: `120 HUF`, `1250,5 HUF` (NBSP before the code). The locale's own standard form would be `120,00 Ft`; the app does not use it, and copy never writes `Ft`, `forint` or any currency name, because the currency is the user's own choice and independent of the language. Compact figures are `1,25k HUF` / `1,25m HUF` / `1,25b HUF` against the locale's `1,25 E HUF` / `1,25 M HUF` / `1,25 Mrd` — an engineering finding, not something to translate around (open decision `compact_currency_suffix`).
- Percent is glued to the number with no space: `40%`, `100%` (U+0025). Discount badges keep `-%lld%%`.
- Time is 24-hour (`Locale.hourCycle` h23, `DateFormatter` template `j` resolves to `H`): `9:41`, `23:59`. The `en` axis labels 12AM / 06AM / 12PM / 06PM become `00:00` / `06:00` / `12:00` / `18:00` (`insights.appUsage.axis*`, `profile.appUsage.axis.*`, `screenTimeReport.axis*`).
- Dates run big-endian with periods and a trailing period on the day: `2026. dec. 31.`, `2026. december 31.`, `május 12.`, and the weekday alone is `kedd`. **Month and weekday names are lower case** in Hungarian, including inside a date; capitalising them is a finding. A date inside a sentence takes the `-n/-án/-én` suffix ("%@ napján"), which is why `streakFreeze.usedBody` and `streakFreeze.dayAccessibility` need the weekday placeholder read as a noun, not a label.
- Quotes are `„ ”` (U+201E, U+201D); the nested pair is `» «` (U+00BB then U+00AB, pointing inward, the reverse of French). The 11 `en` units carrying a straight double quote all take `„ ”`, including `moneyfesting.details.quoteWrapper` "%@" -> "„%@”" and the two mock alert titles. Quote attribution and asides take the en dash `–` with spaces; ranges take the en dash with no spaces (`18–24`, `5–120 perc`). Ellipsis: three periods, matching the `en` majority (9 keys with three periods against 2 with the character).
- The definite article changes with the following sound: `a` before a consonant, `az` before a vowel. Where a placeholder follows the article and its first sound is unknown, Apple writes `A(z)` ("A(z) „%@” hozzá szeretne férni a Képernyőidőhöz"), and the mirrored mock alerts copy that exactly. App-owned copy may write plain `a %@` **only** where the placeholder resolves to the app display name, which is always `Moneyfesting` or `Moneyfesting Dev` (`INFOPLIST_KEY_CFBundleDisplayName` in `Manifesting.xcodeproj/project.pbxproj`): that covers `onboarding.widgets.howTo.searchTitle` "Find %@" and `step3` "Search for %@.". Any other placeholder takes `A(z)`.
- **Plural categories: `one` and `other` only** (ICU 76.1 keywords for `hu`; Xcode's `variationConfigFile.plist` -> `PluralRule.ComplementedPluralKeys.hu` lists the same pair, md5 70f835948deaffec44ffc7d95cc0ffc5). `one` selects on exactly the integer 1; every other integer 0..999999 and every tested fraction selects `other`, including 0, 2, 101 and 1.5. Boundary integers to exercise: **0, 1, 2, 101, and the fraction 1.5**; 0 is `other`, and 101 is `other` too, so Hungarian has none of Romanian's surprises. A `zero` variant, if one is ever written, wins for 0 in Foundation regardless of CLDR, so do not add one.
- **Numerals take the singular noun.** Hungarian counts `5 nap`, `20 perc`, `160 óra` — never `napok`, `percek`, `órák` — so the `one` and `other` branches usually carry the *same* noun and differ only where the rest of the sentence changes, and a plural noun beside a number is a finding in either branch. The counted noun still takes whatever case the sentence governs (`%lld napot`, `7 napból %lld`). Consequences: the 29 `en` plural keys need both Hungarian branches written even when they come out identical (`xcstringstool` requires `other`; Xcode lists `one` for `hu`), and the six keys whose plural exists in `ro` only (`achievements.streak.body`, `.comebackBody`, `.headline`, `.milestoneBody`, `.nextMilestoneBody`, `.weekAccessibility`) need a Hungarian judgement per key rather than an automatic split.
- Multiplication in the formula lines uses `×` (`achievements.category.wages.description`, 5 `en` keys).

## Length trigger

- A `hu` string longer than **1.35×** its `en` length goes on the render list; `scripts/catalog_check.py` C5 puts it there, not among findings. The ratio is measured from Apple's own Hungarian, not guessed: 78 760 English/Hungarian pairs from 1 269 iOS 26.1 (23B86) UI bundles, 45 585 unique, median 1.196, p75 1.429, p90 1.75, and 31.57% of unique pairs above 1.35×, the share that matches Apple's `ro` corpus at the same threshold (length-stats 2026-09-16). Short strings expand hardest: in the 1-10 character bucket the median is already 1.2 and the p90 is 2.0.
- All-caps strings are the worst case, which is exactly where the app's eyebrows and captions live: 941 all-caps pairs, median 1.188, p75 1.5, **p90 2.18**, and 10.6% above 2×. Treat every uppercase caption (`insights.summary.*`, `widgets.focusRing.row.*`, `widgets.focusRing.title`, `plan.dailyPlan.*Header`) as a render item from the first draft.
- Counts never establish fit. Verdicts come from render evidence against each view's own floor and Dynamic Type cap (`references/surface-budgets.md`, `references/render-evidence.md`); the tightest rows are the streak mascot lines (20 characters small, 24 medium) and the Start Focus action capsule (about 118 points of text). Shortening cuts words, never facts; accessibility strings are exempt.

## Emoji and intensity

- Preserve every source emoji and interjection and add none. Hungarian motivational writing reaches for the exclamation more readily than this product does — Apple's own Hungarian coaching copy is full of them ("Hajrá!", "Meg tudod csinálni!") — so intensity is the thing an editor should be watching for, and a brief may soften intensity, never raise it (`references/voice-card.md` `## Persuasion limits`).

## Open decisions

Each item is mirrored in `glossary/hu.json` `open` with the fields `scripts/term_audit.py` reads. Nothing here is decided by an agent; until the owner closes one, agents follow the meanwhile rule and the adjudicator lists every key that touched the term.

- **Address and formality (`address_formality`).** Affects every reader-directed line in the catalog: the 177 second-person possessive units, the 5 "you are" and 5 "are you" units, every criterion and earned line under `achievements.badge.*`, the 25 shield pairs and the six notification bodies. Rule: app-owned copy uses one address throughout; a string that mirrors an iOS alert follows the device and keeps `Ön` either way. Options: **`te`**, the informal address (0 keys, nothing shipped); **`Ön`**, the formal address (0 keys). Evidence on each side is the two-column table under `## Address and gender`: every iOS system surface the app mirrors is `Ön`, while Apple's own Hungarian coaching copy in Fitness switches to `te` in a table shipped beside the formal one. Recommended: **`te`**, because Moneyfesting's whole register is the coaching column — streak celebrations, badge unlocks, shield nudges and welcome headlines — and Apple itself uses `te` for exactly that kind of line in Hungarian. Meanwhile: draft app-owned copy in `te`, keep every mirror at Apple's `Ön`, and have the translator list each ID whose address is visible, so a flip is a mechanical pass over a known list rather than a re-read of the catalog. Buttons are verbal nouns and change either way.
- **The focus term (`focus_session`).** Affects `moneyfesting.details.*`, `focusTask.*`, `widgets.startFocus.*`, `profile.sessionDetails.*`, `insights.sessions.*`, `onboarding.dailyFocusGoal.*`, `widgets.focusRing.*` and the ring and streak widget lines. Rule: one term for the concept across the app, chosen once, never per string. Options: **`fókuszmenet`** (a compound with `menet`, "a round"; 13 characters, fits the streak widget's 20-character small line); **`fókuszidő`** ("focus time", the plainest, but it collides with the app's own time and time-value vocabulary, which prices `idő` in money); **`fókuszfoglalkozás`** (`foglalkozás` is Apple's Hungarian for a guided session, `System/Library/PrivateFrameworks/FitnessFiltering.framework | Localizable.strings | Sessions`, but 17 characters and it reads like a class you attend). Apple's Hungarian for the system Focus mode is `Fókusz` (`Applications/Preferences.app | Localizable.strings | Focus`), the same overlap `en` already accepts between the app's focus and the iOS mode. Recommended: **`fókuszmenet`**, because it keeps `fókusz`, the word a Hungarian reader recognises and the word Apple uses, while staying short enough for the widget lines that `fókuszfoglalkozás` would blow. Meanwhile: a new string follows its own screen's majority and the packet's same-source siblings, and the adjudicator lists every key that touched the term; `daily_focus_goal` in the glossary depends on this choice and follows it.
- **The streak freeze term (`streak_freeze_term`).** Affects the 12 `streakFreeze.*` and frozen-state keys. Rule: where no local image carries the source image, state the meaning plainly rather than translate the picture. Options: **`szériavédő`** (a thing that protects the streak; states what it does); **`szériafagyasztás`** (the literal freeze, a calque no Hungarian app uses); **`szériamentés`** (a save, but `mentés` is already the Save control). Recommended: **`szériavédő`**, because the freeze's whole job is to protect a day and the plain noun says so without an English picture. Meanwhile: use `szériavédő`, and the translator flags any line where a "protect" reading would overstate the feature — one earned freeze covers one day, never two.
- **The forecast label (`forecast`).** Keys: `insights.savings.forecast` (en "Forecast") beside `insights.savings.monthEnd` (the month-end figure), plus the three explanation keys `insights.savings.estimateExplanation`, `.measuredEstimateExplanation`, `.focusHistoryEstimateExplanation` and `insights.appUsage.permissionDescription`. Recommended: **`Előrejelzés`** for the forecast line, leaving `becsült` / `becslés` for the month-end figure and the estimate explanations, which is also Apple's word for an estimate. Meanwhile: keep the two distinct in every draft and reconcile the explanation prefixes in the same batch.
- **The illustrated Edit pill and the step that quotes it (`edit_pill_step_wording`).** Keys: `onboarding.widgets.howTo.editPillLabel` and `step2` ("Tap Edit in the top corner, then Add Widget."), drawn at `Manifesting/Features/Onboarding/Business/WidgetsHowTo/View/HomeScreenJiggleIllustrationView.swift:151`. Rule: an illustrated iOS label mirrors the device verbatim. Apple's Hungarian value for that pill is the plus sign `+` (`SpringBoardHome.strings | EDIT`), not a word, so the mirror is `+` and `step2` cannot quote a verb the way `en` and `ro` do. Options: **`+`** on the pill, with `step2` naming the button ("Koppints a bal felső sarokban lévő + gombra, majd a Widget hozzáadása elemre."); **`Szerkesztés`** on the pill, which keeps a quotable word but contradicts the verbatim rule and shows the user a label their Home Screen does not have. A yes needs a render check of the pill at `.large` (a one-character label in a pill sized for a word) and of the 304-point menu beside it. Meanwhile: no key is written against this step until the owner answers; the other four steps and the nine mirror labels proceed.
- **The hourly rate term (`hourly_rate_term`).** Affects `onboarding.hourlyWage.*`, `profile.hourlyWage.*`, `currency.settings.*`, `screenTimeReport.missingWage` and `achievements.category.wages.description`. Rule: one term for the concept across the app, chosen once, never per string. Options: **`óradíj`** (the rate a person charges for their own time; reads closer to a professional service's billing rate — a lawyer's or plumber's `óradíj` — than a plain personal number); **`órabér`** (the literal rendering of "hourly wage"; in everyday Hungarian it is not employer-only, it is also the ordinary word freelancers and self-employed people use for the hourly rate they themselves set, so banning it outright risks the opposite of "plain beats clever"). Neither the brief nor the glossary cites an Apple string or corpus evidence pinning `órabér` as employer-paid only, so the split is a live product/market call, not a settled fact. Recommended: **`óradíj`**, provisionally, because the app prices the user's own time rather than paying anyone, and `óradíj` keeps that distinction visible even though it risks reading as more formal. Meanwhile: use `óradíj`, and the translator flags any line where `órabér` would plainly read more naturally to a Hungarian freelancer, so a flip is a mechanical pass over a known list.
- **The compact money suffix (`compact_currency_suffix`).** Not a translation item: `AppCurrency.formatCompact` appends a hard-coded Latin `k`/`m`/`b` (`Manifesting/Foundation/Localization/AppCurrency.swift:53-57`), reaching `FocusRingCopy.swift:25`, `MonthlyTimeValueTile.swift:53,68`, `InsightsSavingsCard.swift:36,46` and `ScreenTimeUsageReportViews.swift:126,251`. Hungarian abbreviates thousands as `E`, millions as `M` and billions as `Mrd`, so a Hungarian reader sees `1,25k HUF` where the locale would print `1,25 E HUF` (locale-formats `compact_finding`, severity medium; the thousands letter differs outright, the millions letter only in case). Options: **`E`**, the locale's own suffixes through `Decimal.FormatStyle.Currency(...).notation(.compactName)`, which the facts file already renders for `hu_HU` (`1,25 E HUF`, `1,25 M HUF`, `1,25 Mrd`); **`k`**, keeping `k`/`m`/`b` as a deliberate cross-locale convention with a recorded reason. Recommended: **`E`**, because the `k` a Hungarian reader sees is not a Hungarian abbreviation for anything, and the same change fixes every locale whose compact forms differ. Meanwhile: never translate or hand-edit a suffix, report it as an engineering finding, and generate every packet sample value with the real formatter. `term_audit` cannot count these options, because the suffix is glued to a formatted number rather than written in a catalog value.
