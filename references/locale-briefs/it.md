## Audience and register

- A friendly coach in everyday Italian: short sentences, one idea per line, concrete verbs, no jargon and no imported English shapes even where a tech-literate Italian reader would recognise them ("tracciare", "streak", "reminder").
- Drop the source's scaffolding: 15 `en` keys carry "please" (14 capitalised); Apple's own error and retry copy is a bare imperative with no "per favore" (`generic_controls.retry_jetengine_swiftui` "Riprova"), so `common.editor.deleteFailedMessage` "The deletion couldn't be saved. Please try again." becomes one flat sentence plus "Riprova."
- Italian carries the possessive more lightly than English. 177 `en` keys carry a second-person possessive, and Apple's own Italian regularly drops it where the owner is obvious from context rather than inserting "tuo/tua" on every line: en "your activity data" ships as "i dati sulle attività", with no possessive at all (`Applications/FamilyControlsAuthenticationUI.app | Localizable.strings | NOTIFICATION_AUTH_REQUEST_MESSAGE_INDIVIDUAL`). A line like "il tuo tempo" is correct where the sentence has not yet established whose time is meant; it is an import to flag once the screen has already said so.
- Imported shapes the editor flags. ## Address and gender

- **`tu` throughout, never `Lei`.** Apple's Italian iOS uses `tu` in every register-bearing string harvested: "Puoi attivare le categorie relative ai dati sanitari in seguito nell'app Salute." (`System/Library/PrivateFrameworks/HealthUI.framework | HealthUI-Localizable.strings | AUTHORIZATION_DONT_ALLOW_ALERT_MESSAGE`), "Se consenti a %@ di accedere a "Tempo di utilizzo", l'app potrebbe essere in grado di visualizzare i dati sulle attività…" (`Applications/FamilyControlsAuthenticationUI.app | Localizable.strings | NOTIFICATION_AUTH_REQUEST_MESSAGE_INDIVIDUAL`), "Ricevi una notifica quando è disponibile il resoconto settimanale…" (`System/Library/PrivateFrameworks/ScreenTimeSettingsUI.framework | Localizable.strings | WeeklyReportNotificationGroupSpecifierFooterText`), and the imperative buttons "Consenti" / "Non consentire" / "Continua" (`System/Library/PrivateFrameworks/UserNotificationsServer.framework | Localizable.strings | PERMISSION_ALERT_ALLOW, PERMISSION_ALERT_DENY`; `Applications/FamilyControlsAuthenticationUI.app | Localizable.strings | COMMON_CONTINUE`). No `Lei` form appears anywhere in the harvest (register_summary: address "tu"). Since the app's own `en` source is already informal, no register switch is needed the way `ro` needed one between iOS and app copy.
- Possessive: keep it light — see Audience and register; drop "tuo/tua" where the owner is obvious from context, keep it where the sentence would otherwise read as generic or ambiguous.
- The app speaks about itself in the first person plural, as `en` does in 19 units: "We couldn't load the data." -> "Non siamo riusciti a caricare i dati." (`component.errorView.message`), "We covered %@ with a streak freeze." (`streakFreeze.usedBody`), "we'll use %@" (`onboarding.hourlyWage.footer`), "We'll try again soon." (`widgets.focusRing.a11y.unavailable`). The app's collective "we" takes the default masculine plural participle agreement Italian uses for an entity of unspecified composition ("siamo riusciti", not "riuscite").
- **Gender is the real hotspot here, unlike Dutch.** Italian inflects predicate adjectives and past participles for the person they describe, so every reader-facing "ready" / "focused" / "done" state forces a masculine-or-feminine choice English never asks for. Apple's own Italian defaults such an adjective to masculine with no signal about the reader at all: "Ready" -> "Pronto" (`System/Library/AccessibilityBundles/SiriUICore.axbundle | SiriUICoreAccessibility.strings | progress.indicator.value.idle`; `System/Library/PrivateFrameworks/TextToSpeechVoiceBankingSupport.framework | VoiceBankingSupport.strings | VB_READY`), even in Siri and accessibility contexts where no reader gender is known.
- Strategy, in order: (1) rephrase to avoid a predicate on the reader altogether, the way Apple's own confirmation alerts already do — "Are you sure you want to delete this factor?" ships as "Confermi di voler eliminare questo fattore?" (a verb, "confermi", never an adjective; `System/Library/Health/FeedItemPlugins/MenstrualCyclesAppPlugin.healthplugin | Localizable.strings | DELETE_CYCLE_FACTOR_ALERT_MESSAGE`), never "Sei sicuro/a…?" — apply the same move to `focusTask.alert.deleteTask.message` and `screenTime.restrictedAppsPicker.deleteAlertMessagePrefix` (both "Are you sure…" in `en`). (2) prefer an invariant noun or an object-agreeing participle over one that agrees with the reader — Apple's own "Fine" for Done is already a noun, not an adjective (Control forms); the badge family in Idioms and figurative language uses the same move throughout (participles agree with the badge's own object — "il cartellino", "il turno" — never with the reader). (3) where a predicate adjective on the reader cannot be avoided (the 44 reader-predicate `en` units by the en-inventory count: `you_are` 5, `are_you` 5, `you_showed_up` 1, `welcome_back` 1, `ready` 7, `focused` 19, `done` 15), default masculine, matching Apple's own unmarked choice: Open decisions `reader_gender_default`.
- `user.gender.*` is a data field, not voice: `user.gender.female` "Female", `.male` "Male", `.other` "Other", `.preferNotToSay` "Prefer not to say" — seed 2026-09-16: "Donna", "Uomo", "Altro", "Preferisco non dirlo". The stored profile gender (`Manifesting/Features/User/Domain/Entities/Gender.swift`) is not a copy input without a product decision.
- Grep aid, not a validator: `\bLei\b`, `\bSua\b` (capitalised formal forms), a literal slashed pair such as `sicuro/a` or `pronto/a` (the slash itself is a finding, not a fix), and any `-o/a` doubled ending.

## Control forms

Counts are `en` catalog keys whose `en` value is exactly the control word, the same match `scripts/term_audit.py` makes (en-inventory `## Control forms`); there is no `it` copy to count. **Local convention: the `tu` imperative for the affirmative, "non" plus the infinitive for the negative.** Apple's Italian runtime writes every affirmative button and menu item as a second-person imperative — "Annulla", "Salva", "Modifica", "Elimina", "Continua", "Salta", "Consenti", "Riprova", "Aggiungi widget", "Personalizza" — and negates it with the infinitive, never a negated imperative, because Italian's `tu` imperative has no synthetic negative form: "Non consentire" (`System/Library/PrivateFrameworks/UserNotificationsServer.framework | Localizable.strings | PERMISSION_ALERT_DENY`; `Applications/FamilyControlsAuthenticationUI.app | Localizable.strings | COMMON_DONT_ALLOW`), never "Non consenti". This holds on every app-owned button and every iOS mirror in the harvest without exception. (The task brief for this run floated "infinitive buttons" as Apple's Italian convention; the harvest does not support that — the affirmative is consistently the `tu` imperative, and only the negative takes the infinitive, which is the ordinary Italian grammar rule, not a stylistic choice.)

## Header and label forms

- **Sentence case everywhere in app-owned copy.** Apple's Italian is sentence case throughout the harvest ("Modifica la schermata Home", "Aggiungi widget", "Gestisci gli abbonamenti"), and Italian orthography capitalises only the first word plus proper names. The 114 Title Case `en` keys become sentence case: "Set Your Hourly Wage" -> "Imposta la tua tariffa oraria" (`onboarding.hourlyWage.title`), "Edit Focus Task" -> "Modifica compito di focus" (`focusTask.createTask.screenTitleEdit`), "My Tasks" -> "I miei compiti" (`focusTask.picker.myTasksHeader`). Proper names keep their capitals: Moneyfesting, Tempo di utilizzo, Face ID, App Store, Foto, Salute, Messaggi, Schermata Home.
- **Uppercase eyebrows and captions stay uppercase where `en` is** (34 all-caps `en` strings: `insights.mood.mood` "MOOD", `insights.summary.dayStreakTitle` "DAY STREAK", `widgets.focusRing.title` "TODAY'S FOCUS", `plan.dailyPlan.{planned,done,anytime}Header`, `%@ / HOUR`, `/%lld MIN`). Italian has no dotted/dotless-`i` hazard (Turkish) and no `ß` (German); accented vowels keep their diacritic when uppercased ("PERCHÉ", "CITTÀ") — this is standard Italian orthography, not separately verified against Foundation for `it_IT` at this snapshot, so flag it as unconfirmed rather than cited fact if a rendering surprise ever turns up. Uppercase captions expand hard regardless: length-stats shows `en` strings of 1-10 characters exceeding 1.35× in 38.4% of unique pairs generally, and 35.75% of the all-caps subset of that same length (p75 1.6, p90 2.25) — all-caps is not uniquely worse than short strings generally for Italian, but it is still the render-list hotspot because eyebrows and widget captions are exactly this length. Prefer the shorter everyday noun and file the row when none exists (`references/surface-budgets.md` `## Catalog microcopy templates`).
- Section headers agree in gender and number with the implied noun (see the agreement rule below); the three all-caps plan headers take participles or adverbials matching the implied plural: "PROGRAMMATE", "COMPLETATE", "IN QUALSIASI MOMENTO" — seed 2026-09-16, the call site appends the count.
- Tab titles are bare nouns: Moneyfesting, Piano, Statistiche, Profilo (`main.tab.*`; seed 2026-09-16 — "Approfondimenti" is closer to the literal for "Insights" but the tab shows charts and totals, so the plainer "Statistiche" wins; four labels share about 70pt each, `references/surface-budgets.md`).
- **Toggles and state labels take a predicative participle, and unlike Dutch, Italian participles do inflect** — this is the same gender hotspot as Address and gender. A state label pairing with a non-person noun is safe by that noun's own gender ("Bloccato" agreeing with "il sito"/"l'accesso", masculine); a state label describing the reader directly falls under Open decisions `reader_gender_default`.
- **The agreement rule for a fragment with no visible noun is grammatical gender and number, not just number as in Dutch.** Any article, past participle or attributive adjective in a fragment depends on the implied noun's gender, and Italian's own default for an unclear case is masculine singular, matching the Address-and-gender default. Example of the fault the editor checks: `widgets.streak.copy.frozen.small` "Covered by a freeze." beside `.medium` "Yesterday was covered." — the small tile's fragment has no visible noun, so the safe fallback is masculine singular ("Congelato."), while the medium tile's implied noun is "ieri" (day, masculine), so the same participle is confirmed correct rather than a guess ("Ieri è stato congelato.").

## Numbers, dates, currency and punctuation

All values below are Foundation/ICU output for `it_IT` (locale-formats, ICU 76.1.0.0 / CLDR 46.0.0.0), or the app's own `AppCurrency` path on that locale.

- **Numbers.** Grouping separator `.` (U+002E), decimal separator `,` (U+002C): `12000` -> "12.000", `1250.5` -> "1250,5", `1250000` -> "1.250.000". **Four-digit numbers are not grouped**: `2000` stays "2000" (locale-formats `numbers.four_digit_grouping`: "not grouped"), unlike Romanian or Dutch, which both group at four digits. A literal number in copy follows the same rule: `achievements.badge.wageYear.name` "2,000 hours of focus" -> "2000 ore di focus", never "2.000 ore di focus".
- **Money.** Runtime values arrive formatted through `AppLanguage.effectiveLocale`, so no string hard-codes a currency (`en` has no "EUR" or "euro" literal; the placeholder carries the amount, `references/project-context.md` `## Currency display`). `AppCurrency.format` gives "120 €" and "1250,5 €" with a **no-break space** (U+00A0) between number and symbol; the locale's own standard form is "120,00 €". Compact: the app prints "1,25k €" / "1,25m €" / "1,25b €" where the locale does not abbreviate thousands at all ("1250 €" plain) and writes millions as "1,25 Mln €" (Identity, engineering flags). Never write a currency name into copy.
- **Percent** is glued after the number with no space: "40%", "100%" (U+0025).
- **Time** is 24-hour (`Locale.hourCycle` h23, `DateFormatter` template `j` -> `HH`): "09:41", "23:59"; the shortened form drops the leading zero ("9:41"). The twelve `en` clock literals (`12AM`, `06PM` and kin on `insights.appUsage.{axisMidnight,axisNoon,axisSixAm,axisSixPm}`, `profile.appUsage.axis.*`, `screenTimeReport.{axisMidnight,axisNoon,axisSixAM,axisSixPM}`) become "00:00", "06:00", "12:00", "18:00" — which is also why "mezzanotte" should never appear in Italian chart copy (Money frame, widgets bans).
- **Dates**: "31 dic 2026", "31 dicembre 2026", "12 mag" / "12 maggio", "martedì 12 mag" / "martedì". Month and weekday names are lower case in Italian, mid-sentence and at the start of a label alike (Apple's own runtime confirms this: "martedì", "31 dicembre 2026").
- **Ranges** take an en dash without spaces, as `en` already does in two of three cases: "1–280 caratteri" (`onboarding.personalGoal.validation`), "5–120 minuti" (`widgets.startFocus.description`); `onboarding.deviceUsage.oneToThreeHours` "1-3 hours" normalises to "1–3 ore".
- **Dashes and marks**: the two em dashes (`achievements.streak.comebackBody`, `share.alert.savedToPhotosMessage`) stay em dashes with spaces; the quote attribution `quote.display.attribution` keeps its en dash "– %@"; the five spaced-hyphen author decorations keep the hyphen the source uses; the interpunct "·" is preserved in the eight middle-dot keys; ellipsis follows the source's own split (2 keys use "…", 9 use "...") until an owner call.
- **Quotation marks.** Foundation reports `«` / `»` (U+00AB/U+00BB) as `it_IT`'s primary delimiter and curly `"` / `"` (U+201C/U+201D) as the alternate, but Apple's own Italian UI never reaches for the guillemets in this harvest: every quoted feature name and quoted UI label uses the curly form — "%@ vorrebbe accedere a "Tempo di utilizzo"" (`Applications/FamilyControlsAuthenticationUI.app | Localizable.strings | NOTIFICATION_AUTH_REQUEST_TITLE`), "Se consenti a %@ di accedere a "Tempo di utilizzo"…" (`…MESSAGE_INDIVIDUAL`). The evidence is one-sided enough to decide rather than leave open (Decided conflicts): a string that mirrors an iOS label or mock alert uses the curly form, matching Apple's own habit; the quote-card wrapper around a whole displayed quotation (`moneyfesting.details.quoteWrapper`, `notifications.contentExtension.quoteDecoration`, `moneyfesting.main.quotePlaceholder`, `theme.createTheme.sampleQuote`, `widgets.quotes.placeholderText` — 5 of the 11 `en` keys using a straight double quote) takes the locale's own reported primary, the guillemets, following the traditional Italian convention for a displayed quotation; the remaining keys quoting a UI label inline (`component.errorView.message`, `onboarding.dataMigration.errorMessage`, `theme.picker.errorMessage`, `onboarding.alert.screenTimeMessage`, `focusTask.picker.emptyMessage`) take the curly form as a mirror.
- **Plurals: `one`, `many`, `other`, but `many` is reachable only at 1,000,000 or more.** Below a million, Italian behaves exactly like `en`: `one` selects on the integer 1 alone; `other` selects on 0, on every integer from 2 up to 999,999, and on every fraction tested (0.5, 1.5, 2.5). `many` is selected only by 1000000, 2000000, 10000000 and 100000000 in the tested set — 1000001 and 1500000 both fall back to `other` (locale-formats `plural.large_integers`). Boundary integers to exercise: **0, 1, 2, 11, 20, 21, 100, 101, 120, 1 000 000**. Surprises worth a test row: 0 is `other`, not `one`; 999999 is `other` while exactly 1000000 is `many`, and 1000001 flips straight back to `other`. Xcode 26.1's own plural-variant picker does not list `many` for `it` (`variationConfigFile.plist`), but a hand-added `many` variant still compiles and is selected at runtime; omitting it is not a defect, because `many` is optional here (`recommended_required`: `one`, `other`; `recommended_optional`: `many`) and the catalog's plural-form structure only requires `other`.
- **How numerals attach to nouns.** The numeral stands before the bare noun with an ordinary space and no linking word: "1 giorno", "3 giorni", "%lld minuti". There is no Romanian-style partitive, so a single `%lld` never forces a plural rewrite.
- Six keys are a single `en` `stringUnit` but need Italian `one`/`other` branches, because the count is inside the sentence: `achievements.streak.body`, `.comebackBody`, `.headline`, `.milestoneBody`, `.nextMilestoneBody`, `.weekAccessibility`.

**Verbatim rule.** A string that quotes an iOS label or mocks an iOS alert equals Apple's Italian value exactly — the mock alert bodies and buttons (`onboarding.screenTimePermission.{alertTitle,alertMessage,alertDoNotAllow}`, `onboarding.notificationPermission.{alertTitle,alertMessage,alertAllow,alertDoNotAllow}`), the `onboarding.widgets.howTo.*` mirror labels, and the steps that quote them (`step2`, `step5`). Curly quotation marks travel with the value (Numbers, dates, currency and punctuation). App-owned copy keeps `tu` and uses Apple's app and feature names; deviate only with a recorded reason. In Italian the two registers coincide, so the mirrors do not read as a seam.

Fixed names for the translator prompt: the brand row above, the Apple terms in this table, quote authors, and the restricted-apps group literal "No Restricted Apps" (`ToDoTaskRepository.swift:333`, unlocalized — flag it, never translate it in place). See `references/voice-card.md` `## Brand and fixed names`.

## Length trigger

- **1.35×.** An Italian string longer than 1.35 times its English length goes on the render list; `scripts/catalog_check.py` C5 puts it there, and it is never a finding by itself. The ratio is calibrated from Apple's own Italian translations to keep the same rendered proportion as the `ro` calibration: across 78,133 unique English/Italian pairs in the iOS 26.1 runtime, 31.26% exceed 1.35× (length-stats `recommendation`), the ratio closest to Apple's own `ro` distribution exceeding 1.35× at 31.6% (the Apple statistic closest to the `ro` catalog's 299 of 1135).
- Where it bites: median ratio 1.2 overall, but 1-10-character English strings expand hardest (median 1.2, p90 2.111, 38.4% over 1.35×), and 41-120-character strings gentlest (median 1.176, 18.5% over 1.35×). All-caps strings of 1-10 characters run close behind the general short-string rate (35.75% over 1.35×, p90 2.25) rather than uniquely ahead of it — but eyebrows and widget captions live exactly in that length band, so they are still the practical hotspot (`insights.mood.mood` "MOOD", `widgets.focusRing.row.money` "TIME VALUE, 30 DAYS").
- No catalog share can be quoted for `it`: there are 0 Italian units at the snapshot. Re-state this line with a real share after the first wave.
- Counts never establish fit. Verdicts come from render evidence against each view's own floor and line limit (`references/surface-budgets.md`, `references/render-evidence.md`). Shortening cuts words, never facts; accessibility strings are exempt.

## Decided conflicts

| Conflict | Winner | Rule |
|---|---|---|
| Address: `Lei` versus `tu` | `tu` throughout, mocks included | Apple's Italian iOS uses `tu` in every register-bearing string harvested and has no `Lei` form. Precedence line: Apple's Italian wording for anything mirroring iOS |
| Button form: `tu` imperative versus infinitive | `tu` imperative for the affirmative ("Annulla", "Salva", "Salta"), infinitive only after "non" ("Non consentire") | Apple's runtime uses the imperative for every affirmative button and menu item, without exception, and the infinitive only where Italian grammar requires it (a negated `tu` imperative has no synthetic form) |
| Case: Title Case versus sentence case on app-owned copy | sentence case | Apple's Italian is sentence case throughout; Title Case is an English convention, and 114 `en` keys carrying it do not carry it into Italian |
| Quote marks: guillemets versus curly doubles for a mirrored iOS label | curly `"` `"` for any string that quotes or mocks an iOS label; guillemets `«` `»` for the quote-card's own displayed quotation | Apple's own Italian UI consistently uses curly doubles around a quoted feature name, never the guillemets Foundation reports as `it_IT`'s primary delimiter, so the mirror rule wins for that specific case; the guillemets remain the locale's own choice for an extended displayed quotation, the traditional Italian use for the mark |
| "Discard Changes": "Annulla modifiche" versus "Elimina modifiche" | "Annulla modifiche" | Apple's own Italian splits between the two (`WorkflowUI.framework` and `NanoTimeKit.framework | NTKCustomization.strings | PHOTOS_CROP_CANCEL` say "Annulla modifiche"; `NTKParmesanFaceBundleCompanion.bundle` says "Elimina modifiche"); "Annulla modifiche" stays in the Cancel family and never reads as a deletion of the object itself |
| Four-digit grouping: bare versus grouped | bare ("2000", not "2.000") | Foundation's own `it_IT` output groups only from five digits up; a literal number in copy follows the same rule |
| Compact money suffix `k`/`m`/`b` against the locale's plain thousands and `Mln`/`Mld` | engineering finding, not a translation choice | `AppCurrency.formatCompact` hardcodes the Latin suffix; severity medium because the locale does not abbreviate thousands at all (`compact_finding`) |
| Badge and freeze participles: "guadagnato" versus "ottenuto"/"ricevuto" | "ottenuto"/"ricevuto"/"conquistato" | Apple's own Italian avoids the money-earn verb for a medal too ("hai ricevuto la medaglia", "aggiudicarti questa medaglia"), and keeping `guadagn` fully bannable outside the five fixed disclaimers is simpler than carving an exception into the grep rule |
| `Add Widget`: one key or two | one key | iOS 26.1 Italian labels the edit-menu item and the gallery button identically ("Aggiungi widget"), so the `ro`-style split does not arise |

## Open decisions

Each item is mirrored in `glossary/it.json` `open` with the fields `scripts/term_audit.py` reads. Nothing here is translated yet, so "meanwhile" means what the first batches do, not which shipped keys stay locked.

- **`streak_term` — the noun for a streak.** Affects every noun slot: `widgets.streak.smallHeader` "Streak", `.currentStreakHeader` "Current streak", `.displayName` "Focus Streak", `.daysCount`, `.dayStreakCount`, `insights.summary.dayStreakTitle` "DAY STREAK", `share.card.streakEyebrow`, `achievements.streak.title`, `onboarding.welcome.slide.streak.{eyebrow,headline}`, `paywall.benefit.streak.title`, the whole `streakFreeze.*` family. Rule: everyday Italian a reader without English understands at first read, and it must fit a one-word widget header and an uppercase caption (Length trigger). Options: **"serie"** — the plain Italian noun, 5 characters, fills every header and takes a compound reading ("serie di giorni"); no Apple evidence for this exact sense. **"giorni consecutivi"** — close to Apple's own Italian for a Move streak ("sarà il tuo settimo giorno consecutivo", `System/Library/Health/Assets/FitnessUIAssets.bundle/ActivityAchievements/PerfectWeekStand/localization | Localizable-Modified-tinker.stringsdict | ACHIEVEMENT_UNACHIEVED_PROGRESS_ONE_AWAY_DESC/NSStringLocalizedFormatKey`), but it is a phrase, not a noun: it cannot fill "Streak" as a header and is long uppercased. **"streak"** — the English loan several Italian apps use; ruled out by the skill's own rule that a reader who knows no English must understand each line. Recommended: **"serie" in noun slots, with "giorni consecutivi"/"di fila" as the phrase in running text**, because Apple never uses a noun here and "serie" is the only plain Italian noun that survives the widget budget. Meanwhile: the first batch writes "serie" in headers and "giorni consecutivi"/"di fila" in sentences and lists every key that touched the term, so a flip is one recorded global change rather than a per-string choice.
- **`badge_conceit` — whether the workplace metaphor travels into Italian.** Keys: the 24 `achievements.badge.*.name` values and the 5 `achievements.category.*.name` values, with their `.earned` lines. Rule: one conceit per locale, decided once and kept across the family, gender-neutral, with plain descriptors left plain (`references/project-context.md` `## Content policy`). Options: **keep it** — Italian has the vocabulary natively, and every name can be built to agree with an object rather than the reader: "Timbrato il cartellino" (Clocked in), "Assunzione" (Hired), "In busta paga" (Salaried), "Nuova assunzione" (Rehired), "Di nuovo in turno" (Back on shift), "Turno doppio" / "Turno lungo" / "Turno completo" (Double/Long/Full shift), "A tempo indeterminato" (Permanent), "Anzianità di servizio" (Tenure), "Il libro paga" (Payroll), "Il turno" (The Shift) — none of these force a masculine/feminine call about the reader; **drop it** — replace the employment names with plain achievement names ("Prima sessione", "Sette giorni di fila", "Cinquanta giornate piene"), which loses the wink but removes any reading of the app as an employer, next to a money frame that already has to say five times that nothing is earned. Recommended: **keep it**, because the conceit is what makes the badge family cohere in `en` and Italian carries it with ordinary, gender-safe nouns rather than jargon or a slashed pair; the money frame is protected by the fixed disclaimers and the banned-word table, not by the badge names. Meanwhile: the first achievements batch drafts the conceit with the object-agreeing forms above, marks every name a proposal, and the adjudicator reports the whole family together so a flip is one batch.
- **`reader_gender_default` — the default when a predicate adjective or participle describing the reader cannot be avoided.** Affects the 44 reader-predicate `en` units by the en-inventory count (`you_are` 5, `are_you` 5, `you_showed_up` 1, `welcome_back` 1, `ready` 7, `focused` 19, `done` 15) — for example `shield.reflection.begin.title` "Begin before you're ready.", and any future line stating the reader is done, focused or ready.
