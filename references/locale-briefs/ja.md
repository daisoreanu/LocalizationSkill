## Audience and register

- A friendly coach in everyday Japanese: short sentences, one idea per line, concrete verbs, no jargon. Apple's own register harvest settles the two registers this app needs and shows they never clash: body text and explanations are **polite teineigo (です/ます)**, and controls are **terse nouns or the plain (dictionary) form of a verb**, never a full です/ます conjugation and never the command mood (命令形). Evidence: "“%@”は通知を送信します。よろしいですか?" (polite です/ます, no pronoun, `UserNotificationsServer.framework | Localizable.strings | USER_NOTIFICATION_PERMISSION_ALERT_TITLE`); "あとでヘルスケアアプリでヘルスケアデータのカテゴリをオンにできます。" (polite できます, subject omitted, `HealthUI.framework | HealthUI-Localizable.strings | AUTHORIZATION_DONT_ALLOW_ALERT_MESSAGE`); the button row for the same alerts is "許可 / 許可しない / 続ける" — a bare noun, a plain negative, and the dictionary form of a verb, none of them です/ます (`UserNotificationsServer.framework | Localizable.strings | PERMISSION_ALERT_ALLOW, PERMISSION_ALERT_DENY`; `FamilyControlsAuthenticationUI.app | Localizable.strings | COMMON_CONTINUE`). This is Apple's own register summary, not a house invention (platform-terms 2026-09-16 harvest, `## Register summary`).
- Reader without English: a reader who knows no English must understand each line at first read, which rules out an English loanword where a plain Japanese word already exists (`トラック` for "track", `モニター` for "monitor") even though most Japanese phone users would recognise them. `タスク` (task) is the one exception this brief keeps as a loanword rather than fighting it: it is fully naturalised in Japanese productivity apps (Things, Todoist JP and kin all say `タスク`), unlike `ストリーク`/`フォーカス`, which still read as translated (Open decisions `streak_term`; `references/locale-decisions.md` "Japanese/Korean: … Prefer a familiar local term to an automatic phonetic loanword").
- Imported shapes the editor flags. ## Address and gender

- **Teineigo (です/ます) body text; controls stay terse nouns or dictionary-form verbs; no keigo/sonkeigo, no casual だ/である outside a control label.** This is not a house invention but Apple's own split (Audience and register), and unlike `ro`'s tu/dvs. mismatch the two registers never collide in `ja`: nothing here reads as translated because Apple's own iOS uses the identical split.
- **Pronoun drop is the default, not a style choice.** Across the whole register-evidence sample Apple's own Japanese uses あなた exactly once, and only where the sentence needs to distinguish "you" from a third party in the same message: "“%1$@”でスクリーンタイムにアクセスできるようにすると、“%1$@”で**あなたの**アクティビティデータを表示したり…" (the individual-request alert) against the child-request variant, which names お子様 (the child) instead of stacking a second あなた (`FamilyControlsAuthenticationUI.app | Localizable.strings | NOTIFICATION_AUTH_REQUEST_MESSAGE_INDIVIDUAL` vs `…_MESSAGE`). Rule for `ja`: never write あなたの as a reflex translation of English "your" — state the noun and let context supply the owner ("your focused time" -> 集中した時間, no possessive marker), and reach for あなた(の) only where two parties in the same sentence would otherwise be ambiguous.
- The app speaks about itself in the first person plural in 19 `en` units ("We couldn't load the data.", "We covered %@ with a streak freeze."). Japanese has no equivalent reflex: state the result directly with no subject at all — "データを読み込めませんでした。", never "私たちはデータを読み込めませんでした。" — which is simpler than the source, not harder.
- **Gender.** Japanese predicates carry zero grammatical gender: verbs, adjectives and copulas do not inflect for the subject's gender at all, so every reader-predicate `en` unit that forces a choice in Romance locales (`you_are` 5, `are_you` 5, `you_showed_up` 1, `welcome_back` 1, `ready` 7, `focused` 19, `done` 15 — 44 units total) needs no gender strategy whatsoever in `ja`. The same is true of role nouns: 出勤 (clocked in), 復帰 (came back), 常勤 (salaried-style) and every other agent-noun candidate for the badge family carry no masculine/feminine form to begin with, so the badge-name question in `ja` is purely about whether the workplace conceit fits Japanese gamification convention (Open decisions `badge_conceit`), never about a gendered ending.
- `user.gender.*` is a data field, not voice: `user.gender.female` "Female", `.male` "Male", `.other` "Other", `.preferNotToSay` "Prefer not to say" — seed 2026-09-16: "女性", "男性", "その他", "回答しない". The stored profile gender (`Manifesting/Features/User/Domain/Entities/Gender.swift`) is not a copy input without a product decision.
- Grep aid, not a validator: `貴殿|貴方様` (archaic/over-formal address, never appropriate for a coach app), `あなたの` (flag every hit as a possible unnecessary calque per the rule above), `私たちは|我々は` (the first-person-plural reflex to flag), and a verb ending in `ろ$|なさい$` on a **button label only** (true imperative mood, never on a control — body-text imperative particles inside a sentence are a different, legitimate thing and this stem must not fire there).

## Header and label forms

- **Japanese has no letter case at all** (locale-formats `"case": "no letter case"`). Every English device this catalog uses to carry emphasis or hierarchy through case — ALL CAPS eyebrows and widget rows, Title Case screen titles, sentence case body copy — collapses to the same plain Japanese noun or noun phrase, because there is no orthographic device left to spend: `insights.mood.mood` "MOOD" -> 気分; `widgets.focusRing.title` "TODAY'S FOCUS" -> 今日の集中; `plan.dailyPlan.doneHeader` "DONE" -> 完了 (same word as the Done control, no seam); `onboarding.hourlyWage.title` "Set Your Hourly Wage" (Title Case) -> 時間単価を設定 (plain, per Open decisions `hourly_rate` in the glossary). The 114 Title Case `en` keys and the 34 all-caps `en` keys therefore need no case rule in `ja` at all — the only real decision left is word choice, covered by the terms in `glossary/ja.json`.
- Emphasis that English spends on case, Japanese spends on typography and layout (weight, size, a leading corner-bracket decoration) — a rendering decision for `references/surface-budgets.md`, not a translation one.
- Tab titles are bare nouns: Moneyfesting, プラン (Plan), 統計 (Insights), プロフィール (Profile) — seed 2026-09-16. `main.tab.insights` "Insights" becomes 統計 (statistics) rather than the loanword インサイト: the tab shows charts and totals, and a reader who knows no English should recognise the word immediately, the same reasoning `nl` used for its own Insights tab.
- Section headers are bare nouns or short noun phrases; unlike Dutch, **no article and no grammatical-gender agreement ever applies**, because Japanese nouns carry no gender and do not inflect for number. The trap `nl`'s `## Header and label forms` documents (a fragment with no visible noun needs the right implied article) simply does not exist in `ja` — dropping the noun from a fragment ("Covered by a freeze." -> "凍結でカバー。" or shorter) stays grammatical either way. The one real checkpoint left is semantic, not grammatical: a dropped noun must not leave it unclear *what* was covered, blocked or completed, which is an editor judgment call, not a rule a stem can catch.
- State and toggle labels ("Blocked" -> 制限中, seed 2026-09-16, no Apple citation found in this domain — the mail-blocking citations harvested (`PreferencesManifests.bundle | SettingsSearchManifest-com.apple.mobilemail.settings.strings | BLOCKED` -> "受信拒否設定") are mail-specific and do not transfer to a Screen-Time app-restriction sense) need no participle agreement either, for the same reason: Japanese predicate adjectives and stative forms (…中, …済み) do not inflect for their subject.

## Short forms and units

**Japanese has no separate narrow/abbreviated/spelled-out unit vocabulary the way English has `m`/`min`/`minutes`.** Foundation's own output for `ja_JP` uses the identical characters 分/時間/日/秒 in every register (locale-formats `durations`): narrow `1時間45分` and `25分`; the Screen Time abbreviated style `1時間 45分` (note the space Foundation inserts here that the narrow style does not — flag whichever call site the app actually uses) and `25分`; the wide accessibility form `25分間`-equivalent is simply `25分` again, unchanged; countdown `1:45:00`, `25:00` (numerals only, unaffected by language). **This removes an entire category of work other locales carry**: the 18 "spelled out accessibility" `en` keys need no separate vocabulary in `ja` — VoiceOver reads the same 分/時間/日/秒 the compact labels already use.

## Numbers, dates, currency and punctuation

All values below are Foundation/ICU output for `ja_JP` (locale-formats, ICU 76.1.0.0 / CLDR 46.0.0.0), or the app's own `AppCurrency` path on that locale.

- **Numbers.** Grouping is identical to `en`'s Western-Arabic thousands grouping: `2000` -> "2,000", `12000` -> "12,000", `1250.5` -> "1,250.5", `1250000` -> "1,250,000" (grouping separator U+002C COMMA, decimal U+002E FULL STOP). Japan's own base-10,000 counting (万, 億) applies **only** to the compact/abbreviated presentation, never to a plain grouped number — do not conflate the two.
- **Money.** Runtime values arrive formatted through `AppLanguage.effectiveLocale`, so no string hard-codes a currency (`references/project-context.md` `## Currency display`). `AppCurrency.format` gives "￥120" and "￥1,250" for 1250.5 (JPY carries no minor unit, so the .5 is dropped, matching the locale standard "¥120"/"¥1,250"); the app's own value uses the **fullwidth** yen sign U+FFE5 (locale-formats `separator_scalars_1250.5`), which the locale-standard formatter may render with the halfwidth ¥ — visually near-identical but a different codepoint, worth a copy/paste check, not a blocker. Compact: the app prints "￥1.25m" where the locale would print "125万" (Identity engineering flags; Money is never abbreviated in `ja` copy the app doesn't control). Never write a currency name into copy.
- **Percent** is glued after the number with no space: "40%", "100%" (U+0025), identical to `en`. "%lld%%" and "-%lld%%" keep that shape.
- **Time.** Two different conventions apply depending on what the value represents. A full clock reading (hour and minute) stays colon-format and 24-hour, no AM/PM marker: `Locale.hourCycle` is `h23`, and the app's own samples give "9:41", "23:59" (locale-formats `time`). An **hour-only** value — exactly what the four chart-axis keys need — takes Foundation's own natural skeleton for `ja_JP`, `DateFormatter` template `j` -> "H時": the twelve `en` clock literals (`12AM`, `06PM` and kin on `insights.appUsage.{axisMidnight,axisNoon,axisSixAm,axisSixPm}`, `profile.appUsage.axis.*`, `screenTimeReport.{axisMidnight,axisNoon,axisSixAM,axisSixPM}`) become "0時", "6時", "12時", "18時" — kanji 時 suffix, no leading zero ("06時" reads oddly; "6時" is the idiomatic form), not a colon. Confusing the two conventions is the failure mode to watch for.
- **Dates.** "2026年12月31日" for both the abbreviated and the wide form — Japanese numerals plus 年/月/日 need no month abbreviation the way `en` needs "Dec" vs "December", so the two `en` date lengths collapse to one Japanese form. Weekday: "火曜日" (Tuesday) alone; combined weekday+date is "5月12日 火曜日" — **date first, weekday second**, the reverse of `en`'s "Tuesday, May 12". Call the right Foundation date-style API rather than hand-assembling the English word order.
- **Ranges.** Foundation's Foundation-level range output was not captured for `ja` at this snapshot; by established Japanese typographic convention the wave dash 〜 (U+301C, or its fullwidth-tilde look-alike ～ U+FF5E) is the ordinary range mark, not the en dash `en`/`ro`/`nl` use — seed 2026-09-16, flag for confirmation before the pilot. Governs `onboarding.personalGoal.validation` "1–280 characters", `widgets.startFocus.description` "5–120 minutes", `onboarding.deviceUsage.oneToThreeHours` "1-3 hours".
- **Dashes, ellipsis and dots — typographic seeds, 2026-09-16, none Apple-cited:**
  - The two em dashes (`achievements.streak.comebackBody`, `share.alert.savedToPhotosMessage`) have no natural Japanese equivalent in casual UI copy; restructure into two sentences or join with の/、 rather than forcing a dash mark that Japanese running text does not use this way.
  - The source's ellipsis forms (2 keys use "…", 9 use "...") both become the Japanese typesetting convention of a **doubled** ellipsis, "……" (two U+2026 characters): a single … reads visually short against the fullwidth characters around it, which is why Japanese body text and UI copy conventionally double it. Governs `common.editor.saving` "Saving…", `focusTask.picker.loadingTitle` "Loading tasks...", and the other loading-state keys.
  - The middle dot `·` (U+00B7, 8 keys: `paywall.billed.{lastChance,winBack}`, `paywall.noCommitment`, `paywall.plan.annual.subtitle`, `screenTimeReport.{allDaySummary,selectedHourSummary}`) becomes the native katakana middle dot `・` (U+30FB), the ordinary Japanese separator, not the Latin punctuation mark.
  - The bullet `•` (`widgets.tasks.summary`) is a decorative separator understood universally; no locale-specific change needed.
- **Quotation marks.** Foundation reports 「 」 (U+300C/U+300D) as the primary delimiter and 『 』 (U+300E/U+300F) as the alternate for `ja_JP` (locale-formats `quotes`) — use these for every app-owned quoted string (the 11 `en` keys using a straight `"`, the displayed motivational quotes, the quoted retry-button label inside `component.errorView.message`). Apple's own mock-alert strings instead wrap the app name in curly “ ” (U+201C/U+201D) — e.g. "“%@”がスクリーンタイムへのアクセスを求めています" (`FamilyControlsAuthenticationUI.app | Localizable.strings | NOTIFICATION_AUTH_REQUEST_TITLE`) — and the verbatim rule (Platform terms) means any string that mirrors an iOS alert keeps that curly form exactly, never the corner bracket. This is not an open question: two different kinds of strings, two already-decided rules (Decided conflicts).
- **Plurals: one category only, `other`** (ICU and Xcode's `variationConfigFile.plist` agree; every integer 0-999999 and every fraction tested selects `other`). There is no `one` category to build a boundary test around, unlike every European locale in this run — the boundary-value exercise that matters for `ja` is not plural selection but **counter-word choice per noun family** (Short forms and units): a number never attaches to a bare noun without its counter, and the wrong counter (or none) is the failure mode a Japanese reader notices, not a missed plural branch. The six keys that are a single `en` `stringUnit` but split `one`/`few`/`other` in `ro` (`achievements.streak.{body,comebackBody,headline,milestoneBody,nextMilestoneBody,weekAccessibility}`) need **no** additional `ja` branch at all: one `other` form covers every count.
- **"Please" scaffolding.** 15 `en` keys carry "Please" (14 capitalised); unlike `nl`, which drops "Alsjeblieft" outright because it reads as childish, Japanese's equivalent is **not** decorative scaffolding to strip — ください is the ordinary, expected way to phrase a real request or instruction in teineigo, and dropping it from an actual instruction ("Please go to Settings" -> "設定に移動してください") would read as terse or curt, working against the register this brief already chose. Keep ください wherever the sentence is a genuine request or instruction; there is no separate "drop the please" rule for `ja`.

## Length trigger

- **1.20×, measured in display width, not character count.** `scripts/catalog_check.py` C5 puts a Japanese string over this ratio on the render list; the ratio is never a finding by itself. Japanese text is measured with East Asian Wide/Fullwidth characters counted **double**, because that is how they actually render: a kanji or kana glyph occupies roughly twice the horizontal space of a Latin letter at the same point size, so a character-count ratio alone would badly understate the real overflow risk (length-stats `recommendation`, `metric: "display width"`).
- **Japanese text is shorter in character count but often wider on screen than English — this is the trap to watch for.** Across 45,499 unique English/Japanese pairs in the iOS 26.1 runtime, the median **character**-count ratio is only 0.556 (Japanese needs about half the characters English does), which makes a translated string *look* like an easy fit; but the median **width** ratio is 1.026, and 18.3% of pairs exceed 1.35× in width even though almost none do in raw character count. 1.20× in width is the point that matches the Romanian calibration (Apple's `ro` exceeds 1.35× in 31.6% of pairs, the statistic closest to the `ro` catalog's 299 of 1135); Apple's Japanese exceeds 1.20× in width in 28.6% of pairs, while a flat 1.35× on width would flag only 16.4%.
- Where it bites hardest: short strings and all-caps captions. English strings of 1-10 characters have a median **width** ratio of 1.0 but 31.8% exceed 1.35× and 4.0% exceed 2.00×; the all-caps subset of that same bucket is worse still — median width ratio 1.2, 39.65% over 1.35× and 11.14% over 2.00×. These are exactly the eyebrows and widget captions this catalog has plenty of (`insights.mood.mood` "MOOD", `widgets.focusRing.title` "TODAY'S FOCUS", `insights.summary.lastThirtyDaysSubtitle` "LAST 30 DAYS").
- No catalog share can be quoted for `ja`: there are 0 Japanese units at the snapshot. Re-state this line with a real share after the first wave.
- Counts never establish fit. Verdicts come from render evidence against each view's own floor and line limit (`references/surface-budgets.md`, `references/render-evidence.md`). Shortening cuts words, never facts; accessibility strings are exempt.

## Decided conflicts

| Conflict | Winner | Rule |
|---|---|---|
| Register: teineigo throughout, badge/achievement lines included, versus a punchier casual だ/た-form for short celebratory lines | teineigo everywhere | Apple's own Health achievement copy stays polite even in short exclamatory unlock lines ("…できますよ!"), so the app's badge and celebration copy follows the same register as the rest of the app; no plain-form exception |
| Control label: a bare noun or dictionary-form verb versus a full です/ます conjugation or the command mood | bare noun / dictionary form | Apple's own runtime uses exactly this shape on every button harvested (許可, 保存, 編集, 削除, 続ける, 閉じる); a masu-form or imperative button would read as translated |
| Quotation marks: native corner brackets 「」/『』 versus Apple's own curly “ ” around a quoted app or feature name | both — split by string type, not an owner call | Foundation reports 「」/『』 as `ja_JP`'s own delimiters, which app-owned quoted copy uses; Apple's mock-alert strings themselves consistently wrap the app name in curly “ ”, and the verbatim rule (Platform terms) means any string mirroring an iOS alert keeps that form |
| Length signal: character count versus display width | display width | Japanese text is shorter than English by character count (median ratio 0.556) but renders wider per character (kanji/kana are double-width), so a character-count trigger would systematically understate real overflow; the 1.20× trigger is calibrated on width specifically (Length trigger) |
| Counted nouns: a bare number attached to a noun versus a number plus counter word | counter word required | Japanese numerals cannot attach to a noun the way English "%lld apps" or Romanian "%lld de aplicații" can; every counted-noun family in Short forms and units needs its own counter, and there is only one plural category (`other`) to build a form around, not several |
| Compact money suffix `k`/`m`/`b` against the locale's own `万`/`億` groupings | engineering finding, not a translation choice, and higher severity than the `en`/`nl` case | `AppCurrency.formatCompact` hardcodes the Latin suffix; severity **high**, `reads_wrong_to_local_reader: true` (`compact_finding`), because `万`/`億` are not thousands/millions/billions at all, unlike `nl`'s case/first-letter mismatch |
| "Please" scaffolding: strip it (as `nl` does with "Alsjeblieft") versus keep ください on genuine requests | keep ください | Unlike Dutch "Alsjeblieft", ください is the ordinary, unmarked way to phrase a real request or instruction in teineigo; dropping it from an actual instruction would read as curt, working against the register this brief already chose (Numbers, dates, currency and punctuation) |

## Open decisions

Each item is mirrored in `glossary/ja.json` `open` with the fields `scripts/term_audit.py` reads. Nothing here is translated yet, so "meanwhile" means what the first batches do, not which shipped keys stay locked.

- **`streak_term` — the noun for a streak.** Affects every noun slot: `widgets.streak.smallHeader` "Streak", `.currentStreakHeader` "Current streak", `.displayName` "Focus Streak", `.daysCount`, `.dayStreakCount`, `insights.summary.dayStreakTitle` "DAY STREAK", `share.card.streakEyebrow`, `achievements.streak.title`, `onboarding.welcome.slide.streak.{eyebrow,headline}`, `paywall.benefit.streak.title`, the whole `streakFreeze.*` family. Rule: everyday Japanese a reader without English understands at first read, and it must fill a one-word widget header the way English "Streak" does. Options: **"連続記録"** (renzoku kiroku, "consecutive record") — a real compound noun that stands alone in a header ("連続記録: 7日") the way "streak" does in English, built from the same root Apple's own "○日連続" construction uses, but not a form Apple itself ever needed as a bare noun. **"連続"** (renzoku, "consecutive/in a row") alone — the exact word Apple's own Japanese uses for a Move streak, but only as a phrase-forming element ("7日連続"), never as a standalone noun; it cannot fill a bare header slot the way "Streak" does. **"ストリーク"** — the English loanword some Japanese habit apps use; ruled out by the rule that a reader who knows no English must understand each line at first read. Recommended: **"連続記録" in noun slots, with "連続" as the phrase-forming element in running text ("7日連続")**, because Apple never needed a bare noun here and "連続記録" is the only candidate that is both plain Japanese and fills a header. Meanwhile: the first batch writes "連続記録" in headers and "○日連続" in sentences and lists every key that touched the term, so a flip is one recorded global change, not a per-string choice.
- **`badge_conceit` — whether the workplace metaphor (雇用・シフト・給料 vocabulary) travels into Japanese.** Keys: the 24 `achievements.badge.*.name` values and the 5 `achievements.category.*.name` values, with their `.earned` lines. Rule: one conceit per locale, decided once and kept across the family (`references/project-context.md` `## Content policy`); Japanese needs no gendered-form check either way, since Japanese role nouns carry no gender to begin with (Address and gender). Options: **keep it** — Japanese has softened workplace vocabulary available (皆勤 "perfect attendance", 常勤 "regular/full-time status", 勤続 "years of service") that carries some of the same wink without the heavier 正社員/給料 register; **drop it** — replace the employment names with plain, literal, criterion-stating names ("60分集中", "1年連続", "10日達成"), the shape Japanese mobile-game and habit-app badges already use. Recommended: **drop it**, because Japanese achievement systems conventionally state the criterion plainly rather than personify it as an employment milestone the way English and Dutch both can; an occupational pun here is more likely to read as a translated English joke than as a native Japanese app convention, and the money frame is already protected by the fixed disclaimers and the banned-word table, not by the badge names (Idioms and figurative language carries the literal proposal in full). Meanwhile: the first achievements batch drafts the literal names, marks every one a proposal, and the adjudicator reports the whole family together so a flip is one batch.
- **`get_started_wording` — the onboarding welcome button.** Key: `onboarding.welcome.startButton` "Get started". Rule: the app's own first-time CTA, not a system mirror, so Apple's own inconsistency across contexts (below) is evidence of variation, not a single answer. Options: **"始める"** (invites the reader into the experience, a warm first-person-adjacent verb commonly used to open an onboarding flow); **"開始"** (terser noun, the more frequent of Apple's own two Health renderings: `System/Library/Health/FeedItemPlugins/MenstrualCyclesAppPlugin.healthplugin | Localizable-Pregnancy.strings | MENSTRUAL_CYCLES_ONBOARDING_SETUP_BUTTON_TITLE` "Get Started" -> "開始"); **"使用開始"** (Apple's alternate rendering for the same English string in a different onboarding flow: `System/Library/Health/FeedItemPlugins/Heart.healthplugin | AFibBurden-Localizable.strings | ONBOARDING_START_CONTINUE_BUTTON_TITLE` "Get Started" -> "使用開始"). Recommended: **"始める"**, because this button greets a brand-new user rather than mirroring a system control, and the warmer verb fits a first-time welcome screen better than the terser noun Apple reaches for on its own settings-style onboarding rows. Meanwhile: draft "始める" and record the key so a flip touches only this one control.
