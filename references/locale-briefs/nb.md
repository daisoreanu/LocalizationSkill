## Identity

- Tag `nb`, bare. iOS ships Norwegian UI text as `no.lproj` (24 of 26 UI bundles checked; absent only in `UIKit.framework` and `ScreenTime.framework`), never as `nb.lproj`: the runtime-wide scan found only 4 stray `nb.lproj` folders, all outside the UI (`ProofReader.framework`, two `SoftwareLicenseAgreements` bundles, `MultipickerStrings/iPhone.bundle`), and 0 with both `nb` and `no`. `xcstringstool compile` on a catalog entry `no` writes `nb.lproj` (`LOCLocaleCode.plist`: `no` has displayName "Norwegian", lproj `no`; there is no `nb` entry at all), and Foundation canonicalises `no` to `nb`, so `nb` is the only tag Xcode keeps for a catalog language; a catalog holding both `nb` and `no` collides in one `nb.lproj` folder whose value differs between compiles (2 of 8 runs disagreed per key order at harvest time). `nb-NO`, `nn-NO` and `no-NO` preference lists all negotiate to `nb` once the catalog carries `[en, ro, nb]` (`apple-coverage.json` `app_negotiation`, `wave2_app_result`). Map `nb` to `no.lproj` for every Apple-wording citation in this brief and to App Store Connect's `no` for the storefront.
- Audience: Bokmål-writing Norwegian speakers on a Norwegian-language iPhone, primarily in Norway. Latin script, left to right, letters æ ø å. Format locale `nb_NO`, audience currency NOK; the app's own `CurrencyCatalog` carries NOK with `NO` among its region codes and host ICU's default for `NO` is also NOK, so there is no currency gap.
- Snapshot 2026-09-16 against catalog commit `c8a49dd`: 0 `nb` units of 1189 keys (1187 at `c8a49dd`); the catalog carries `en` (1142) and `ro` (1135) only.
- Precedence: app `CLAUDE.md` first; then Apple's Norwegian wording for anything that mirrors or names an iOS surface (`## Platform terms`); then this brief. `glossary/nb.json` records the outcomes.
- Every row here is agent research: no shipped Norwegian copy, no native speaker, no owner and no certified translator has reviewed any line in this brief or in `glossary/nb.json`. Treat each as a seed the owner may flip; a native read of `## Control forms`, `## Money frame` and `## Platform terms` is recommended before the pilot batch, and the first batch's `examples-nb.md` is written from that batch, not seeded here.
- Engineering flags that block a Norwegian ship, all to report rather than fix: `AppLanguage.supportedLanguageCodes` is `["en", "ro"]` (`Manifesting/Foundation/Localization/AppLanguage.swift:6`), so the in-app language override ignores `nb`; `knownRegions` is `(en, ro, Base)` (`Manifesting.xcodeproj/project.pbxproj:6891-6895`), and `nb` must also be added to `Localizable.xcstrings` and `InfoPlist.xcstrings`; `CFBundleLocalizations` in `Manifesting/ScreenTimeShieldConfigurationExtension/Info.plist` is `(en, ro)`; the 25 shield pairs are per-language struct fields, `titleEnglish`, `titleRomanian`, `bodyEnglish`, `bodyRomanian` (`Manifesting/Foundation/ShieldSupport/ShieldMessage.swift:14-17`) selected by `languageCode == "ro"` (`:20`, `:24`; also `Manifesting/Foundation/ShieldSupport/ShieldPresentationCopy.swift:9`), so a third language is a struct change and `scripts/catalog_check.py` `SHIELD_TARGET_FIELD` and `scripts/term_audit.py` can see only `en` and `ro` shield text until it lands. App Store Connect lists this language as Norwegian (`no`), not `nb`; the Norway storefront defaults to English (U.K.), with Norwegian only an additional language there (`apple-coverage.json` `app_store_connect`, `storefront_rows`), a listing decision separate from in-app copy. No currency gap (above). One formatting defect: `AppCurrency.formatCompact` appends a hardcoded Latin `k`/`m`/`b` (`Manifesting/Foundation/Localization/AppCurrency.swift:53-56`), so Norwegian renders "1,25k NOK" and "1,25m NOK", while `nb_NO`'s own compact notation gives "1,25k" for thousands (a match) but "1,25 mill." for millions (`## Numbers, dates, currency and punctuation`; open decision `compact_currency_suffix`).

## Audience and register

- A dry, matter-of-fact coach in everyday Bokmål: short main clauses, one idea per line, concrete verbs, no imported English scaffolding. Apple's Norwegian is the register floor and it stays plain and direct even where the English source is formal or passive: en's "These can be configured in Settings." keeps a Norwegian `-s` passive that reads natively rather than translated ("Disse kan konfigureres i Innstillinger.", iOS 26.1 (23B86) runtime: `System/Library/PrivateFrameworks/UserNotificationsServer.framework | Localizable.strings | USER_NOTIFICATION_PERMISSION_ALERT_BODY`) — unlike Finnish, the `-s` passive is native Norwegian, not an anglicism to smooth into an active voice, so this brief does not carry a "convert passive to active" rule the way the Finnish brief does.
- Norwegian drops English's "Please" scaffolding and keeps the bare imperative or a native idiom instead of translating the word: "Please ensure your device is connected to the internet and try again." becomes "Kontroller at enheten er koblet til internett, og prøv igjen." (no "Vennligst"), and "Please Wait…" becomes the idiom "Et øyeblikk…", not "Vennligst vent…" (iOS 26.1 (23B86) runtime: `System/Library/Health/FeedItemPlugins/Profiles.healthplugin | Localizable-Tinker.strings | stop_receiving_shared_profile_error_alert_message`; `System/Library/CoreServices/SpringBoard.app | SpringBoard.strings | PLEASE_WAIT`). 15 `en` keys carry "Please" (`currency.saveFailed`, `common.editor.saveFailedMessage`, `common.editor.deleteFailedMessage`, `focusTask.selection.failedMessage`, `moneyfesting.details.finalizeFailureAlertMessage` among them), all 14 capitalised mid-sentence.
- Norwegian drops the serial (Oxford) comma before "og" ("and") in a three-item list: en "alerts, sounds, and icon badges" becomes "varsler, lyder og symbolmerker", no comma before "og" (iOS 26.1 (23B86) runtime: `System/Library/PrivateFrameworks/UserNotificationsServer.framework | Localizable.strings | USER_NOTIFICATION_PERMISSION_ALERT_BODY`). 8 `en` keys carry a comma directly before "and" (`onboarding.notificationPermission.alertMessage`, `onboarding.screenTimePermission.alertMessage`, `insights.mood.reflectionPrompt`, `achievements.badge.rehired.earned`, `achievements.category.comeback.description`, `moneyfesting.details.stopConfirmationMessage` among them).
- Imported shapes the editor flags. Every target example is an agent-authored illustration, not shipped copy.

## Address and gender

- `du` throughout app-owned copy: second-person singular verbs, and the possessive determiners `din`/`ditt`/`dine` where ownership is not already obvious, never a polite form. Bokmål orthography also permits an optional feminine `di` (a three-gender system), but this brief standardizes on the plain, modern two-gender system most consumer software uses: `din` for every non-neuter noun, `ditt` for neuter, `dine` for plural, and no `di` anywhere in app-owned copy, matching the dry, matter-of-fact register this section calls for — this keeps a feminine noun such as "uke" (week) from shipping as "di uke" in one string and "din uke" in another. Apple's Norwegian does the same on the exact screens this app mirrors: "%@ vil sende deg varslinger" (`System/Library/PrivateFrameworks/UserNotificationsServer.framework | Localizable.strings | USER_NOTIFICATION_PERMISSION_ALERT_TITLE`), "Du kan slå på helsedatakategorier senere i Helse-appen." (`System/Library/PrivateFrameworks/HealthUI.framework | HealthUI-Localizable.strings | AUTHORIZATION_DONT_ALLOW_ALERT_MESSAGE`), "Bli varslet når ukesrapporten er klar. ... hvor mye tid du har brukt på enhetene dine ..." (`System/Library/PrivateFrameworks/ScreenTimeSettingsUI.framework | Localizable.strings | WeeklyReportNotificationGroupSpecifierFooterText`). This is not an open decision: Norwegian consumer software has one register here, and Apple, the system alerts the app mocks, and the app's own voice all sit in it.
- Controls are second-person singular imperatives, including the negative imperative: "Tillat" / "Ikke tillat" / "Fortsett" (`System/Library/PrivateFrameworks/UserNotificationsServer.framework | Localizable.strings | PERMISSION_ALERT_ALLOW`, `PERMISSION_ALERT_DENY`; `Applications/FamilyControlsAuthenticationUI.app | Localizable.strings | COMMON_CONTINUE`).
- The app speaks about itself in the first person plural on 19 `en` keys ("We couldn't load the data.", `component.errorView.message`; "we'll use %@", `onboarding.hourlyWage.footer`; "We covered %@ with a streak freeze.", `streakFreeze.usedBody`). Norwegian keeps the actor as a plural "vi", the same shape Apple itself uses when its own copy speaks as the company ("Vi bruker denne informasjonen ..."; general iOS privacy-sheet register, not this app's own screens): "Vi kunne ikke laste inn dataene.", "bruker vi %@", "Vi dekket %@ med en frys." Do not turn these into a passive that hides who acted.
- Gender: Norwegian nouns carry grammatical gender (masculine, feminine, neuter, with masculine and feminine widely merged into one common gender in ordinary Bokmål), but the second-person pronoun `du` carries none, and a predicate adjective referring to `du` always takes the base, uninflected form, never the neuter `-t` form. Apple's own Norwegian confirms this on exactly the pattern this app needs: "You're Ready to Go" -> "Du er klar!", not "klart" (`System/Library/PrivateFrameworks/TextToSpeechVoiceBankingUI.framework | VoiceBankingUI.strings | VB_READY_TO_RECORD_TITLE`); "Are you sure you want to delete this data?" -> "Er du sikker på at du vil slette dataene?", not "sikkert" (`System/Library/Health/FeedItemPlugins/MenstrualCyclesAppPlugin.healthplugin | Localizable.strings | DELETE_FROM_HEALTH_MESSAGE`). Norwegian past-tense verbs do not conjugate for person or gender at all, so most sentences about the reader carry no gender marking of any kind. This means the `en` keys that force a gendered predicate adjective in `ro` need no neutral strategy in `nb`: `achievements.streak.body`, `achievements.badge.fullDaysFifty.earned`, `achievements.badge.hired.name`, `achievements.badge.salaried.name`, `achievements.badge.rehired.name`, `achievements.badge.clockedIn.earned`, `achievements.gauge.title`, `widgets.tasks.allDone`, `focusTask.alert.deleteTask.message`, `onboarding.dailyFocusGoal.subtitle`, `screenTime.createRestrictedList.savedMessage`, `shield.reflection.begin.title`.
- What does need agreement is number and grammatical gender of the noun itself, not any property of the reader: a participle labelling a noun matches that noun's gender and number (a neuter noun such as "et program" takes a neuter participle, "programmet er slettet"; a common-gender noun does not add `-t`), and a fragment with no visible noun needs the noun named rather than a bare participle left to guess its gender (`## Header and label forms`).
- The only reader-gender surface left is the picker itself, four plain nouns: `user.gender.female`, `.male`, `.other`, `.preferNotToSay`. The stored profile gender (`Manifesting/Features/User/Domain/Entities/Gender.swift`) is not a copy input without a product decision.
- Grep aid, not a validator, for a formal register that slipped in: `Dem`, `Deres` (formal "you"/"your"), the imperative plural `-e` ending where the app should be talking to one reader.

## Header and label forms

- Sentence case everywhere in app-owned copy: Norwegian capitalises the first word and proper names only, even inside a multi-word label. Apple's Norwegian does this consistently: "Rediger Hjem-skjerm" (`SpringBoardHome.strings | REARRANGE_ICONS_SHORTCUT_ITEM_TITLE`), "Krev Face ID" (`System/Library/PrivateFrameworks/AppProtectionUI.framework | Localizable.strings | REQUIRE_AUTH_FACEID`), "Automatisk appinstallering" (a compound, not two capitalised words). At this brief's own count (2-4 word `en` strings, content words only, stopwords `a/an/the/of/to/in/on/for/and/or/with/your/you/is/are` excluded), 149 of 518 such strings are Title Case in `en`; each loses its inner capitals in `nb` unless the capitalised word is a proper name from `## Platform terms` ("Edit Focus Task" -> "Rediger fokusoppgave", `focusTask.createTask.screenTitleEdit`).
- Uppercasing Norwegian is safe and lossless: æ ø å keep their marks (there is no dotted/dotless i pair, that is Turkish, and no ß, that is German), so `.textCase(.uppercase)` and `String.uppercased()` need no locale argument for `nb`; the cost of uppercase is width, not correctness (`## Length trigger`). 30 `en` keys have no lower-case letter (`insights.mood.mood`, `insights.appUsage.axisMidnight`, `insights.summary.dayStreakTitle` among them); until `streak_term` closes, the DAY STREAK caption has no fixed noun to uppercase (`## Open decisions`).
- Section headers and toggle/state labels take the past participle agreeing in gender and number with the labelled noun, never with the reader: a neuter noun such as "programmet" takes a neuter participle ending `-et`/`-t`, a common-gender or plural noun does not add it. "Focus paused" -> "Fokus pauset" (no ending needed, "fokus" being neuter but the short adjectival form of "pauset" already ends without a further `-t` in ordinary use); "In progress" -> "Pågår" (`widgets.startFocus.inProgress`, a verb form, not an adjective, so no agreement question arises at all).
- A fragment with no visible noun names the subject rather than standing on a bare participle whose gender the reader must guess: `widgets.streak.copy.frozen.small` "Covered by a freeze." names the day, not a bare adjective (`## Open decisions`, `streak_term`); the packet's `agreement_target` should carry the noun for the editor to check once the freeze and streak terms are settled.
- Tab titles are single nouns (`main.tab.*`): Moneyfesting, Plan, Statistikk, Profil.
- Status pills that describe the action rather than the reader take the participle alone ("Lagret", `common.editor.saved`, matching Apple's own generic-controls register).

## Numbers, dates, currency and punctuation

Foundation and ICU output below is `nb_NO` on the 2026-09-16 harvest (`locale-formats.json`); invisible characters are named.

- Runtime values arrive formatted through `AppLanguage.effectiveLocale` (app `CLAUDE.md:42`), so strings never hard-code an amount or a currency name. Grouping is U+00A0 NO-BREAK SPACE and the decimal mark is a comma: "2 000", "12 000", "1 250,5", "1 250 000". Four-digit numbers are grouped, so "2000" in a literal becomes "2 000" ("2,000 hours of focus", `achievements.badge.wageYear.name` -> "2 000 timer med fokus").
- Money: `AppCurrency.format` prints "120 NOK" and "1 250,5 NOK" with U+00A0 before the code, the ISO code after the number, because the app uses a narrow symbol only for USD/EUR/JPY/GBP and the ISO code otherwise (`Manifesting/Foundation/Localization/AppCurrency.swift:33-36`); the locale's own standard form instead uses the "kr" symbol, "120,00 kr". Compact figures print "1,25k NOK", "1,25m NOK", "1,25b NOK", while `nb_NO`'s own compact tokens are "k" (thousand, a match), "mill." (million) and "mrd." (billion): "1,25k" matches, "1,25 mill." and "1,25 mrd." do not. That is an engineering finding to report, never something to translate around (open decision `compact_currency_suffix`).
- Percent follows the number with U+00A0: "40 %", "100 %". So a badge reads "-56 %", not "-56%".
- Time is 24-hour (`Locale.hourCycle` h23) and the separator stays a colon, matching `en`'s own separator even though the hour cycle differs: "09:41", "23:59" (no AM/PM). The axis labels `insights.appUsage.axisMidnight`, `axisSixAm`, `axisNoon`, `axisSixPm` and their `profile.appUsage.axis.*` and `screenTimeReport.axis*` twins become "00:00" (Foundation renders the day boundary as "00:00" here, not "24:00"), "06:00", "12:00", "18:00". The widget countdown prints "1:45:00" and "25:00", the same shape `en` uses.
- Dates: abbreviated "31. des. 2026"; wide "31. desember 2026"; weekday "tirsdag"; weekday with a numeric day "tirsdag 12. mai" (`locale-formats.json` `nb.dates`).
- Ranges take an en dash with no spaces: "Tankestrek brukes for å angi ytterpunkter i tid og rom, tallområder ... Her skal det ikke være mellomrom på hver side av tankestreken." with the examples "årene 1349–54" and "kapitlene 2–10" (Språkrådet, https://sprakradet.no/godt-og-korrekt-sprak/rettskriving-og-grammatikk/tegn/tankestrek/, fetched 2026-09-16). Governs the three `en` numeric-range keys and `insights.appUsage.*` axis pairs.
- Quotation marks are « » at the ICU default (`locale-formats.json` `nb.quotes`), and Apple's own Norwegian uses exactly that pair in running text: "«%@» vil legge til noe i Bilder." (iOS 26.1 (23B86) runtime: `System/Library/PrivateFrameworks/TCC.framework | Localizable.strings | REQUEST_ACCESS_SERVICE_kTCCServicePhotosAdd`). The 11 `en` keys with a straight `"` take « » (`moneyfesting.details.quoteWrapper` -> "«%@»", `notifications.contentExtension.quoteDecoration`, `moneyfesting.main.quotePlaceholder`, `theme.createTheme.sampleQuote`, `widgets.quotes.placeholderText`); the two mock-alert title keys are governed by `## Open decisions` (`mock_permission_alerts`) instead, because Apple's own mock alerts for this app's exact scenarios drop the quotes around the app name entirely rather than swapping the mark.
- Ellipsis follows the source character per key: 9 `en` keys use three periods and 2 use "…"; do not normalise one into the other. Attribution keeps the source's dash (`quote.display.attribution`), and the multiplication sign × stays × ("Fokustimene dine × timeprisen din", `achievements.category.wages.description`).
- Plural categories are `one` and `other` only (ICU 76.1.0.0 / CLDR 46.0.0.0, and Xcode's `variationConfigFile.plist` `PluralRule.ComplementedPluralKeys.nb` lists the same two, identical to `en`'s own two categories). `one` is selected by the integer 1 and nothing else; `other` covers 0, 2, 3, 4, 11, 21, 100, 101, 1000, every larger integer, and every fraction tested (0.5, 1.5, 2.5). Exercise 0, 1, 2, 11, 21, 101 and 1000, and note the two traps shared with `en`: 0 is `other`, and 1.5 is `other` although 1 is `one`.
- Six `en` keys have no plural variations but need the `one`/`other` split in Norwegian for the same reason `ro` and `fi` split them ("1 minutt" vs "2 minutter", "1 dag" vs "2 dager"): `achievements.streak.body`, `achievements.streak.comebackBody`, `achievements.streak.headline`, `achievements.streak.milestoneBody`, `achievements.streak.nextMilestoneBody`, `achievements.streak.weekAccessibility`. Report them as variation additions, not as prose fixes.
- A composed string that concatenates a number with a fixed noun fragment carries the noun's own plural form even where the surrounding sentence does not change case (Norwegian nouns have no case system beyond a genitive `-s`, unlike Finnish, so this is a smaller risk than in the Finnish brief, but the plural split above still applies): flag a concatenation that hard-codes a singular noun fragment, and propose one merged key with a format string rather than bending the grammar (`references/agent-protocol.md` `## A. Forward translator`).

## Length trigger

- A Norwegian string longer than 1.15× its English length goes on the render list (`scripts/catalog_check.py` C5), not into findings. The ratio is measured from Apple's own Norwegian translations at iOS 26.1 (23B86), 78,363 English/Norwegian pairs from `no.lproj` of which 44,818 are unique. On the unique, unweighted set 1.15× is the ratio, rounded to 0.05, that Apple's `nb` translations exceed as often (30.4%) as Apple's `ro` translations exceed 1.35× (31.6%, the Apple statistic closest to the ro catalog's 299 of 1135); a flat 1.35× would flag only 11.5% of the `nb` pairs (`length-stats.json` `recommendation`).
- Across the full 78,363-pair set the median ratio is 1.044, p75 1.2, p90 1.412, and 12.45% exceed 1.35×. Short strings expand hardest, which is where this app's controls and captions live: 1-10 characters median 1.1, p90 1.667, 21.0% above 1.35×; 11-20 characters median 1.059, p90 1.455; 41-120 characters median 1.029, p90 1.208. The 951 all-caps pairs run about the same (median 1.0, p90 2.0, 22.6% above 1.35×).
- Apple's own pairs, as calibration for a draft: Cancel -> Avbryt 0.83×, Done -> Ferdig 1.5×, Continue -> Fortsett 1.63×, Edit -> Rediger 1.75×, Delete -> Slett 0.83×, Skip -> Hopp over 2.25×, Retry -> Prøv igjen 2.5×, Settings -> Innstillinger 1.86×, Task -> Oppgave 1.75×, Screen Time -> Skjermtid 0.82×, FREE TRIAL -> PRØV GRATIS 1.1×.
- Counts never establish fit; verdicts come from render evidence against each view's own floor and line limit (`references/surface-budgets.md`, `references/render-evidence.md`). Shortening cuts words, never facts; accessibility strings are exempt.

## Open decisions

- Streak term (`streak_term`). Keys: the `streak` concept's whole surface, `widgets.streak.*` (about 39), `share.card.streak*`, `streakFreeze.*` (11), `paywall.benefit.streak.*` (2), `onboarding.welcome.slide.streak.*` (2), `insights.summary.dayStreakTitle`, `achievements.streak.*` (8). Rule: one head noun per concept, decided once and applied everywhere (`glossary/concepts.json` `streak`: consecutive days of enough focus, a fact about the calendar, not praise). Options: "rekke", the shorter everyday word for an unbroken run of countable units; "serie", the closest lexicalized Norwegian precedent for a streak specifically — sports and gaming media use "vinnerserie"/"tapsserie" for winning/losing streaks — so it is a stronger rival than merely naming discrete-item sets; "streak", the bare loanword, increasingly used untranslated in Norwegian tech and gaming contexts. Apple's own Norwegian never names the concept with a noun at all and instead states the count plus "på rad" ("in a row"; iOS 26.1 (23B86) runtime: `System/Library/Health/Assets/FitnessUIAssets.bundle/ActivityAchievements/MonthlyAchievements/localization/challenge/MoveStreak_SMALL_INCREASE | Localizable.stringsdict | ACHIEVEMENT_UNACHIEVED_ALERT_DESCRIPTION/NSStringLocalizedFormatKey`), so none of the three options is Apple-evidenced. Recommended: "rekke", still the shortest and plainest for a widget header, though the owner should weigh "serie"'s sports-media precedent and "streak"'s currency as a loanword before closing this. Meanwhile agents write the days instead of the noun ("%lld dager på rad"), never choose per string, and the adjudicator lists every key that touched the concept; nothing enters `glossary/nb.json` `terms` until the owner closes this.
- Mock permission alerts (`mock_permission_alerts`). Keys: `onboarding.screenTimePermission.alertTitle`, `alertMessage`, `alertDoNotAllow`; `onboarding.notificationPermission.alertTitle`, `alertMessage`, `alertAllow`, `alertDoNotAllow`; the Screen Time mock's Continue is the shared `common.continue`, quoted by `onboarding.alert.screenTimeMessage`. Rule: a mock of an iOS alert mirrors the device verbatim. The `en` source mocks already copy Apple's own English wording quotes and all, but Apple's Norwegian differs in three ways: no quotation marks around the app name, an active `du`-voiced access sentence rather than the `en` mock's imperative shape, and a capitalised "Innstillinger" where the `en` mock lower-cases "settings". Options: Apple's wording verbatim, quotes dropped ("%@ vil ha tilgang til Skjermtid"); the `en` shape translated with quotes kept ("«%@» ønsker å få tilgang til Skjermtid"). Recommended: Apple's wording verbatim, because the same onboarding step raises the real alert moments later and a mock in a shape iOS never uses shows the reader a screen they will not then recognise. Meanwhile the seven keys stay unwritten in Norwegian and findings go to an audit scope the owner names.
- Compact currency suffix (`compact_currency_suffix`). Keys: every surface that renders a compact amount, `widgets.focusRing.row.money`, `widgets.monthlyTimeValue.*`, `insights.savings.*`, `screenTimeReport.*`, and the insights, celebration and session screens (`Manifesting/Foundation/Localization/AppCurrency.swift:53-56`). Rule: a figure the app formats is engineering, not copy; a locale brief reports it and never writes around it. `AppCurrency.formatCompact` appends a hardcoded Latin "k", "m" or "b", so Norwegian renders "1,25k NOK" (a match for thousands) but "1,25m NOK" where `nb_NO`'s own compact notation gives "1,25 mill." for millions and "1,25 mrd." for billions (`locale-formats.json` `nb.money_compact_locale`, `nb.compact_finding`, severity recorded as low because the thousand token already matches). Options: keep "m"/"b" for Norwegian, accepting a low-severity mismatch on millions (and, at amounts this app is unlikely to reach, billions); use the locale's own compact notation, which changes the formatter for every locale, not just `nb`. Recommended: the locale's own notation, because the suffix sits directly against a money figure the money frame already asks readers to read carefully. Meanwhile agents never translate around the suffix, never spell it into copy, and report it once per batch that renders a compact amount; accessibility labels use the full format and are unaffected.
- Restore button (`restore_button`). Key: `paywall.button.restore`, seeded as "Gjenopprett abonnement", app-owned because the StoreKit purchase sheet has no restore control. Rule: app-owned copy defaults to Apple's wording for a matching control; Apple's own iOS 26.1 (23B86) nb runtime keeps two distinct labels for this action (`System/Library/Frameworks/_StoreKit_SwiftUI.framework | Localizable.strings | RESTORE_SUBSCRIPTION_LABEL` "Gjenopprett abonnement"; `RESTORE_PURCHASES_LABEL` "Gjenopprett manglende kjøp"), the identical ambiguity the `ro` brief opens as its own `restore_button` decision rather than settling silently. Options: "Gjenopprett abonnement" (seeded); "Gjenopprett manglende kjøp". Recommended: "Gjenopprett abonnement", because the button restores a subscription (the paywall's plans are annual and weekly) and that is Apple's own label for exactly that action. A yes needs a paywall render check. Meanwhile the key stays at the seeded form and findings go to an audit scope the owner names.
