## Audience and register

- A friendly coach in everyday Dutch: short sentences, one idea per line, concrete verbs, no jargon and no English sentence shapes. A reader who knows no English must understand each line at first read, which rules out the loanwords Dutch tech writing reaches for (`streak`, `tracken`, `reminder`, `goal`) even though a Dutch reader would recognise them.
- Drop the source's scaffolding: 15 `en` keys carry "please" (14 capitalised), and Dutch UI never writes "Alsjeblieft" — `common.editor.deleteFailedMessage` "The deletion couldn't be saved. Please try again." is one sentence plus "Probeer het opnieuw."
- Dutch writes compounds closed. Apple's own Dutch does it everywhere: `Schermtijd`, `Applimieten`, `Apparaatvrije tijd`, `beginscherm`, `bewegingsring`, `activiteitsgegevens` (Platform terms and the lookups cited there). Spaced compounds ("focus sessie", "streak widget") are the single most recognisable mark of a translated Dutch app and the first thing the editor should flag.
- Imported shapes the editor flags. The first two columns are the marker list the coordinator appends to the editor prompt, target text only; the key column stays coordinator-side (`references/agent-protocol.md` `## Packet separation`). **The Dutch examples are agent-authored illustrations, not shipped copy** — no `nl` string exists yet.

## Address and gender

- **`jij`/`je` throughout, never `u`.** Apple's Dutch iOS uses `je` in every register-bearing string harvested: "%@ wil je meldingen sturen" (`UserNotificationsServer.framework | Localizable.strings | USER_NOTIFICATION_PERMISSION_ALERT_TITLE`), "Als je %@ toegang geeft tot 'Schermtijd', kan deze mogelijk je activiteitsgegevens zien…" (`FamilyControlsAuthenticationUI.app | Localizable.strings | NOTIFICATION_AUTH_REQUEST_MESSAGE_INDIVIDUAL`), "Je kunt categorieën voor gezondheidsgegevens later inschakelen via de app Gezondheid." (`HealthUI.framework | HealthUI-Localizable.strings | AUTHORIZATION_DONT_ALLOW_ALERT_MESSAGE`). No `u` form appears anywhere in the harvest, and Apple's Dutch support pages address the reader the same way ("Met Schermtijd zijn er een aantal instellingen … die je kunt gebruiken…", https://support.apple.com/nl-nl/105121, fetched 2026-09-16). Unlike Romanian, the mock alerts therefore need no register switch: iOS Dutch and app-owned Dutch both say `je`.
- Use the unstressed `je` by default and the stressed `jij` only for contrast; the possessive is `je`, `jouw` only under contrast. `u`/`uw` anywhere is a finding.
- The app speaks about itself in the first person plural, as `en` does in 19 units: "We couldn't load the data." -> "We konden de gegevens niet laden." (`component.errorView.message`), "We covered %@ with a streak freeze." (`streakFreeze.usedBody`), "we'll use %@" (`onboarding.hourlyWage.footer`), "We'll try again soon." (`widgets.focusRing.a11y.unavailable`). Keep `we`, not `wij`, outside contrast.
- **Gender.** Dutch does not inflect predicate adjectives or past participles for the reader, so the shapes that force a choice in Romance locales are already neutral here: "Je hebt je gebruikelijke bedtijd gemist" (`SleepHealth.framework | Localizable-SleepDetails-Descriptions.strings | HIGH_DURATION_EARLYBEDTIME_V3_%lld`) works for any reader. The 44 reader-predicate `en` units (`you_are` 5, `are_you` 5, `you_showed_up` 1, `welcome_back` 1, `ready` 7, `focused` 19, `done` 15) need no gender strategy at all.
- The one hotspot is **role nouns**, and it is confined to the badge family: `achievements.badge.hired.name` "Hired", `.salaried.name` "Salaried", `.rehired.name` "Rehired", `.clockedIn.name` "Clocked in", `.backOnShift.name` "Back on shift", `.permanent.name` "Permanent", `.tenure.name` "Tenure", plus the category names `achievements.category.payroll.name` "Payroll" and `.shift.name` "The Shift". Strategy, in this order: (1) a participle or a prepositional phrase instead of an agent noun — "Aangenomen", "In vaste dienst", "Ingeklokt", "Terug in dienst"; (2) where an agent noun is unavoidable, the common-gender `-er` form, which Dutch uses for every reader; (3) never a paired or slashed form ("werknemer/werkneemster"), never the marked feminine `-ster`/`-es`. The conceit itself is an owner call (Open decisions `badge_conceit`).
- `user.gender.*` is a data field, not voice: `user.gender.female` "Female", `.male` "Male", `.other` "Other", `.preferNotToSay` "Prefer not to say" — seed 2026-09-16: "Vrouw", "Man", "Anders", "Zeg ik liever niet"; "Anders" is neutral in Dutch and needs no shipped-masculine exception. The stored profile gender (`Manifesting/Features/User/Domain/Entities/Gender.swift`) is not a copy input without a product decision.
- Grep aid, not a validator: `\bu\b`, `\buw\b`, `\bhij of zij\b`, `\bzijn/haar\b`, and the marked feminines `werkneemster|medewerkster|gebruikster`. Bare `ster` is not a stem: it is the everyday word for star.

## Control forms

Counts are `en` catalog keys whose `en` value is exactly the control word, the same match `scripts/term_audit.py` makes (en-inventory `## Control forms`); there is no `nl` copy to count. Preferred and role forms mirror `glossary/nl.json` `controls` exactly, role names as the glossary spells them, because the script reads the glossary, not this table.

**Local convention: the bare imperative stem.** Apple's Dutch iOS writes every button and menu item as an imperative with the separable particle split off — "Sta toe", "Sta niet toe", "Ga door", "Bewaar", "Wijzig", "Verwijder", "Sluit", "Hervat", "Voeg widget toe", "Pas aan", "Deel app", "Sla over" (the `generic_controls.*` and `home_edit.*` rows in Platform terms). Imperative it is, on app-owned buttons and on the iOS mirrors alike (Decided conflicts).

## Header and label forms

- **Sentence case everywhere in app-owned copy.** Apple's Dutch is sentence case throughout the harvest ("Voeg widget toe", "Wijzig beginscherm", "Beheer abonnementen"), and Dutch orthography capitalises only the first word plus proper names. The 114 Title Case `en` keys become sentence case: "Set Your Hourly Wage" -> "Stel je uurtarief in" (`onboarding.hourlyWage.title`), "Edit Focus Task" -> "Wijzig focustaak" (`focusTask.createTask.screenTitleEdit`), "My Tasks" -> "Mijn taken" (`focusTask.picker.myTasksHeader`). Proper names keep their capitals: Moneyfesting, Schermtijd, Face ID, App Store, Foto's, Gezondheid, Berichten, Beginscherm.
- **Uppercase eyebrows and captions stay uppercase where `en` is** (34 all-caps `en` strings: `insights.mood.mood` "MOOD", `insights.summary.dayStreakTitle` "DAY STREAK", `widgets.focusRing.title` "TODAY'S FOCUS", `plan.dailyPlan.{planned,done,anytime}Header`, `%@ / HOUR`, `/%lld MIN`). Uppercasing rules for Dutch:
  - A word-initial `ij` takes **two capitals**: `"ijsberen".uppercased(with: Locale(identifier: "nl_NL"))` returns `IJSBEREN` on Foundation, host Version 15.7.9 (Build 24G830), and the root locale gives the same; the national language body states the rule for names and for any word at the start of a sentence ("Namen die met een *ij* beginnen, krijgen in feite twee hoofdletters aan het begin", https://onzetaal.nl/taalloket/ijsland-ijsland, fetched 2026-09-16). So an eyebrow typed by hand must carry `IJ`, never `Ij`, and `.textCase(.uppercase)` already produces it.
  - Dutch has no ß and no dotted/dotless `i` pair, so neither the German nor the Turkish uppercase hazard applies. Accented vowels keep their diacritics when uppercased (`ÉÉN`, `GEBRUIKERSINTERFACE` unaffected).
  - Uppercase captions expand hardest here: of Apple's own all-caps Dutch strings of 1-10 English characters, 35.7% exceed 1.35× and 10.3% exceed 2× (length-stats `all_caps`). Prefer the shorter everyday noun over the exact one and file the row when none exists (`references/surface-budgets.md` `## Catalog microcopy templates`).
- Section headers are plural nouns or participles agreeing with the listed items; the three all-caps plan headers take participles ("GEPLAND", "AFGEROND", "ONGEPLAND" — seed 2026-09-16, the call site appends the count).
- Tab titles are bare nouns: Moneyfesting, Plan, Statistieken, Profiel (`main.tab.*`; seed 2026-09-16 — "Inzichten" is the literal for "Insights" but the tab shows charts and totals, so the plainer noun wins; four labels share about 70pt each, `references/surface-budgets.md`).
- Toggles and state labels take a predicative participle, which does not inflect in Dutch: "Bevroren", "Afgerond", "Geblokkeerd" (`Blocked` -> "Geblokkeerd", `PreferencesManifests.bundle \| SettingsSearchManifest-com.apple.mobilemail.settings.strings \| BLOCKED`). This removes the Romanian agreement trap from state labels.
- **The Dutch agreement rule for a fragment with no visible noun is grammatical gender, not number.** Any article, demonstrative or attributive adjective in the fragment depends on whether the implied noun is a `de`-word or a `het`-word, so the packet's `agreement_target` must name it: `de reeks`, `de taak`, `de sessie`, `de lijst`, `de dag`, `de waarde` against `het doel`, `het uurtarief`, `het beginscherm`, `het widget`? no — **`de widget`** (Apple treats it as a `de`-word: "Als je deze widget verwijdert…", `SpringBoardHome.framework \| SpringBoardHome.strings \| REMOVE_WIDGET_BODY_REMOVE_DATA`). Example of the fault the editor checks: `widgets.streak.copy.frozen.small` "Covered by a freeze." beside `.medium` "Yesterday was covered." — the small tile's fragment has no noun, so "Afgedekt." is safe while "Afgedekte dag." would need the right article.

## Numbers, dates, currency and punctuation

All values below are Foundation/ICU output for `nl_NL` (locale-formats, ICU 76.1.0.0 / CLDR 46.0.0.0), or the app's own `AppCurrency` path on that locale.

- **Numbers.** Grouping separator `.` (U+002E), decimal separator `,` (U+002C): `2000` -> "2.000", `12000` -> "12.000", `1250.5` -> "1.250,5", `1250000` -> "1.250.000". Four-digit numbers **are** grouped ("2.000"), so a literal in copy follows suit: `achievements.badge.wageYear.*` "2,000 hours of focus" -> "2.000 uur focus".
- **Money.** Runtime values arrive formatted through `AppLanguage.effectiveLocale`, so no string hard-codes a currency (`en` has no "EUR" or "euro" literal; the placeholder carries the amount, `references/project-context.md` `## Currency display`). `AppCurrency.format` gives "€ 120" and "€ 1.250,5" with a **no-break space** (U+00A0) between symbol and number; the locale's own standard form is "€ 120,00". Compact: the app prints "€ 1,25k" / "€ 1,25m" / "€ 1,25b" where the locale would print "€ 1,25K" / "€ 1,25 mln." (Identity, engineering flags). Never write a currency name into copy.
- **Percent** is glued after the number with no space: "40%", "100%" (U+0025). "%lld%%" and "-%lld%%" keep that shape.
- **Time** is 24-hour (`Locale.hourCycle` h23, `DateFormatter` template `j` -> `HH`): "09:41", "23:59"; the shortened form drops the leading zero ("9:41"). The twelve `en` clock literals (`12AM`, `06PM` and kin on `insights.appUsage.{axisMidnight,axisNoon,axisSixAm,axisSixPm}`, `profile.appUsage.axis.*`, `screenTimeReport.{axisMidnight,axisNoon,axisSixAM,axisSixPM}`) become "00:00", "06:00", "12:00", "18:00" — which is also why "middernacht" should never appear in Dutch chart copy (Money frame, widgets bans).
- **Dates**: "31 dec 2026", "31 december 2026", "12 mei", "dinsdag 12 mei". Month and weekday names are lower case in Dutch, mid-sentence and at the start of a label alike.
- **Ranges** take an en dash without spaces, as `en` already does in two of three cases: "1–280 tekens" (`onboarding.personalGoal.validation`), "5–120 minuten" (`widgets.startFocus.description`), and `onboarding.deviceUsage.oneToThreeHours` "1-3 hours" is normalised to "1–3 uur".
- **Dashes and marks**: the two em dashes (`achievements.streak.comebackBody`, `share.alert.savedToPhotosMessage`) stay em dashes with spaces; the quote attribution `quote.display.attribution` keeps its en dash "– %@"; the five spaced-hyphen author decorations keep the hyphen the source uses; the interpunct "·" is preserved in the eight middle-dot keys; ellipsis follows the source's own split (2 keys use "…", 9 use "...") until an owner call.
- **Quotation marks** are an open item: Foundation reports `‘` (U+2018) and `’` (U+2019) as both the primary and the alternate delimiters for `nl_NL`, while Apple's own Dutch UI wraps feature names in straight single quotes ("Als je %@ toegang geeft tot 'Schermtijd'…"). See Open decisions `quote_marks`; until it closes, use `‘ ’` for a UI label quoted inside a sentence and leave the quote-card wrapper on the recommended option with a note.
- **Plurals: two categories, `one` and `other`** (ICU and Xcode's `variationConfigFile.plist` agree; no optional categories). `one` selects on the integer 1 alone; `other` selects on 0, on every integer from 2 up, and on every fraction tested (0.5, 1.5, 2.5). Boundary integers to exercise: **0, 1, 2, 11, 20, 21, 100, 101, 120, 1 000 000**, plus the fraction 1,5 for the decimal hours line (`widgets.cash.focusedHoursDecimal` "%@ focused hours", which is not a plural key and reads "1,5 uur focus"). The surprises worth a test row: 0 is `other`, and 1,5 is `other` while 1 is `one`.
- **How numerals attach to nouns.** The numeral stands before the bare noun with an ordinary space and no linking word: "1 dag", "3 dagen", "%lld minuten", and Apple writes it the same way ("{{goal_value}} dagen op rij"). There is no Romanian-style partitive, so a single `%lld` never forces a plural rewrite. Apple does insert a no-break space between a numeral and a unit abbreviation ("ten minste 5 km", `FitnessUIAssets.bundle/ActivityAchievements/FiveKilometerWorkout/localization | Localizable.stringsdict | ACHIEVEMENT_UNACHIEVED_DESCRIPTION/NSStringLocalizedFormatKey`); the app's tight widget lines glue the unit instead, which the render decides (Short forms and units).
- Six keys are a single `en` `stringUnit` but need Dutch `one`/`other` branches, because the count is inside the sentence: `achievements.streak.body`, `.comebackBody`, `.headline`, `.milestoneBody`, `.nextMilestoneBody`, `.weekAccessibility`.

**Verbatim rule.** A string that quotes an iOS label or mocks an iOS alert equals Apple's Dutch value exactly — the mock alert bodies and buttons (`onboarding.screenTimePermission.{alertTitle,alertMessage,alertDoNotAllow}`, `onboarding.notificationPermission.{alertTitle,alertMessage,alertAllow,alertDoNotAllow}`), the `onboarding.widgets.howTo.*` mirror labels, and the steps that quote them (`step2`, `step5`). No-break spaces and Apple's own quotation habits travel with the value. App-owned copy keeps `je` and uses Apple's app and feature names; deviate only with a recorded reason. In Dutch the two registers coincide, so the mirrors do not read as a seam.

## Length trigger

- **1.25×.** A Dutch string longer than 1.25 times its English length goes on the render list; `scripts/catalog_check.py` C5 puts it there, and it is never a finding by itself. The ratio is measured from Apple's own Dutch translations: across 44 184 unique English/Dutch pairs in the iOS 26.1 runtime, 33.1% exceed 1.25×, which is the point that matches the Romanian calibration (Apple's `ro` exceeds 1.35× in 31.6% of pairs, the statistic closest to the `ro` catalog's 299 of 1135); a flat 1.35× would flag only 21.5% and would let real Dutch expansion through (length-stats `recommendation`).
- Where it bites: short strings expand most. Median ratio 1.156 overall, but 1.222 for English strings of 1-10 characters with 36.0% over 1.35×, against 1.149 and 12.1% for strings of 41-120 characters. All-caps strings of 1-10 characters are the worst case: 35.7% over 1.35× and 10.3% over 2× — exactly the eyebrows and widget captions (`insights.mood.mood` "MOOD", `widgets.focusRing.row.money` "TIME VALUE, 30 DAYS", `insights.summary.lastThirtyDaysSubtitle` "LAST 30 DAYS").
- No catalog share can be quoted for `nl`: there are 0 Dutch units at the snapshot. Re-state this line with a real share after the first wave.
- Counts never establish fit. Verdicts come from render evidence against each view's own floor and line limit (`references/surface-budgets.md`, `references/render-evidence.md`). Shortening cuts words, never facts; accessibility strings are exempt.

## Decided conflicts

| Conflict | Winner | Rule |
|---|---|---|
| Address: `u` versus `je` | `je` throughout, mocks included | Apple's Dutch iOS uses `je` in every register-bearing string harvested and has no `u` form; Apple's Dutch support pages do the same. Precedence line: Apple's Dutch wording for anything mirroring iOS |
| Button form: imperative stem versus infinitive | imperative stem ("Sta toe", "Bewaar", "Sla over") | Apple's runtime uses the imperative for every button and menu item, and a Dutch UI style guide states the same preference (fetched 2026-09-16). The infinitive stays available only where a separable imperative splits badly, and that is a per-string editor call recorded in the key's comment |
| Compound spelling: "focus sessie" versus "focussessie" | closed compound | Dutch orthography and Apple's own Dutch (`Schermtijd`, `Applimieten`, `beginscherm`, `bewegingsring`) |
| Case: Title Case versus sentence case on app-owned copy | sentence case | Apple's Dutch is sentence case throughout; Title Case is an English convention, and 114 `en` keys carrying it do not carry it into Dutch |
| Discard and Delete would both be "Verwijder" | Apple's wording wins, with the object spelled out ("Verwijder wijzigingen") | Apple's own value for "Discard Changes" (`NanoTimeKit.framework \| NTKCustomization.strings \| PHOTOS_CROP_CANCEL`); the object keeps a discard from reading as a deletion |
| Minute unit: bare "m" versus "min" | "min" for every label and running line; "m" only in the share-card narrow triple | Foundation's Dutch abbreviated minute unit is "min" ("1 uur, 45 min") and its narrow one is "m" ("1 u, 45 m"); a bare "m" beside a number otherwise reads as metres |
| Hours plural after a numeral | "uur" in both branches | Foundation prints "1 uur, 45 min" and Apple's "Hours" label is "Uur"; `share.duration.hoursSpelled` therefore has two identical plural forms |
| `Add Widget`: one key or two | one key | iOS 26.1 Dutch labels the edit-menu item and the gallery button identically ("Voeg widget toe"), so the `ro` split does not arise |
| Compact money suffix `k`/`m`/`b` against the locale's `K`/`mln.`/`mld.` | engineering finding, not a translation choice | `AppCurrency.formatCompact` hardcodes the Latin suffix; severity low (`compact_finding`) |

## Open decisions

Each item is mirrored in `glossary/nl.json` `open` with the fields `scripts/term_audit.py` reads. Nothing here is translated yet, so "meanwhile" means what the first batches do, not which shipped keys stay locked.

- **`streak_term` — the noun for a streak.** Affects every noun slot: `widgets.streak.smallHeader` "Streak", `.currentStreakHeader` "Current streak", `.displayName` "Focus Streak", `.daysCount`, `.dayStreakCount`, `insights.summary.dayStreakTitle` "DAY STREAK", `share.card.streakEyebrow`, `achievements.streak.title`, `onboarding.welcome.slide.streak.{eyebrow,headline}`, `paywall.benefit.streak.title`, the whole `streakFreeze.*` family. Rule: everyday Dutch a reader without English understands at first read, and it must fit a one-word widget header and an uppercase caption (Length trigger). Options: **"reeks"** — the plain Dutch noun, 5 characters, fills every header and takes a compound ("reekswidget", "reeksbevriezing"); no Apple evidence for this sense. **"dagen op rij"** — Apple's own Dutch for a Move streak (`FitnessUIAssets.bundle/…/MoveStreak_SMALL_INCREASE \| Localizable-Modified-tinker.stringsdict \| ACHIEVEMENT_UNACHIEVED_ALERT_DESCRIPTION_NAME`; also "je recente record voor de meeste dagen beweging op rij"), but it is a phrase, not a noun: it cannot fill "Streak" as a header, cannot take "%lld day streak" compactly, and is 12 characters uppercased. **"streak"** — the English loan many Dutch apps use; ruled out by the skill's own rule that a reader who knows no English must understand each line. Recommended: **"reeks" in noun slots, with "dagen op rij" as the phrase in running text**, because Apple never uses a noun here and "reeks" is the only plain Dutch noun that survives the widget budget. Meanwhile: the first batch writes "reeks" in headers and "dagen op rij" in sentences and lists every key that touched the term, so a flip is one recorded global change rather than a per-string choice.
- **`quote_marks` — the marks around a displayed quotation and around a quoted UI label.** Keys: `moneyfesting.details.quoteWrapper` "%@", `notifications.contentExtension.quoteDecoration` "%@", `moneyfesting.main.quotePlaceholder`, `theme.createTheme.sampleQuote`, `widgets.quotes.placeholderText`, plus the UI labels quoted inside `component.errorView.message`, `onboarding.dataMigration.errorMessage`, `theme.picker.errorMessage`, `onboarding.alert.screenTimeMessage`, `focusTask.picker.emptyMessage` (11 `en` keys use a straight `"`). Rule: the locale's own delimiters, and a mock or quoted iOS label follows the device. Options: **‘ ’** (U+2018/U+2019) — what Foundation reports as both the primary and the alternate delimiters for `nl_NL`, and the same shape Apple's Dutch UI uses around feature names, there as a straight `'` ("toegang tot 'Schermtijd'"); **“ ”** — the double form a Dutch pull-quote or quote card often takes, which reads more like a quotation on the share card and the Daily Quote widget but is not the locale's reported delimiter. Recommended: **‘ ’**, because it is the locale's own delimiter and matches Apple's habit for the quoted-label case, which is the more frequent one (five keys against five). `onboarding.alert.screenTimeMessage` is not one of the mock-alert bodies the Platform terms verbatim rule enumerates, so its quotes around "Continue" and "Moneyfesting" follow this general recommendation, not the verbatim mirror (Platform terms, "Screen Time access alert (mock)" row). Meanwhile: the first batch uses ‘ ’ everywhere, curly in both positions, and the adjudicator lists the quote-card keys separately so a flip touches only them.
- **`badge_conceit` — whether the workplace metaphor travels into Dutch.** Keys: the 24 `achievements.badge.*.name` values and the 5 `achievements.category.*.name` values, with their `.earned` lines. Rule: one conceit per locale, decided once and kept across the family, gender-neutral, with plain descriptors left plain (`references/project-context.md` `## Content policy`). Options: **keep it** — Dutch has the vocabulary natively and without gendered role nouns: "Ingeklokt", "Aangenomen", "In vaste dienst", "Opnieuw aangenomen", "Terug in dienst", "Dubbele dienst", "De loonlijst", "De dienst"; **drop it** — replace the employment names with plain achievement names ("Eerste sessie", "Zeven dagen op rij", "Vijftig volle dagen"), which loses the wink but removes any reading of the app as an employer, next to a money frame that already has to say five times that nothing is earned. Recommended: **keep it**, because the conceit is what makes the badge family cohere in `en` and Dutch carries it with ordinary words rather than jargon; the money frame is protected by the fixed disclaimers and the banned-word table, not by the badge names. Meanwhile: the first achievements batch drafts the conceit, marks every name a proposal, and the adjudicator reports the whole family together so a flip is one batch.
- **`nl_be_audience` — whom the Dutch copy is written for.** Affects vocabulary across the catalog, not any single key. Rule: one `nl` resource serves both audiences, because `nl-NL` and `nl-BE` both negotiate to `nl` and iOS ships only `nl.lproj` (apple-coverage `system_negotiation`). Options: **Netherlands wording** — the Netherlands storefront's default language is Dutch, Belgium's default is English (U.K.) with Dutch as an additional language, and Apple's own `nl.lproj` is Netherlands Dutch, so the Belgian reader already sees this variety everywhere on the device; **NL/BE-neutral wording** — avoid the handful of terms that differ and check the register, at the cost of blunting everyday phrasing in the larger market. Recommended: **Netherlands wording**, for the storefront reason above and because a neutral variety invented by an agent, with no native read on either side, is more likely to read as translated in both. Meanwhile: write Netherlands Dutch, and record any term an editor flags as Belgium-divergent in the batch report rather than hedging the line.
