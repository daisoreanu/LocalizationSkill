## Identity

- **`pt-PT` and `pt-BR` are two separate resources, not one shared one.** Unlike Dutch's `nl-NL`/`nl-BE` pair, `pt-PT` and `pt-BR` negotiate to two different tags in every tested list (`en, pt-BR, pt-PT` resolves `pt-BR` to `pt-BR` and `pt-PT` to `pt-PT` in all 504 shared negotiation cells checked), so this brief and its `pt-BR` twin are written in parallel and must never converge on one wording, one glossary or one voice: **Portugal evidence only**, and a shipped word here is not evidence for the other file. Apple's own `pt-PT.lproj` is European Portuguese throughout the harvest (`Fotografias` for Photos, `Ecrã principal` for Home Screen, `Definições` for Settings), the everyday vocabulary this brief cites, distinct from Brazilian's `Fotos`, `Tela de início`, `Configurações`.

## Audience and register

- A friendly coach in everyday European Portuguese: short sentences, one idea per line, concrete verbs, no jargon and no English sentence shapes. A reader who knows no English must understand each line at first read, which rules out the English loanwords Portuguese tech writing sometimes reaches for (`streak`, `tracking`, `feedback`) even though a Portuguese reader would recognise them.
- Drop the source's scaffolding: 15 `en` keys carry "please" (14 capitalised), and neither register attested for Portuguese (Portugal) writes "Por favor" on a UI control — `common.editor.deleteFailedMessage` "The deletion couldn't be saved. Please try again." is one plain sentence plus "Tenta outra vez." (or the formal "Tente outra vez.", per the address open item below).
- **Two registers coexist in Apple's own `pt-PT` corpus, and this brief's decision (Open decisions `address_register`) is to follow the dominant one.** Every system permission alert harvested — the Screen Time access alert, the notification alert, the Health "Don't Allow" message, Apple's own support pages — drops the pronoun and uses the third-person/formal verb form ("pode", "poderá", "os seus", never "tu"/"teu"): see `## Address and gender` and the platform-terms register summary (`{"address": "3rd person formal (lhe, os seus, Pode)", "buttons": "infinitive: Permitir / Não permitir / Continuar"}`). A handful of newer Fitness achievement strings (file names ending `-tinker`, likely an internal draft variant) use the informal `tu` instead ("Completa o anel…", "correres", "Força!"), against the older, non-`-tinker` file for the same string, which stays formal ("Obterá esta distinção…"). Because the formal register is the one that is consistent across frameworks, across Apple's own support documentation, and across every mock alert this app must mirror verbatim, this brief defaults app-owned copy to the same formal, pronoun-dropping register — never `tu`/`teu` — so app-owned lines never create an audible seam next to the mock alerts they sit beside.
- The app speaks about itself in the first person plural, as `en` does in 19 units: "We couldn't load the data." → "Não foi possível carregar os dados." (`component.errorView.message`; Portuguese's own impersonal construction reads more naturally here than a literal "Nós não conseguimos…"), "We covered %@ with a streak freeze." (`streakFreeze.usedBody`) → "Uma congelação da sequência protegeu %@.", "we'll use %@" (`onboarding.hourlyWage.footer`) → "será usado %@". Where a literal "nós" would sound stiff in formal register, the impersonal or passive form is preferred; the meaning (the app acting) must still be clear.
- Imported shapes the editor flags. ## Address and gender

- **Formal, pronoun-dropping address by default; never `tu`/`teu` in app-owned copy** (Decided conflicts; Open decisions `address_register` records the counter-evidence and what would flip it). Every register-bearing system string harvested drops the subject pronoun and inflects for the implied "você": "Ao conceder acesso a Tempo de ecrã, poderá permitir que "%@" veja os seus dados de atividade, aplique restrições de conteúdo e limite o uso de aplicações e sites." (`Applications/FamilyControlsAuthenticationUI.app | Localizable.strings | NOTIFICATION_AUTH_REQUEST_MESSAGE_INDIVIDUAL`), "Pode voltar a ativar as categorias de dados de saúde na aplicação Saúde." (`System/Library/PrivateFrameworks/HealthUI.framework | HealthUI-Localizable.strings | AUTHORIZATION_DONT_ALLOW_ALERT_MESSAGE`), "Notificações podem incluir alertas, sons e emblemas, e podem ser configuradas nas Definições." (`System/Library/PrivateFrameworks/UserNotificationsServer.framework | Localizable.strings | USER_NOTIFICATION_PERMISSION_ALERT_BODY`). Apple's own pt-PT support prose does the same: "Com o Tempo de ecrã, existem várias definições e controlos parentais que **pode** utilizar…" (https://support.apple.com/pt-pt/105121, fetched 2026-09-16). No harvested permission alert or support sentence uses `tu`.
- Possessives follow the same register: "seu"/"sua", never "teu"/"tua", in any string that mirrors iOS or reads like it could (`os seus dados`, `na aplicação Saúde`, above).
- Buttons are infinitives regardless of the address decision, because the Portuguese infinitive does not mark person and reads as the polite command either way: "Permitir", "Não permitir", "Cancelar", "Guardar", "Editar", "Continuar" (Control forms). A handful of fixed marketing phrases instead take the formal third-person **command** form, distinct from the infinitive but still formal, not `tu`: "Poupe %@ – %@" for a discount (`System/Library/PrivateFrameworks/Preferences.framework | Localizable.strings | ST_SIZE_INFO_FMT`), "Saiba mais" for "Learn More" (`System/Library/CoreServices/EscrowSecurityAlert.app | Localizable.strings | LEARNMORE`). Both forms are formal; neither is `tu`.
- **Gender.** Unlike Dutch, Portuguese predicate adjectives and past participles *do* inflect for the gender of their subject ("pronto"/"pronta", "focado"/"focada"), and the stored profile gender (`Manifesting/Features/User/Domain/Entities/Gender.swift`) is not a copy input without a product decision (`references/project-context.md` `## Product and engineering invariants`), so this is a real constraint, not a formality. The 44 reader-predicate `en` units (`you_are` 5, `are_you` 5, `you_showed_up` 1, `welcome_back` 1, `ready` 7, `focused` 19, `done` 15) split into two groups:
  - Most already predicate on a grammatically-gendered noun, not on the reader, so Portuguese agreement lands on that noun and never touches the user's gender: "Session done" → "Sessão concluída" (agrees with `a sessão`, feminine), "Focus complete" → "Foco concluído" (agrees with `o foco`, masculine), `achievements.gauge.title` "Session done" and `widgets.startFocus.finished` "Focus complete" both fall here.
  - Where the predicate really targets the reader (`welcome_back`, `you_are`, `are_you`, `you_showed_up`), prefer a verb-only construction that carries no gendered agreement at all: `achievements.streak.comebackBody` "Welcome back." → not "Bem-vindo(a) de volta." but **"Estás de volta."** under the address decision's formal counterpart, or the invariant fixed phrase "De volta." — "de volta" is a prepositional phrase and does not inflect. `achievements.streak.body` "You showed up %lld days in a row." → "Estiveste presente %lld dias consecutivos." — the past tense of "estar" does not mark gender; only an adjective or participle would.
  - Where no rephrase preserves the line within budget, the fallback is the unmarked masculine form, standard Portuguese generic usage absent a chosen gender, flagged per key in the translator's comment for a future gender-aware split if the app ever exposes profile gender as a copy input.
- **Badge and category role nouns are the sharper version of the same problem**, because unlike Dutch's invariant `-er` agent suffix, Portuguese role nouns mark gender by default (`contratado`/`contratada`, `assalariado`/`assalariada`). Strategy, in this order, matching Apple's own avoidance of gendered agent nouns for achievements ("Earn this award" → "**Obterá** esta distinção" / "**Conseguirás** esta distinção" — Apple's pt-PT never uses "ganhar" or an agent noun for an achievement, `FitnessUIAssets.bundle/ActivityAchievements/FiveKilometerWorkout/localization | Localizable.stringsdict | ACHIEVEMENT_UNACHIEVED_DESCRIPTION/NSStringLocalizedFormatKey` and `.../FirstWorkout-HKWorkoutActivityTypeCardioDance/... | Localizable.stringsdict | ACHIEVEMENT_UNACHIEVED_DESCRIPTION/NSStringLocalizedFormatKey`): (1) a nominalised event instead of an agent noun — `achievements.badge.hired.name` "Hired" → "Contratação" (the hiring, feminine noun, invariant — no agreement to the person at all), `.rehired.name` "Rehired" → "Recontratação"; (2) a prepositional phrase instead of a predicate adjective — `.salaried.name` "Salaried" → "Em efetivo" (on permanent staff, an established Portuguese employment idiom, invariant); (3) an adjective that happens to be gender-invariant — `.permanent.name` "Permanent" → "Permanente" (ends in `-e`, does not inflect); (4) never a paired or slashed form ("contratado/a"), never defaulting silently without a flag. The conceit itself — whether the workplace metaphor travels into Portuguese at all — is an owner call (Open decisions `badge_conceit`).
- `user.gender.*` is a data field, not voice: `user.gender.female` "Female", `.male` "Male", `.other` "Other", `.preferNotToSay` "Prefer not to say" — seed 2026-09-16: "Feminino", "Masculino", "Outro", "Prefiro não dizer".
- Grep aid, not a validator: `\btu\b`, `\bteu\b`, `\btua\b`, and paired/slashed forms `\w+ado/a\b`, `\w+ada?/o\b`.

## Header and label forms

- **Sentence case everywhere in app-owned copy.** Apple's Portuguese (Portugal) is sentence case throughout the harvest ("Adicionar widget", "Editar ecrã principal", "Gerir assinaturas"), and Portuguese orthography capitalises only the first word plus proper names. The 114 Title Case `en` keys become sentence case: "Set Your Hourly Wage" → "Define a tua tarifa horária" (register per Open decisions `address_register`; `onboarding.hourlyWage.title`), "Edit Focus Task" → "Editar tarefa de foco" (`focusTask.createTask.screenTitleEdit`), "My Tasks" → "As minhas tarefas" (`focusTask.picker.myTasksHeader`). Proper names keep their capitals: Moneyfesting, Tempo de ecrã, Face ID, App Store, Fotografias, Saúde, Mensagens, Ecrã principal.
- **Uppercase eyebrows and captions stay uppercase where `en` is** (34 all-caps `en` strings: `insights.mood.mood` "MOOD", `insights.summary.dayStreakTitle` "DAY STREAK", `widgets.focusRing.title` "TODAY'S FOCUS", `plan.dailyPlan.{plannedHeader,doneHeader,anytimeHeader}`, `%@ / HOUR`, `/%lld MIN`). Portuguese has no letter-doubling or ligature hazard on uppercasing (no `ß`, no dotted/dotless `i` pair): accented vowels and `ç` keep their diacritics uppercased (`AÇÃO`, `CONCLUÍDAS`), Foundation `uppercased(with: Locale(identifier: "pt_PT"))` behaves the same as the root locale here.
- Uppercase captions expand hard in Portuguese too: of Apple's own all-caps Portuguese (Portugal) strings of 1-10 English characters, 35% exceed 1.35× and 8.68% exceed 2× (length-stats `all_caps`, chars "1-10"). Prefer the shorter everyday noun over the exact one and file the row when none exists (`references/surface-budgets.md` `## Catalog microcopy templates`).

## Numbers, dates, currency and punctuation

All values below are Foundation/ICU output for `pt_PT` (locale-formats, ICU 76.1.0.0 / CLDR 46.0.0.0), or the app's own `AppCurrency` path on that locale.

- **Numbers.** Grouping separator U+00A0 (no-break space), decimal separator `,` (U+002C): `12000` → "12 000", `1250000` → "1 250 000", `1250.5` → "1250,5". **Four-digit numbers are not grouped**: `2000` stays "2000" (locale-formats `four_digit_grouping`), unlike Dutch and Romanian which do group at four digits — so a literal like `achievements.badge.wageYear.*` "2,000 hours of focus" becomes "2000 horas de foco", never "2 000 horas".
- **Money.** Runtime values arrive formatted through `AppLanguage.effectiveLocale`, so no string hard-codes a currency (`en` has no "EUR" or "euro" literal; the placeholder carries the amount, `references/project-context.md` `## Currency display`). `AppCurrency.format` gives "120 €" and "1250,5 €" with a **no-break space** (U+00A0) between the number and the symbol; the locale's own standard form is "120,00 €" / "1250,50 €". Compact: the app prints "1,25k €" / "1,25m €" / "1,25b €" where the locale would print "1,25 mil €" / "1,25 M €" — see the Identity engineering flag: `mil` and the app's `m` share their first letter, a **high**-severity collision unique to this locale among the ones surveyed so far. Never write a currency name into copy.
- **Percent** is glued after the number with no space: "40%", "100%" (U+0025). "%lld%%" and "-%lld%%" keep that shape.
- **Time** is 24-hour (`Locale.hourCycle` h23, `DateFormatter` template `j` → `HH`): "09:41", "23:59" (locale-formats `time`); no shortened form drops the leading zero in the harvest sample (`23:59` stays `23:59`; verify "09:41" against a render, since the shortened path was not exercised separately for this locale). The twelve `en` clock literals (`12AM`, `06PM` and kin on `insights.appUsage.{axisMidnight,axisNoon,axisSixAm,axisSixPm}`, `profile.appUsage.axis.*`, `screenTimeReport.{axisMidnight,axisNoon,axisSixAM,axisSixPM}`) become "00:00", "06:00", "12:00", "18:00".
- **Dates**: "31/12/2026" (day/month/year, abbreviated), "31 de dezembro de 2026" (wide), "12 de maio" (day + month), "terça-feira, 12/05" (weekday + abbreviated date), "terça-feira" (wide weekday). Month and weekday names are lower case in Portuguese, mid-sentence and at the start of a label alike.
- **Ranges** take a numeric hyphen as `en` already does for one of its two range keys: `onboarding.deviceUsage.oneToThreeHours` "1-3 hours" → "1-3 horas"; verify the render for `onboarding.personalGoal.validation` "1–280 characters" and `widgets.startFocus.description` "5–120 minutes" against the app's own dash choice before assuming an en dash, since the locale-formats harvest did not exercise a range literal directly.
- **Quotation marks are an open item.** Foundation reports `«` / `»` (U+00AB / U+00BB) as the primary delimiters for `pt_PT` and `“` / `”` (U+201C / U+201D) as the alternate pair — but Apple's own UI habit for quoting a placeholder inline in a sentence uses the **alternate** curly pair, not the primary guillemets: "“%@” gostaria de aceder a Tempo de ecrã" (`Applications/FamilyControlsAuthenticationUI.app | Localizable.strings | NOTIFICATION_AUTH_REQUEST_TITLE`), "“%@” gostaria de adicionar às suas fotografias." (`System/Library/PrivateFrameworks/TCC.framework | Localizable.strings | REQUEST_ACCESS_SERVICE_kTCCServicePhotosAdd`). See Open decisions `quote_marks`; until it closes, use `“ ”` for a UI label quoted inside a sentence and leave the quote-card wrapper on the recommended option with a note.
- **Plurals: `one`, `many` and `other`**, but `many` only reaches integers of 1,000,000 or more; every integer from 0 to 999,999 selects `one` (the integer 1 only) or `other` (0, and 2 through 999,999). Boundary integers to exercise: **0, 1, 2, 11, 20, 21, 100, 101, 999999, 1000000, 1000001, 2000000**, plus the fractions 0.5, 1.5, 2.5 (all `other`). Xcode 26.1's `variationConfigFile.plist` does not list `many` for `pt-PT` (only `one`/`other`, `recommended_required`), so **`one` and `other` are the two branches every plural key needs**; a hand-added `many` variant compiles and is honoured at runtime for the rare seven-figure count, but the catalog falls back to `other` if it is missing, which is an acceptable gap at this app's scale (locale-formats `plural`, `xcode_vs_icu`, `runtime_check`).
- **How numerals attach to nouns.** The numeral stands before the bare noun with an ordinary space and no linking word: "1 dia", "3 dias", "%lld minutos". There is no partitive construction, so a single `%lld` never forces a different noun form the way it can in Romanian.
- Six keys are a single `en` `stringUnit` but need Portuguese `one`/`other` branches, because the count is inside the sentence: `achievements.streak.body`, `.comebackBody`, `.headline`, `.milestoneBody`, `.nextMilestoneBody`, `achievements.streak.weekAccessibility`.

## Length trigger

- **1.30×.** A Portuguese (Portugal) string longer than 1.30 times its English length goes on the render list; `scripts/catalog_check.py` C5 puts it there, and it is never a finding by itself. The ratio is measured from Apple's own Portuguese translations: across 45,520 unique English/target pairs in the iOS 26.1 runtime, 26.5% exceed a flat 1.35×, which would under-flag; 1.30×, rounded to 0.05, is the point that matches the Romanian calibration (Apple's `ro` exceeds 1.35× in 31.6% of its own pairs, the statistic closest to the `ro` catalog's 299 of 1135 units) — at 1.30× Apple's Portuguese pairs exceed it 31.71% of the time, the closest rounded match (length-stats `recommendation`).
- Where it bites: short strings expand most. Median ratio 1.172 overall, but 1.25 for English strings of 1-10 characters (38.43% over 1.35×), against 1.125 and 11.81% for strings of 41-120 characters. All-caps strings of 1-10 characters are the worst case: 35% over 1.35× and 8.68% over 2× — exactly the eyebrows and widget captions (`insights.mood.mood` "MOOD", `widgets.focusRing.row.money` "TIME VALUE, 30 DAYS", `insights.summary.lastThirtyDaysSubtitle` "LAST 30 DAYS").
- No catalog share can be quoted for `pt-PT`: there are 0 Portuguese (Portugal) units at the snapshot. Re-state this line with a real share after the first wave.
- Counts never establish fit. Verdicts come from render evidence against each view's own floor and line limit (`references/surface-budgets.md`, `references/render-evidence.md`). Shortening cuts words, never facts; accessibility strings are exempt.

## Decided conflicts

| Conflict | Winner | Rule |
|---|---|---|
| Address: `tu`/`teu` versus formal, pronoun-dropping address | formal, mocks and app-owned copy alike | Every register-bearing system string harvested (permission alerts, Health, support pages) drops the pronoun and inflects formally, with only a handful of newer Fitness "-tinker" files using `tu`; the dominant, cross-framework pattern wins so app copy never seams against the mock alerts it must mirror verbatim. Open decisions `address_register` records the counter-evidence |
| Button form: infinitive versus the informal `tu` imperative | plain infinitive on ordinary controls ("Cancelar", "Guardar", "Editar") | Apple's runtime uses the infinitive for nearly every button; a small set of fixed marketing phrases uses the formal command form instead ("Poupe", "Saiba mais"), never the informal imperative |
| Four-digit grouping: grouped versus not | not grouped ("2000", not "2 000") | Foundation's `pt_PT` number formatter leaves four-digit integers ungrouped while grouping from five digits up (locale-formats `four_digit_grouping`) |
| Photos app name: "Fotografias" versus "Fotos" | Fotografias | Apple's `pt_PT.lproj` CFBundleDisplayName for Photos.app is "Fotografias"; "Fotos" is the Brazilian form this brief must stay distinct from |
| Compact money suffix `k`/`m`/`b` against the locale's `mil`/`M` | engineering finding, not a translation choice, flagged high severity | `AppCurrency.formatCompact` hardcodes the Latin suffix; `mil` (thousand) and the app's `m` (millions) share their first letter, a collision severe enough that a local reader can misread the order of magnitude (`compact_finding`, severity "high") |
| `Add Widget`: one key or two | one key | iOS 26.1 Portuguese (Portugal) labels the edit-menu item and the gallery button with the same text (only a layout leading space differs), so the `ro` split does not arise |
| Earning an achievement: "ganhar" versus "obter"/"conseguir" | obter/conseguir for badges and freezes; "ganhar" stays inside the five fixed money-frame disclaimers only | Apple's own Portuguese never uses "ganhar" for an achievement ("Conseguirás esta distinção…", "Obterá esta distinção…"), and reusing it for badges would blur the one word this app must keep pinned to the money frame |

## Open decisions

Each item is mirrored in `glossary/pt-PT.json` `open` with the fields `scripts/term_audit.py` reads. Nothing here is translated yet, so "meanwhile" means what the first batches do, not which shipped keys stay locked.

- **`address_register` — `tu` versus formal, pronoun-dropping address for app-owned copy.** Affects every sentence with a second-person subject, not any single key: `onboarding.*`, `moneyfesting.details.*`, `achievements.*.earned`, the whole reader-predicate list in `## Address and gender`. Rule: whichever register wins, mock alerts (`onboarding.screenTimePermission.alert*`, `onboarding.notificationPermission.alert*`) always mirror Apple's own formal wording verbatim regardless, because those strings quote the device. Options: **formal, pronoun-dropping** ("pode", "os seus", "poderá") — the register of every permission alert, the Health deny message and Apple's own pt-PT support pages (https://support.apple.com/pt-pt/105121, fetched 2026-09-16), so app copy never seams against the mocks it sits beside; **`tu`/`teu`** — the informal register this app's warmth and short sentences might otherwise reach for, and the one a handful of newer Apple Fitness achievement strings use ("Completa o anel…", "Força!", file names ending `-tinker`, likely an internal draft variant rather than the stable convention). Recommended: **formal, pronoun-dropping**, because it is the register Apple itself uses consistently across frameworks and in its own support writing, and because the mock alerts this app must mirror verbatim are unambiguously formal — a `tu` app voice next to a formal mock alert would be the exact kind of audible seam the skill asks the editor to flag. Meanwhile: the first batch writes every sentence in the formal register and lists any line where that reads stiff (the private, one-off customization line `onboarding.widgets.howTo.placeTitle` "Make it yours." is flagged as a candidate exception in `## Idioms and figurative language`) so a flip is one recorded global change, not a per-string choice.
- **`streak_term` — the noun for a streak.** Affects every noun slot: `widgets.streak.smallHeader` "Streak", `.currentStreakHeader` "Current streak", `.displayName` "Focus Streak", `.daysCount`, `.dayStreakCount`, `insights.summary.dayStreakTitle` "DAY STREAK", `share.card.streakEyebrow`, `achievements.streak.title`, `onboarding.welcome.slide.streak.{eyebrow,headline}`, `paywall.benefit.streak.title`, the whole `streakFreeze.*` family. Rule: everyday Portuguese a reader without English understands at first read, and it must fit a one-word widget header and an uppercase caption (Length trigger). Options: **"sequência"** — the plain Portuguese noun for a run of consecutive occurrences, takes a compound ("sequência de foco") and an uppercase caption ("SEQUÊNCIA"), with no direct Apple noun evidence for this exact sense but a supporting adjective ("dia consecutivo") in Apple's own Fitness copy; **"dias consecutivos"** — a phrase, not a noun, cannot fill a header or a compact compound; **"streak"** — the English loan, ruled out by the no-English-needed rule. Recommended: **"sequência" in noun slots, with "dias consecutivos" as the phrase in running text**, because it is the only plain Portuguese noun that survives the widget budget. Meanwhile: the first batch writes "sequência" in headers and "dias consecutivos" in sentences and lists every key that touched the term, so a flip is one recorded global change.
- **`quote_marks` — the marks around a displayed quotation and around a quoted UI label.** Keys: `moneyfesting.details.quoteWrapper` "%@", `notifications.contentExtension.quoteDecoration` "%@", `moneyfesting.main.quotePlaceholder`, `theme.createTheme.sampleQuote`, `widgets.quotes.placeholderText`, plus the UI labels quoted inside `component.errorView.message`, `onboarding.dataMigration.errorMessage`, `theme.picker.errorMessage`, `onboarding.alert.screenTimeMessage`, `focusTask.picker.emptyMessage` (11 `en` keys use a straight double quote). Rule: the locale's own delimiters, and a mock or quoted iOS label follows the device. Options: **« »** (U+00AB/U+00BB) — Foundation's reported primary delimiter for `pt_PT`; **" "** (U+201C/U+201D) — Foundation's reported alternate delimiter, and the shape Apple's own UI actually uses to quote a placeholder inline (""%@" gostaria de aceder a Tempo de ecrã"). Recommended: **" "**, because it is the shape the mock alerts must mirror anyway and the more frequent of the two use cases in this app (UI labels quoted mid-sentence outnumber the standalone quote-card wrapper). Meanwhile: the first batch uses " " everywhere, and the adjudicator lists the quote-card keys separately so a flip touches only them, leaving « » available for a more literary quote-card treatment if the owner prefers it there.
- **`badge_conceit` — whether the workplace metaphor travels into Portuguese, and in what shape.** Keys: the 24 `achievements.badge.*.name` values and the 5 `achievements.category.*.name` values, with their `.earned` lines. Rule: one conceit per locale, decided once and kept across the family, gender-neutral, with plain descriptors left plain (`references/project-context.md` `## Content policy`). Options: **keep it, nominalised** — Portuguese carries the whole workplace vocabulary ("contratação", "turno", "em efetivo", "a folha de pagamento"), but every agent-noun name needs the nominal or prepositional rewrite in `## Address and gender` to stay gender-neutral, which changes the family's rhythm more than Dutch's simple `-er` fix did; **drop it** — replace the employment names with plain achievement names ("Primeira sessão", "Sete dias seguidos", "Cinquenta dias completos"), losing the wink but sidestepping every gender-agreement decision at once, next to a money frame that already says five times that nothing is earned. Recommended: **keep it, nominalised**, because the conceit is what makes the badge family cohere in `en` and Portuguese's own employment vocabulary carries it without inventing anything, but this is the closer call of the two options surveyed in this run (Dutch, by contrast, kept it with almost no rewrite needed) and the owner may reasonably prefer the simpler, gender-safe "drop it" path. Meanwhile: the first achievements batch drafts the nominalised conceit, marks every name a proposal, and the adjudicator reports the whole family together so a flip is one batch.
