## Identity

- Tag `sv`, bare: iOS ships one Swedish interface bundle, `sv.lproj` (present in 24 of 26 UI bundles checked; absent only in `UIKit.framework` and `ScreenTime.framework`, both `en`-only frameworks), and both `sv-SE` and `sv-FI` negotiate to `sv` in every system bundle sampled (`Preferences`, `UIKitCore`, `SpringBoardHome`, `UserNotificationsServer`, `FamilyControlsAuthUI`, `StoreKitUIService`). No `sv_SE.lproj` folder exists anywhere in the runtime (0 found against 3157 `sv.lproj` folders). `xcstringstool` compiles to `sv.lproj` (`LOCLocaleCode.plist` `sv`: displayName "Swedish", lproj `sv`).
- Audience: Swedish speakers on a Swedish-language iPhone, primarily in Sweden; Latin script, left to right, letters å ä ö. Format locale `sv_SE`, audience currency SEK. Sweden's App Store storefront defaults to Swedish with English (U.K.) as the additional language; Sweden is one of the few version-1 storefronts whose default listing language is the local one, unlike Finland's storefront (default English (U.K.), Finnish additional) or most other wave rows. SEK is in `CurrencyCatalog.options` with region `SE` (`Manifesting/Foundation/Localization/CurrencyOption.swift:63`) and the host ICU default for `SE` is also SEK, so there is no currency gap for this audience.
- Snapshot 2026-09-16 against catalog commit `c8a49dd`: 0 `sv` units of 1189 keys in the working tree (1187 committed at `c8a49dd`); the catalog carries `en` and `ro` only. A live count above 1187 reflects uncommitted keys, not a defect.
- Precedence: app `CLAUDE.md` first; then Apple's Swedish wording for anything that mirrors or names an iOS surface (`## Platform terms`); then this brief. `glossary/sv.json` records the outcomes.
- Every row here is agent research: no shipped Swedish copy, no native source, no human review. - Engineering flags that block a Swedish ship, all to report rather than fix: `AppLanguage.supportedLanguageCodes` is `["en", "ro"]` (`Manifesting/Foundation/Localization/AppLanguage.swift:6`), so the in-app language override ignores `sv`; `knownRegions` is `(en, ro, Base)` (`Manifesting.xcodeproj/project.pbxproj:6891`), and `sv` must also be added to `Localizable.xcstrings` and `InfoPlist.xcstrings`; `CFBundleLocalizations` in `Manifesting/ScreenTimeShieldConfigurationExtension/Info.plist` is `(en, ro)`; the 25 shield pairs are per-language struct fields, `titleEnglish`, `titleRomanian`, `bodyEnglish`, `bodyRomanian` (`Manifesting/Foundation/ShieldSupport/ShieldMessage.swift:14-17`) selected by `languageCode == "ro"` (`:19,23`), so a third language is a struct change, not a resource add, and `scripts/term_audit.py` and `scripts/catalog_check.py` can see only `en` and `ro` shield text until it lands. ## Address and gender

- `du` throughout app-owned copy: second-person singular verbs, the possessive `din/ditt/dina` where ownership is not already obvious, never the archaic/formal `ni`. Apple's own Swedish uses `du` on every mirrored permission and notification screen: "vill skicka notiser till dig" (`System/Library/PrivateFrameworks/UserNotificationsServer.framework | Localizable.strings | USER_NOTIFICATION_PERMISSION_ALERT_TITLE`), "Om ”%@” får tillgång till Skärmtid kan den se dina aktivitetsdata" (`Applications/FamilyControlsAuthenticationUI.app | Localizable.strings | NOTIFICATION_AUTH_REQUEST_MESSAGE_INDIVIDUAL`), "Du kan slå på hälsodatakategorier senare i appen Hälsa." (`System/Library/PrivateFrameworks/HealthUI.framework | HealthUI-Localizable.strings | AUTHORIZATION_DONT_ALLOW_ALERT_MESSAGE`), "Bli meddelad när din veckorapport är tillgänglig..." (`ScreenTimeSettingsUI.framework | Localizable.strings | WeeklyReportNotificationGroupSpecifierFooterText`). This is not an open decision: Sweden's `du-reformen` settled `du` as the default register for strangers and institutions decades ago, and every Apple surface this app mirrors already uses it.
- Controls are second-person or bare imperatives: "Tillåt" / "Tillåt inte" / "Fortsätt" (`UserNotificationsServer.framework | Localizable.strings | PERMISSION_ALERT_ALLOW`, `PERMISSION_ALERT_DENY`; `FamilyControlsAuthenticationUI.app | Localizable.strings | COMMON_CONTINUE`); negation is postposed (`inte` after the verb), never a separate "no"-prefixed word.
- The app speaks about itself in the first person plural on 19 `en` keys ("We couldn't load the data.", `component.errorView.message`; "we'll use %@", `onboarding.hourlyWage.footer`; "We covered %@ with a streak freeze.", `streakFreeze.usedBody`). Swedish keeps the actor as a plain subject: "Vi kunde inte läsa in data.", "vi använder %@", "En frysning täckte %@." Do not turn these into a passive that hides who acted — Swedish can form an impersonal passive with `-s` ("täcktes") but Apple's own mirrored strings choose the active voice with a named subject wherever the app itself acts (see the `du`/`den`/`vi` examples above), so this app's copy should too.
- Gender: Swedish grammatical gender (common/neuter, `en`/`ett`) attaches to nouns, not to people, and modern Swedish has no requirement to mark the reader's gender at all — predicate adjectives and past participles agree with the noun they modify, never with the person addressed. The `en` keys that force a gendered predicate in some Romance languages carry none in Swedish: `achievements.streak.body` ("You showed up %lld days in a row." -> "Du var med %lld dagar i rad."), `achievements.badge.fullDaysFifty.earned`, `achievements.badge.hired.name`, `achievements.badge.salaried.name`, `achievements.badge.rehired.name`, `achievements.badge.clockedIn.earned`, `achievements.gauge.title`, `widgets.tasks.allDone`. Reader-predicate sentences behave the same way: `focusTask.alert.deleteTask.message`, `onboarding.dailyFocusGoal.subtitle`, `screenTime.createRestrictedList.savedMessage`, `shield.reflection.begin.title` — none need a neutral-form strategy.
- What does need agreement is number and the noun's own grammatical gender, not the reader's gender: a past participle describing a noun agrees with that noun's gender and number (`%lld app blockerad` common-gender singular / `%lld appar blockerade` plural, `screenTime.restrictedApps.appsRestricted`; `Fokus pausat` neuter singular, `widgets.startFocus.paused`), and a fragment with no visible noun still needs the noun named so the participle has something to agree with (`## Header and label forms`).
- The only reader-gender surface is the picker itself, four plain nouns: `user.gender.female`, `.male`, `.other`, `.preferNotToSay`. Sweden's third neutral pronoun `hen` exists and is common in official use, but this app's copy never needs a third-person pronoun for the reader, so `hen` has no call site to flag. The stored profile gender (`Manifesting/Features/User/Domain/Entities/Gender.swift`) is not a copy input without a product decision.
- Grep aid, not a validator, for a slipped-in formal register: `Ni`/`ni` as a second-person pronoun (not the counting word "ni" doesn't exist in Swedish, so any capital-N "Ni" addressing the reader is the tell), `Er`/`Ert`/`Era` as a possessive, and an imperative ending in `-en` borrowed from a formal plural.

## Header and label forms

- Sentence case everywhere in app-owned copy: Swedish capitalises the first word and proper names only, even in multi-word labels. Apple's Swedish does this consistently on every harvested term: "Hemskärm och appbibliotek" (`Applications/Preferences.app | Localizable.strings | Home Screen & App Library`), "Gratis provperiod" (`System/Library/Frameworks/StoreKit.framework | Localizable.strings | MODE_FREE`), "Språk och region" (`System/Library/PrivateFrameworks/Settings/GeneralSettingsUI.framework | General.strings | INTERNATIONAL`). English Title Case loses its inner capitals in `sv` unless the capitalised word is a proper name from `## Platform terms`: "Edit Focus Task" -> "Ändra fokusuppgift" (`focusTask.createTask.screenTitleEdit`; note the closed compound "fokusuppgift"), "Total Screen Time" -> "Total skärmtid" (`profile.appUsage.totalScreenTimeTitle`).
- Uppercasing Swedish is safe and lossless: å, ä, ö keep their marks in ALL CAPS, and Swedish has no dotted/dotless i pair (that is Turkish) and no ß (that is German), so `.textCase(.uppercase)` and `String.uppercased()` need no locale argument for `sv`; the cost of uppercase is width, not correctness (`## Length trigger`).
- Eyebrows and stat captions stay uppercase where `en` is uppercase: MOOD -> HUMÖR, TODAY -> IDAG, DAY STREAK -> the `streak_term` open decision's compact form in caps, "%@ / HOUR" -> "%@ / TIMME". `plan.dailyPlan.plannedHeader` / `.doneHeader` / `.anytimeHeader` ("PLANNED" / "DONE" / "ANYTIME") become "PLANERAT" / "KLART" / "NÄR SOM HELST" — the third is the longest of the three and is a render-list candidate (`## Length trigger`) rather than a shortened guess.
- Section headers and stat labels are plain nouns or past participles agreeing with the labelled item, never with the reader: "Recently earned" (`achievements.gallery.recentSection`) -> "Nyligen intjänade" (the allowed "earned" sense for badges, `## Money frame`); tab titles are single nouns (`main.tab.*`): Moneyfesting (brand, unchanged), Plan (unchanged — "plan" is itself standard Swedish), Insikter, Profil.
- Toggles and state labels take the past participle or a plain adjective agreeing in gender and number with the labelled noun, never with the reader: `%lld app blockerad` (common-gender singular) / `%lld appar blockerade` (plural) (`screenTime.restrictedApps.appsRestricted`, a plural key with the same `one`/`other` categories as `en`, so both the number word and the participle need both forms); "Focus paused" -> "Fokus pausat" (neuter singular, since "fokus" takes neuter agreement, `widgets.startFocus.paused`); "In progress" -> "Pågår" (`widgets.startFocus.inProgress`).
- A fragment with no visible noun names the subject rather than standing on a bare participle, because a Swedish participle needs a noun to agree with in gender and number: `widgets.streak.copy.frozen.small` "Covered by a freeze." needs the noun named ("Gårdagen täcktes av en frysning." or similar), beside `widgets.streak.a11y.frozen` "Yesterday was covered by a freeze." which already carries its own subject. The packet's `agreement_target` names the noun for the editor to check.
- Status pills that describe the action rather than the reader take the participle alone: "Saved" -> "Sparat" (neuter, `common.editor.saved`).
- "Screen Time" (Apple's proper feature name, capital S) and generic "screen time" (hours spent on a screen) share the same Swedish word, "skärmtid" — case is the only separator, and `term_audit.py` matches case-insensitively, so a count against the glossary's `screen_time` term will mix the feature sense (`insights.appUsage.title`) with the generic sense (`screenTimeReport.noData`, `insights.savings.estimateExplanation`); this mirrors the same ambiguity found in Finnish's harvest and is noted, not solved, by capitalisation alone.

## Numbers, dates, currency and punctuation

Foundation and ICU output below is `sv_SE` on the 2026-09-16 harvest; invisible characters are named.

- Runtime values arrive formatted through `AppLanguage.effectiveLocale` (app `CLAUDE.md:42`), so strings never hard-code an amount or a currency name. Grouping is U+00A0 NO-BREAK SPACE and the decimal mark is a comma: "2 000", "12 000", "1 250,5", "1 250 000" (copied from the `locale-formats.json` harvest, not retyped). Four-digit numbers are grouped, so a literal "2,000" in source text becomes "2 000" (`achievements.badge.wageYear.name` "2,000 hours of focus" -> "2 000 timmars fokus").
- Money: `AppCurrency.format` (isoCode presentation) prints "120 SEK" and "1 250,5 SEK" with U+00A0 before the code; the locale's own standard form uses the symbol instead, "120,00 kr" and "1 250,50 kr". Compact figures print "1,25k SEK", "1,25m SEK", "1,25b SEK", while `sv_SE`'s own compact tokens are "tn" (thousand), "mn" (million) and "md" (billion, miljard): "1,25 tn SEK", "1,25 mn SEK", "1,25 md SEK" — a medium-severity engineering finding (thousands: app "k" vs locale "tn"; millions: app "m" vs locale "mn", same initial letter so lower risk; billions: app "b" vs locale "md", a complete letter mismatch with no shared risk-reducing letter, arguably worse than the thousands case), to report and never translate around (open decision `compact_currency_suffix`).
- Percent follows the number with U+00A0: "40 %", "100 %". A badge reads "-56 %", not "-56%".
- Time is 24-hour (`Locale.hourCycle` h23, `DateFormatter` template `j` -> `HH`) and the separator is a colon, exactly like `en`: "09:41", "23:59" — no change from the source format's separator, only from its 12-hour clock. The widget countdown prints "1:45:00" and "25:00" the same as `en`.
- Dates: numeric "31 dec. 2026" (day, abbreviated month, year — no comma); wide "31 december 2026"; weekday "tisdag"; weekday with a numeric day "tisdag 12 maj".
- Ranges: no Apple Swedish range example was harvested this run; propose an en dash with no spaces pending a fetch, matching the Nordic-neighbour pattern seen in `fi` ("10–20 minuter"), and flag the three `en` numeric-range keys and the `insights.appUsage.*` axis pairs for a native check before the pilot batch closes this.
- Quotation marks are ” at both ends (U+201D twice; alternates are ’ ’), matching ICU's `sv_SE` delimiters and Apple's own mock-alert quoting ("”%@” vill få tillgång till Skärmtid", `Applications/FamilyControlsAuthenticationUI.app | Localizable.strings | NOTIFICATION_AUTH_REQUEST_TITLE`). The 11 `en` keys with a straight `"` take ” ” (`moneyfesting.details.quoteWrapper` -> "”%@”", `notifications.contentExtension.quoteDecoration`, `theme.createTheme.sampleQuote`, `widgets.quotes.placeholderText`); the two mock-alert families are governed by `## Platform terms` instead, because their exact quoting is part of the mirror.
- Ellipsis and dashes follow the source character per key: 9 `en` keys use three periods and 2 use "…"; do not normalise one into the other absent a specific Apple Swedish citation either way.
- Plural categories are `one` and `other` only (ICU 76.1.0.0 / CLDR 46.0.0.0; Xcode's `variationConfigFile.plist` `PluralRule.ComplementedPluralKeys.sv` lists the same two) — the identical pair `en` uses. `one` is selected by the integer 1 and nothing else; `other` covers 0, 2, 3, 4, 11, 21, 100, 101, 1000, every larger integer, and every fraction tested (0.5, 1.5, 2.5). Because this matches `en` exactly, a plural mistake in Swedish is easy to make invisibly: 29 `en` keys already carry a declared plural variation (e.g. `screenTime.restrictedApps.appsRestricted`, `widgets.cash.focusedHoursWhole`), and those need only a direct `one`/`other` translation, no restructuring. But 6 `en` keys hardcode the plural noun with no variation declared at all because `en`'s own single string only reads naturally at count > 1 — `achievements.streak.body`, `.comebackBody`, `.headline`, `.milestoneBody`, `.nextMilestoneBody`, `.weekAccessibility` (verified against the live catalog: e.g. `achievements.streak.headline` is the bare string "%lld day streak" with no `one` form for "1 day streak"). These need the `one`/`other` split added for `sv` for the identical reason they would need it added for `en` itself: report them as variation additions, not as prose fixes.
- Numerals attach to nouns the same way `en` does — no special case or partitive marking, just singular after 1 and plural after everything else — so a concatenated "%lld" + fixed-noun-fragment string is safe in Swedish wherever it would be safe in English, unlike in Finnish or Romanian.

## Length trigger

- A Swedish string longer than 1.20× its English length goes on the render list (`scripts/catalog_check.py` C5), not into findings. The ratio is measured from Apple's own Swedish translations at iOS 26.1 (23B86), 78,330 English/Swedish pairs from `sv.lproj` of which 44,663 are unique. On the unique, unweighted set, 1.20× is the rounded ratio at which Swedish exceeds as often as Apple's Romanian exceeds 1.35× (the ratio the `ro` catalog itself is calibrated against): the harvest's own reasoning line puts the matching share at 28.4% (unrounded ratio 1.185).
- Across all 78,330 pairs the median ratio is 1.083, p75 1.25, p90 1.462, and 14.9% exceed 1.35×. Short strings expand more than long ones, which is where this app's controls and captions live: 1-10 characters median 1.111, p90 1.8, 23.2% above 1.35×; 41-120 characters median 1.083, p90 1.286, only 4.7% above 1.35×. The 906 all-caps pairs behave similarly (median 1.047, p90 1.75).
- Apple's own pairs, as calibration for a draft: Cancel -> Avbryt (0.83×), Retry -> Försök igen (2.0×), Edit -> Ändra (1.0×), Done -> Klar (0.8×), Continue -> Fortsätt (1.5×), Screen Time -> Skärmtid (0.73×), Settings -> Inställningar (1.85×), Require Face ID -> Kräv Face ID (0.94×), Got it -> OK (1.0×). Swedish both shortens and lengthens depending on the word, unlike Finnish which mostly lengthens on controls.
- Counts never establish fit; verdicts come from render evidence against each view's own floor and line limit (`references/surface-budgets.md`, `references/render-evidence.md`). Shortening cuts words, never facts; accessibility strings are exempt.

## Open decisions

- Streak term (`streak_term`). Keys: the `streak` concept's whole surface, `widgets.streak.*` (39 keys at this snapshot), `share.card.streak*`, `streakFreeze.*` (11), `paywall.benefit.streak.*`, `onboarding.welcome.slide.streak.*`, `insights.summary.dayStreakTitle`, `achievements.streak.*`. Rule: one head noun per concept, decided once and applied everywhere (`glossary/concepts.json` `streak`: consecutive days of enough focus, a fact about the calendar, not praise). Options: "svit", the established Swedish sports-writing word for an unbroken run (e.g. "en vinstsvit på fem matcher" — a five-match winning streak), short enough to compound into a widget header ("DAGSVIT" for the DAY STREAK caption); "räcka", the more literary word for a run or series, less commonly seen in sports/consumer contexts; the bare English loanword "streak", informally used in Swedish gaming and betting slang but not Apple's own style anywhere harvested, and liable to read as jargon rather than product copy. Apple's own Fitness translations never use a single noun for this concept at all, always the periphrasis "X dagar i följd" (evidence in `## Idioms and figurative language`) — which supports the plain "days in a row" fallback for body copy but does not by itself answer what a compact widget header needs. Recommended: "svit", because it is the shortest option that unambiguously means an unbroken run and compounds cleanly for captions. Meanwhile agents write the days instead of the noun ("%lld dagar i följd"), never pick per string, and the adjudicator lists every key that touched the concept; nothing enters `glossary/sv.json` `terms` until the owner closes this.
- Focus session term (`focus_session_term`). Keys: `moneyfesting.details.*` (21), `focusTask.*` (100), `widgets.startFocus.*` (15), `profile.sessionDetails.*` (29), `onboarding.welcome.preview.*` (28), `profile.home.focusSection`, `insights.focus.title`, `onboarding.dailyFocusGoal.*` (3). Rule: one term per concept, and the app's own focus session is not the iOS Focus mode. Options: "fokuspass", built from the Swedish suffix "-pass" that already names a timed session or segment of activity everywhere in everyday Swedish (träningspass = training session, yogapass, lunchpass), so the compound reads as native rather than translated; "fokussession", a direct loanword compound, matching Apple's own unchanged borrowing of "Session" in accessibility strings (iOS 26.1 (23B86) runtime: `System/Library/PrivateFrameworks/SensorKitUI.framework/AuthGroups/ECG.bundle | InfoPlist.strings | Session`, sv "Session" unchanged) but reading more clinical/technical than the "-pass" pattern; "koncentrationspass", using the more formal noun for focus/concentration, longer than either alternative for no clear gain. Recommended: "fokuspass", because "-pass" is the one option that reads as ordinary Swedish rather than a loanword or a formal noun, and it is no longer than the `en` source term. The overlap with the iOS Focus mode is small: Apple names the system mode bare "Fokus" (`Applications/Preferences.app | Localizable.strings | Focus`), and "fokuspass" never collides with it because the mode has no session concept of its own. Meanwhile a new string follows its own screen's majority and the packet's same-source siblings, and the adjudicator lists every key that touched the term.
- Mock permission alerts (`mock_permission_alerts`). Keys: `onboarding.screenTimePermission.alertTitle`, `alertMessage`, `alertDoNotAllow`; `onboarding.notificationPermission.alertTitle`, `alertMessage`, `alertAllow`, `alertDoNotAllow`; the Screen Time mock's Continue is the shared `common.continue`, quoted by `onboarding.alert.screenTimeMessage`. Rule: a mock of an iOS alert mirrors the device verbatim. Apple's Swedish differs from the `en` mocks in two ways: the Screen Time message is a conditional clause ("Om ”%@” får tillgång till Skärmtid kan den se dina aktivitetsdata...") rather than the `en` mock's imperative ("Provide... to allow it to..."), and the notification message capitalises "Inställningar" where the app's own `en` source lower-cases "settings" (itself a defect against Apple's own English, which capitalises "Settings"). Options: Apple's wording verbatim, restructured as needed; the `en` shape translated clause-for-clause so the mock keeps the app's other strings' rhythm. Recommended: Apple's wording verbatim, because the same onboarding step raises the real alert moments later and a preview in another shape shows the reader a screen iOS will not. A yes costs a source flag first (both `en` messages already differ from Apple's own English, independent of `sv`). Meanwhile the seven keys stay unwritten in Swedish and findings go to an audit scope the owner names.
- Require Face ID label (`require_face_id_label`). Keys: `onboarding.widgets.howTo.requireFaceIDLabel`. Rule: an illustrated iOS label mirrors the device verbatim, character for character. Apple's Swedish glues "Face" and "ID" with U+00A0 NO-BREAK SPACE ("Kräv Face ID", iOS 26.1 (23B86) runtime: `System/Library/PrivateFrameworks/AppProtectionUI.framework | Localizable.strings | REQUIRE_AUTH_FACEID`), while the `en` source key uses a plain space throughout the catalog. Options: match Apple's NBSP exactly, which is invisible on screen but changes how the string wraps; keep a plain space, which risks the label breaking across two lines inside the illustrated menu row where Apple's own never would. Recommended: match Apple's NBSP, for the same reason the `ro` brief recommends it on the equivalent key — the menu row is illustrated precisely so it reads like the device's own menu, and a wrap point Apple's own label never has would look wrong at the illustration's width. A yes is a one-key catalog change plus a render check of the menu row in `HomeScreenJiggleIllustrationView`. Meanwhile the key stays unwritten in Swedish and findings go to an audit scope the owner names.
- Compact currency suffix (`compact_currency_suffix`). Keys: every surface that renders a compact amount — `widgets.focusRing.row.money`, `widgets.monthlyTimeValue.*`, `insights.savings.*`, `screenTimeReport.*`, and the insights, celebration and session screens (`Manifesting/Foundation/Localization/AppCurrency.swift:53-56`). Rule: a figure the app formats is engineering, not copy; a locale brief reports it and never writes around it. `AppCurrency.formatCompact` appends a hardcoded Latin "k", "m" or "b", so Swedish would render "1,25k SEK", "1,25m SEK" and "1,25b SEK", while `sv_SE`'s own compact tokens from Foundation's `Decimal.FormatStyle.Currency(...).notation(.compactName)` are "tn", "mn" and "md" ("1,25 tn SEK", "1,25 mn SEK", "1,25 md SEK"). Severity is medium, not high: the app's "m" (millions) shares its first letter with the locale's "mn", but the app's "k" (thousands) and "b" (billions) share nothing with the locale's "tn" and "md" respectively, so a reader could misread either figure as a much smaller amount than intended — the billion case if anything worse, since "b" and "md" share no letters at all. Options: keep "k"/"m"/"b" for Swedish and accept a form no Swedish reader writes; use the locale's own compact notation, which changes the formatter for every locale, not just `sv`. Recommended: the locale's own notation, for the same reason the `fi` brief gives — the suffix sits directly against a money figure the money frame already asks readers to read carefully, and a mismatched thousands abbreviation beside a currency code reads as a bug, not a style choice. Meanwhile agents never translate around the suffix, never spell it into copy, and report it once per batch that renders a compact amount; accessibility labels use the full format and are unaffected.
