## Identity

- Tag `tr`, a bare code. `tr-TR` negotiates to `tr` in both the v1 and wave-2 preference lists, iOS ships `tr.lproj` (24 of 26 UI bundles checked; absent only from `UIKit.framework` and `ScreenTime.framework`, whose strings live in `UIKitCore.framework` and `ScreenTimeSettingsUI.framework`), and `xcstringstool compile` writes `tr.lproj` (`LOCLocaleCode.plist`: displayName "Turkish", lproj `tr`). There is no `tr_TR.lproj` anywhere in the runtime (0 folders against 3156 `tr.lproj`).
- Audience: Turkish speakers in Türkiye and the diaspora using an iPhone in Turkish; Latin script with ç ğ ı İ ö ş ü, left to right, format locale `tr_TR`, audience currency TRY.
- Snapshot 2026-09-16 against catalog commit `c8a49dd`. 0 `tr` units; the `en` side is 1108 `stringUnit:translated`, 29 plural keys, 47 key literals with no `en` entry, 5 units `new` (format-only keys).
- Precedence: app `CLAUDE.md` (informal address, preserve translator context, never hard-code currency or `AppleLanguages`), then Apple's Turkish wording for anything that mirrors or names iOS (Platform terms), then this brief. `glossary/tr.json` records the outcomes.
- Every row here is agent research: no shipped copy, no native source, no human review. A native read of the hero set, the shield pairs and the store text is recommended before the pilot batch; the recommendation is not an order, and `done` needs a named source.
- Budgets: `references/surface-budgets.md`. There is no `references/examples-tr.md`; the first batch returns six rows to seed it (`references/agent-protocol.md` `## A. Forward translator`).

## Audience and register

- A friendly coach in everyday Turkish: short sentences, one idea per line, no jargon, no English sentence shapes. A reader who knows no English understands each line at first read. Plain beats clever, and a common word beats a rarer exact one.
- Turkish is head-final and agglutinative: the verb ends the sentence, and the relations English carries in prepositions and word order are suffixes. A line that keeps the English order is the single most recognisable mark of a translated Turkish app.
- Drop English scaffolding. 14 `en` keys carry "Please" (case-sensitive; 15 case-insensitive, the extra being `quotes.user.saveError`); none of them gets a "Lütfen". "In order to" (`onboarding.alert.screenTimeMessage`, `onboarding.screenTimePermission.message`) becomes a plain `-mek için` clause.
- Imported shapes the editor flags. The first two columns are the marker list the coordinator appends to the editor prompt, target text only; the key column stays on the coordinator side (`references/agent-protocol.md` `## Packet separation`). Every example is an agent-authored illustration, because no Turkish copy exists yet.

## Address and gender

- Turkish has no grammatical gender: no gendered adjectives, participles, role nouns or third-person pronoun. The reader-predicate hotspots that force a choice in Romance locales are free here — `you_are` 5 keys, `are_you` 5, `you_showed_up` 1 (`achievements.streak.body`), `welcome_back` 1 (`achievements.streak.comebackBody`), `ready` 7, `done` 15, `focused` 19 — and so are the 24 badge role nouns (`achievements.badge.*.name`, "Hired", "Salaried", "Rehired", "Permanent") and `user.gender.other`. No neutral strategy and no shipped masculine default is needed.
- What replaces it is the T/V choice, which Turkish marks on every finite verb and every possessive, so it cannot be avoided by rewriting. It is a product decision: open decision `address_form`.
- Apple's own iOS Turkish uses **siz** in body text and the **bare-stem imperative** on buttons:
  - siz in body: "“%@” Size Bildirimler Göndermek İstiyor" (`System/Library/PrivateFrameworks/UserNotificationsServer.framework | Localizable.strings | USER_NOTIFICATION_PERMISSION_ALERT_TITLE`); "aktivite verilerinizi görmesine" (`Applications/FamilyControlsAuthenticationUI.app | Localizable.strings | NOTIFICATION_AUTH_REQUEST_MESSAGE_INDIVIDUAL`); "daha sonra Sağlık uygulamasında açabilirsiniz" (`System/Library/PrivateFrameworks/HealthUI.framework | HealthUI-Localizable.strings | AUTHORIZATION_DONT_ALLOW_ALERT_MESSAGE`). Apple's Turkish iPhone guide agrees ("iPhone’da Ekran Süresi’ni kullanmaya başlama", support.apple.com/tr-tr/guide/iphone/iphbfa595995/ios, fetched 2026-09-16).
  - bare stem on buttons: "İzin Ver", "İzin Verme" (`UserNotificationsServer.framework | Localizable.strings | PERMISSION_ALERT_ALLOW`, `PERMISSION_ALERT_DENY`), "Sürdür" (`FamilyControlsAuthenticationUI.app | Localizable.strings | COMMON_CONTINUE`), "Vazgeç", "Kaydet", "Düzenle", "Sil", "Kapat" (`System/Library/PrivateFrameworks/UIKitCore.framework | Localizable.strings | Cancel`, `Save`, `Edit`, `Delete`, `Close`). A bare-stem button label is not read as `sen` address: it names the action. Some SpringBoard labels use the siz imperative instead ("Yeniden Deneyin", "Araç Takımlarında Arayın"), which is why button forms are pinned per control below rather than derived from the address choice.
- Apple itself switches to **sen** for coaching and achievement copy in Turkish, in the same bundle as its siz copy: `System/Library/Health/Assets/FitnessUIAssets.bundle/ActivityAchievements/MonthlyAchievements/localization/challenge/MoveStreak_SMALL_INCREASE | Localizable-Modified-tinker.stringsdict | ACHIEVEMENT_UNACHIEVED_ALERT_DESCRIPTION/NSStringLocalizedFormatKey` reads "…Hareket serisi tamamla ve bu ödülü kazan. Yapabilirsin!", while the same key in `Localizable.stringsdict` reads "…Hareket serisinin aynısını yapın." That split maps exactly onto Moneyfesting: celebration, streak, badge and widget copy on one side, permission and billing mirrors on the other.
- Local consumer-app norm: Turkish consumer apps address the reader with `sen` in coaching and marketing copy and reserve `siz` for legal, billing and system-facing text. This is the register observation behind the recommendation, not a citation; the citable half is Apple's own sen/siz split above.
- Meanwhile rule until `address_form` closes: app-owned copy uses `sen`; a string that mirrors an iOS alert or quotes an iOS label uses Apple's value verbatim, siz included (Platform terms); the adjudicator lists every key that turned on the choice.
- The app also speaks in first person plural in 19 `en` units ("We couldn't load the data.", `component.errorView.message`; "We covered %@ with a streak freeze.", `streakFreeze.usedBody`; "We'll try again soon.", `widgets.focusRing.a11y.unavailable`). Turkish keeps that as `-dık/-dik` and `-eceğiz/-acağız` ("Veriler yüklenemedi.", "Kısa süre sonra yeniden deneyeceğiz."), and it is independent of the sen/siz choice, which is about the reader.
- Grep aid, not a validator: the siz endings `ınız|iniz|unuz|ünüz|sınız|siniz|sunuz|sünüz|yınız|yiniz` plus the words `Size|Sizin`. A hit outside the mock-alert and howTo-mirror key lists in Platform terms is the finding. There is no equally clean grep for `sen`, because its endings (`-ın`, `-in`) are also the genitive and the bare imperative.
- The stored profile gender (`Manifesting/Features/User/Domain/Entities/Gender.swift`) is not a copy input without a product decision. The four `user.gender.*` values are ordinary vocabulary in Turkish: Kadın / Erkek / Diğer / Belirtmek istemiyorum.

## Header and label forms

- Sentence case for app-owned copy, including buttons of more than one word ("Odak görevini kaydet", `focusTask.createTask.createButton`), per `references/voice-card.md`. Title Case appears only where a value must equal an Apple label ("Ana Ekranı Düzenle", "Araç Takımını Sil"). The 114 Title Case `en` strings are a source convention, not a target one.
- Screen titles are sentence case even where `en` uses Title Case: "Set Your Hourly Wage" -> "Saatlik ücretini belirle" (`onboarding.hourlyWage.title`).
- Tab titles are nouns: Moneyfesting, Plan, İstatistikler, Profil (`main.tab.*`; "Insights" -> "İstatistikler" follows `System/Library/PrivateFrameworks/AccessibilitySharedUISupport.framework | AccessibilitySharedUISupport.strings | Insights`).
- Section headers are noun phrases or participles used as nouns, not sentences: `plan.dailyPlan.plannedHeader` PLANLANAN, `.doneHeader` BİTENLER, `.anytimeHeader` HER ZAMAN; `achievements.gallery.recentSection` "Son kazanılanlar".
- Toggles and state labels take the plain participle or the passive, with no agreement to manage: "Seçilen uygulamalar engellendi"; "%lld uygulama engellendi" (`screenTime.restrictedApps.appsRestricted`); "Odak duraklatıldı" (`widgets.startFocus.paused`); "Devam ediyor" (`widgets.startFocus.inProgress`). Turkish has no adjective agreement, so the packet's `agreement_target` is used to choose the right **case and possessive** on the implied noun, not a gender or number ending: `widgets.streak.copy.frozen.small` "Covered by a freeze." is about yesterday, so "Bir dondurma hakkıyla kapatıldı." is right (per open decision `streak_freeze`'s meanwhile rule) and a bare "Kapatıldı." is not.
- Uppercase is the Turkish trap, and it is decided in the catalog, never at runtime:
  - `"istatistik".uppercased(with: Locale(identifier: "tr_TR"))` is `İSTATİSTİK` (İ = U+0130) while the same call with `en_US` gives `ISTATISTIK`; `"IŞIK".lowercased(with: tr_TR)` is `ışık` (ı = U+0131) while `en_US` gives `işik`, and `"İSTATİSTİK".lowercased(with: en_US)` gives `i̇stati̇sti̇k` with a stray combining dot (Foundation on macOS 15.7.9 (24G830)).
  - Rule: type the uppercase eyebrow literally in the catalog value, with İ and I where they belong, and never rely on a runtime uppercasing. The 34 all-caps `en` strings and the 15 eyebrow/caption keys are the ones this governs: MOOD -> RUH HÂLİ, DAY STREAK -> GÜN SERİSİ, TASKS DONE -> BİTEN GÖREVLER, TODAY'S FOCUS -> BUGÜNKÜ ODAK, LAST 30 DAYS -> SON 30 GÜN, EST. TIME VALUE -> TAHMİNİ ZAMAN DEĞERİ (spelled out: "TAH." is not a recognised Turkish abbreviation, unlike `dk`/`sa`/`sn` or the everyday "yak." for yaklaşık; confirm the spelled-out form fits in a render before shipping), TIME VALUE, 30 DAYS -> ZAMAN DEĞERİ, 30 GÜN (all agent-authored proposals, seed 2026-09-16).
  - `.textCase(.uppercase)` resolves against the SwiftUI environment locale, which the app sets to `AppLanguage.effectiveLocale` (`Manifesting/App/ManifestingApp.swift:79`, `Manifesting/Features/Share/Business/Shared/ShareCardRenderer.swift:17`), so `UserProfileView.swift:183` and `ShareCardCanvasView.swift:182` are correct once `tr` is effective — confirm in a render, do not assume.
  - `Text(plan.title.uppercased())` (`OnboardingSubscriptionPaywallView.swift:420`) has no locale. Until it takes one, `paywall.plan.annual.title` and `paywall.plan.weekly.title` must contain no lowercase `i` ("Yıllık Plan", "Haftalık Plan" are safe); the locale argument is the engineering flag to propose.
- ß has no Turkish counterpart; the letters that matter are the dotted/dotless pair above, plus ç ğ ö ş ü, which uppercase without surprise.
- The week strip is a render risk, not a copy one: `tr_TR`'s `veryShortStandaloneWeekdaySymbols` are `["P","P","S","Ç","P","C","C"]` — three P and two C — so the seven letters in `StreakWeek.letters` (`Manifesting/Foundation/WidgetSupport/Streak/StreakWeek.swift:37`) and `Day` (`Manifesting/Foundation/SharedModels/Day.swift:27`) are not distinguishable. VoiceOver reads the full names, so accessibility is unaffected; propose `shortStandaloneWeekdaySymbols` ("PAZ", "PZT", "SAL"…) as an engineering finding after a render.
- Head-final composition: `WithoutMoneyfestingView.swift:19-24` and its With counterpart stack fixed fragments around two big numbers. Write each fragment for its exact slot and accept a label-like reading rather than bending the meaning; where no natural Turkish order exists across the five slots, flag a merge into one key with a proposed format string (`references/agent-protocol.md` `## A. Forward translator`), never reorder the view silently. The same applies to `onboarding.withMoneyfesting.fourthLine` "of your time, or" and `fifthLine` "in one year.", which are mid-sentence in English.

## Numbers, dates, currency and punctuation

All values are Foundation/ICU 76.1.0.0 (CLDR 46.0.0.0) output for `tr_TR` on macOS 15.7.9 (24G830) unless stated.

- Separators: grouping `.` (U+002E), decimal `,` (U+002C) — `2.000`, `12.000`, `1.250,5`, `1.250.000`. Four-digit numbers are grouped. A literal number written into copy follows the same shape ("2.000 saat", `achievements.badge.wageYear.*`).
- Money arrives formatted; no string hard-codes a currency (0 `en` keys name one). `AppCurrency.format` uses the ISO-code presentation for TRY, and in `tr_TR` the code comes **before** the amount: `TRY 120`, `TRY 1.250,5` (the separator after the code is U+00A0). Copy must never assume a trailing code, and never write "lira" or "₺" into a string: the currency is the user's own choice. The locale's own standard form would be `₺120,00`; the app's isoCode choice is project policy, not a translation question.
- Compact money is the high-severity finding above: the app prints `TRY 1,25k / 1,25m / 1,25b` while `tr_TR` compact names are `1,25 B` (bin), `1,25 Mn`, `1,25 Mr`. `b` is the locale's own letter for **thousand**.
- Time is 24-hour (`Locale.hourCycle` = h23, `DateFormatter` template `j` = `HH`): `09:41`, `23:59`. The eight chart axis keys become `00:00`, `06:00`, `12:00`, `18:00` (`insights.appUsage.axis*`, `profile.appUsage.axis.*`, `screenTimeReport.axis*`).
- Dates: `31 Ara 2026`, `31 Aralık 2026`, `12 Mayıs`, weekday `Salı`, weekday plus date `12 May Salı`. Month and weekday names are capitalised when they name a date, lower-case in generic use.
- Percent: the sign goes **before** the number with no space — `%40`, `%56`, `%100`. This inverts every `en` percent string and changes the format strings (Short forms and units). 6 `en` keys carry a percent.
- Multiplication keeps `×` ("Odaklandığın saatler × saatlik ücretin", `achievements.category.wages.description`; 5 `en` keys).
- Quotes are `“ ”` (U+201C / U+201D) with `‘ ’` inside them: Foundation's `tr_TR` delimiters, and Apple's own Turkish alerts use them (`FamilyControlsAuthenticationUI.app | Localizable.strings | NOTIFICATION_AUTH_REQUEST_TITLE` = `“%@” Ekran Süresi’ne Erişmek İstiyor`). The CLDR 46 summary chart for `tr` (unicode.org, fetched 2026-09-16) lists straight marks; Apple's shipped strings win (Decided conflicts). The 11 straight-quote `en` keys and the 7 curly ones all take `“ ”`.
- The apostrophe before a suffix on a proper noun, an abbreviation or a numeral is `’` (U+2019), not `'`: TDK, "Özel adlara getirilen iyelik, durum ve bildirme ekleri kesme işaretiyle ayrılır" and "Sayılara getirilen ekleri ayırmak için konur" (tdk.gov.tr/icerik/yazim-kurallari/kesme-isareti/, fetched 2026-09-16); Apple writes `Ayarlar’ı Aç`, `Ayarlar’da`, `Ekran Süresi’ne` with that character.
- That same rule is the placeholder trap: a suffix after `%lld` or `%@` cannot be harmonised at runtime, because the correct ending depends on how the number or name is pronounced (`5’te` but `6’da`, `1’de`). Never suffix a placeholder; put a noun after it instead (`%lld gün`, `%@ uygulamasına`), the way Apple does in `NOTIFICATION_AUTH_REQUEST_MESSAGE_INDIVIDUAL` ("“%@” uygulamasına … erişim izni verme").
- Asides take a spaced em dash where `en` has one (2 keys); ranges keep the source's en dash without spaces ("5–120 dakika", `widgets.startFocus.description`; "1–280 karakter", `onboarding.personalGoal.validation`). Ellipsis mirrors the `en` key: three periods on the 9 keys that have them, `…` on the 2 that have it.
- Plural categories are **one** and **other** (ICU 76.1.0.0 / CLDR 46.0.0.0 keywords; Xcode's `PluralRule.ComplementedPluralKeys.tr` in `XCStringsParser.framework` lists the same two, with no disagreement in either direction). `one` selects on exactly 1; every other integer 0–999999, and the fractions 0.5, 1.5 and 2.5, select `other`. Boundary integers to exercise: **0** (other), **1** (one), **2** (other), **101** (other, unlike Romanian), **1000** and **1000000** (other).
- Turkish does not pluralise a noun counted by a numeral: `5 gün`, never `5 günler`; `45 dakika`, never `45 dakikalar`. So for almost every one of the 29 `en` plural keys the `one` and `other` texts are the same string. They must still both be written: `scripts/catalog_check.py` C2 reports "source is plural but the target is a flat string" as **major** when `en` has variations and the target is a single unit, and its default required set for an unlisted locale is already `{one, other}`, which is correct for `tr` — no script change is needed. Write `one` only differently from `other` where you actually want the number spelled ("bir gün" against "%lld gün"); pick one habit per key group and say so in the comment.
- Direction is left to right; no bidi controls anywhere.

## Length trigger

- A Turkish string longer than **1.20×** its English length goes on the render list; pass `scripts/catalog_check.py --length-trigger 1.20` (its default is 1.35 and would under-report here). C5 puts the key on the render list, not among findings.
- Source of the ratio: Apple's own iOS 26.1 (23B86) Turkish translations, 79167 English/Turkish pairs (45284 unique) from 1269 UI bundles, VoiceOver tables excluded. 1.20 is the ratio, rounded to 0.05, that Turkish exceeds as often (29.9% of pairs) as Apple's Romanian exceeds 1.35× (31.6%), the statistic the 299-of-1135 `ro` catalog share was calibrated on. A flat 1.35× would flag only 17.0%.
- Where the expansion sits: all pairs median 1.059, p75 1.264, p90 1.545. Short strings are the problem — the 1–10 character bucket (23828 pairs) runs median 1.1, p75 1.5, p90 2.0, with 30.9% above 1.35×, while the 41–120 bucket runs median 1.039 and 4.9% above it.
- All-caps strings are worse still: 1357 all-caps pairs, median 1.333, p75 2.5, 49.6% above 1.35× and 32.9% above 2.0×; in the 1–10 character all-caps bucket (1100 pairs) the median is 1.6 and p75 is 2.5. The eyebrows, stat captions and section headers in Header and label forms are the first render list.
- No catalog share can be given: there are 0 Turkish units. Counts never establish fit anyway; verdicts come from render evidence against each view's own floor (`references/surface-budgets.md`, `references/render-evidence.md`). Shortening cuts words, never facts; accessibility strings are exempt.
